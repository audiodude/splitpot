## Deal & structure
- Hold'em/Omaha hybrid: each player is dealt **four** hole cards, pre-flop betting is done with all four, the three-card flop is dealt and flop betting is done with all four, then **each player must discard two cards before the turn is dealt**. From then on it is ordinary Hold'em: the two remaining hole cards plus the board, any combination.
- Pagat states the discard timing precisely: it is "similar to Crazy Pineapple"; all four cards are kept through the second betting round, and each active player discards two immediately before the fourth board card (the turn). Wikipedia and CardPlayer agree (discard after the flop betting round is complete).
- Standard blinds/button, burns before flop/turn/river. Structure: usually no-limit or pot-limit (CardPlayer); fixed-limit possible.
- Discarded cards are permanently mucked and hidden.
- Max seats: 2-10 in the PokerListings write-up. [INFERENCE] 10 x 4 hole cards + burns/board fits in 52 cards.

## Showdown / what wins
- High hand only, best five of the two kept cards + five board cards; **no Omaha "exactly two" restriction**: you may use zero, one or two of the cards you kept (CardPlayer: "unlike Omaha where each hand must use two and three, Irish allows any combination after the discard").
- Standard rankings, ties split, odd chip by position from the button.

## Rooms disagree
- **When exactly the discard happens.** Pagat/Wikipedia/CardPlayer: after the flop betting round, before the turn. A PokerListings paragraph reads as if the discard happens as soon as the flop is dealt, but its full walkthrough puts it after flop betting. Use "after flop betting, before the turn" as the primary rule.
- **Not in the WSOP live-action rules.** WSOP defines Pineapple and Crazy Pineapple but not Irish, so no canonical house-rule text exists in the main source set.
- **"Irish poker" is also a drinking game** (PokerListings describes a card-guessing "Irish Poker drinking game" with 4 cards face down per player); it shares only the name. The Irish Poker Open is a tournament series in Dublin and is not the variant.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Playing it like Omaha after the discard; you do NOT have to use exactly two hole cards (but you only have two left anyway).
- Over-folding the discard: the discard is made with the flop known, so the best two cards are usually obvious; the decision is about which draw to keep (CardPlayer: card removal matters; mind the outs your discards remove).
- Treating hands as Omaha-strength after the discard: ranges narrow to two cards, so bluffing a hidden discard is a real part of the game (CardPlayer, PokerListings).

## Lore & reputation
- Spread evidence is weak. CardPlayer: it "originated as a home or party variant, likely with US roots despite its Irish name", is "not as widely spread in casinos or online as Texas Hold'em or PLO" and appears in some mixed-game lineups in Europe and online. Pagat and Wikipedia list it as a Hold'em variant; no room rule sheet or WSOP/TDA text in the reading set includes it.
