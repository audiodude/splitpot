## Deal & structure
- Same as super-stud: ante, each player gets 4 down + 1 up, first betting round, then each player discards 2 of the 4 down cards before 4th street (failure to discard = dead hand, WSOP 251-252), then the usual 4th/5th/6th up and 7th down with betting after each (five betting rounds). Small bet 3rd/4th street, big bet 5th-7th.
- **Bring-in is the HIGH card** (unlike regular Stud 8, where the low card brings it in). WSOP 250: "unlike Seven Card Stud 8/O, in Super Stud 8/O the initial forced bet is made by the high card." Haney (Card Player, 2025): for this purpose the ace is considered LOW and ties break alphabetically by suit, so K♠ is the automatic bring-in. CountingOuts: "Unlike regular Stud Hi/Lo the high card brings in."
- After the discard, play follows seven-card-stud-hi-lo: the best HIGH hand showing acts first on 4th street and later; tied high boards go to the tied player first clockwise from the dealer (WSOP/Robert's; TDA RP-10D uses high card by suit).
- No open-pair big bet on 4th street (Stud 8 rule carries over, WSOP 241/250).
- Players: WSOP says up to seven. Badger: six-handed is most common and seven can be played with occasional reshuffling.
- Running out of cards: WSOP 253 — reshuffle stub + burn cards + muck before 6th/7th street, never a community card; CountingOuts says a community card on 7th is normal in the Las Vegas / Atlantic City games he describes.

## Showdown / what wins
- Exactly Stud 8: half the pot to the best five-card high hand, half to the best qualifying (five unpaired cards, all 8 or lower; ace low) ace-to-five low. Straights/flushes don't hurt a low. No qualifying low: high scoops. Use any five cards for high and any five (same or different) for low out of the seven cards you ended up with.
- Odd chip: in the total pot to the HIGH hand; within tied halves, to the highest card by suit among the five-card playing hand (WSOP intro to Super Stud; TDA 20); Robert's all-cards/low-card variations in the stud-8 rules (see seven-card-stud-hi-lo).
- Quartering and scooping exactly as in Stud 8; with a 5-card start, rolled-up and scoop hands occur more often.

## Rooms disagree
- **Bring-in card:** WSOP 250, Haney and CountingOuts say the HIGH card brings it in; BARGE 2.60 says Super Stud 8 is brought in by the LOW card like regular Stud 8 (2♣ lowest) — only Razz-based supers are brought in by the high card there. [Conflict not resolved; WSOP is canonical.]
- **Ace for the bring-in:** Haney treats the ace as low (so K♠ brings in); WSOP 250 says only "the high card." If rooms treated the ace as high in a high-card bring-in, A♠ would be the bring-in; no source I found says so.
- **Cards run out:** WSOP reshuffle vs CountingOuts community card (see super-stud).
- **Timing of the discard:** after 3rd-street betting (WSOP, Badger, Las Vegas) vs concurrently (some Atlantic City games per CountingOuts).
- **Supreme Stud 8** (home-game version): Haney (2025) reports a Minnesota home game invented "Supreme Stud 8," where after 3rd street betting each player indicates how many hole cards they intend to keep (2, 3 or even all 4); players keeping more than two put their later 4th/5th cards face down and expose what they kept as the cards are dealt. Haney had never played it and rates it 7/10. Not a room game.
- **"Three Cards Down" Stud 8** at the Resorts World $8-16 mix (Haney, Card Player 2023) is a different thing (three hole cards before a discard of one, probably); see seven-card-stud-hi-lo.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Forgetting the discard (dead hand).
- Playing Stud 8 hands in Super 8: Badger says "however loose or tight you play regular Stud 8-or-Better, you should play Super Stud 8 tighter"; a hand like 6♦4♥2♠ that many play in regular Stud 8 "is garbage most of the time" (Badger). Everyone has five starting cards, so more players have three coordinated low/suited/paired cards.
- Over-valuing high pairs: you will see rolled-up trips and pocket aces more often (Haney, CountingOuts). CountingOuts: rolled up ~1.3% of deals vs ~0.24% in regular stud (about 5-6x).
- Ignoring what you discarded: your own discards are dead cards only you know.
- Expecting the bring-in to be the low card.

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, Apr 2025): "increasingly more Stud 8 is being played as 'Super' to better co-exist with" exotic action games; he grades it 10/10, "as good as any game out there", and notes some players "wouldn't dream of missing" a round of it while skipping regular Stud 8.
- CountingOuts: "if [mixes] include draw games most often the 'super sized' version of Stud 8/b is quickly becoming the game of choice"; the most common spread in Las Vegas and Atlantic City is 4 down + 1 up with the high card bringing it in.
- WSOP rules list "Super Stud 8/O" and "Super Stud" in sample mixes.
