## Deal & structure
Identical to base `drawmaha`; only what wins the draw half changes.
- Button + blinds (WSOP: two blinds standard, but one blind, several blinds, an ante or blinds + ante are all allowed). Everyone gets 5 hole cards face down.
- Round 1 of betting, then a 3-card flop, round 2 of betting.
- **One draw, after the flop betting.** Each player declares how many cards to replace (WSOP: 1-3 cards, or stand pat). The declared number cannot be changed once announced. Replacements come off the same deck.
- Turn (burn + card), betting round 3; river, betting round 4; showdown.
- Limit: first two rounds at the small bet, last two at the big bet (BARGE Dramaha text). WSOP rules allow limit, pot-limit or no-limit; Counting Outs and PokerListings say the real-world spread is mostly limit in casinos, pot-limit in home games. Card Player (Haney) says casino mixed games spread the limit version.
- WSOP seat cap for Drawmaha is 7 players (the draw eats cards from the deck).

## Showdown / what wins
The pot is split into two halves; there is no qualifier for either half, so the pot always splits two ways.
1. **Omaha-high half:** best 5-card high hand using exactly 2 of your 5 hole cards + exactly 3 board cards (standard Omaha high).
2. **2-7 half:** your five hole cards only (board does not play), ranked as deuce-to-seven lowball (WSOP lowball section, rules 285-286):
   - Lowest hand wins; it is the "worst" conventional poker hand.
   - Aces are always HIGH (never a low card, never part of A-2-3-4-5 straight).
   - Straights and flushes count against you; pairs/trips etc. count against you.
   - Nuts is 7-5-4-3-2 not all one suit; next are 7-6-4-3-2, 7-6-5-3-2, 7-6-5-4-2, then eight-highs starting 8-5-4-3-2 (compare from the highest card down; 7-6-5-4-3 is a straight and so is worse).
   - 5-4-3-2-A is NOT a straight in 2-7; it is ace-high and beats pairs, but loses to king-high hands. A pair of aces is the highest pair (WSOP).
3. One player can win both halves (scoop). Ties inside a half split that half; an odd chip is awarded by position, first player clockwise from the button (WSOP).
4. Side pots: both halves are contested per pot (main and side pots are each split separately). PokerListings advises building side pots before the showdown and resolving the smallest one first.

## Rooms disagree
- **Order of turn vs. draw cards.** WSOP rules text: once all draw counts are declared (and locked), the dealer burns and places the turn on the board, THEN deals the draw cards, then the betting round. A Poker Chip Forum thread (Jun 2024) reports Bellagio, Resorts World, Aria, Talking Stick and Gila River also place the turn before the draws, with players putting their discards face down in front of them before the turn appears (so the turn is a fresh card, not a discard); the thread's original poster, however, describes a Vegas Mixed Game Festival table where the turn was placed face up before draws and says seeing it first would change drawing decisions. BARGE, Counting Outs and Haney describe the draw first, then the turn. Check each room.
- **Max cards drawn.** WSOP: 1-3 or stand pat. BARGE 'Dramaha' (the base game): 0-5. BARGE 'Dramaha 49'/'Dramadugi': 0-3. PokerListings: up to 5 with 5 or fewer players, cap at 3 with 6-7 players. Home-game variant "Draw2maha": cap at 2. Cardplayer Lifestyle: often capped at 3 to avoid reshuffling.
- **Player cap:** WSOP 7; pokergamedatabase says 6; PokerListings 7.
- **Limit vs pot-limit:** WSOP allows all three; Card Player (Haney) says limit is the version spread in casinos; Cardplayer Lifestyle says it is "most often played pot-limit".
- **Burns:** WSOP burns before the turn; one forum post says to skip burning in Drawmaha to preserve the stub; another says burn then use the burn cards as extra stub if the deck runs out. If the stub is exhausted, mucked cards are reshuffled (PokerListings: reshuffle everything except the board burns).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Trying to use five hole cards in the Omaha half, or using the board cards in the 2-7 half. Each half has its own rule.
- Treating the ace as low in the 2-7 half (it is high; A-2-3-4-5 is ace-high).
- Forgetting straights and flushes ruin a 2-7 hand: a pat 6-5-4-3-2 is a straight (bad), not a 6-low.
- Chasing the Omaha half with a good 2-7 hand: Steve Badger's strategy note says the best 2-7 hands (e.g. 7432x) are poor Omaha hands, and nut 2-7 hands "can't scoop" boards like AKQ98.
- Drawing two or three cards instead of standing pat: Badger's key principle is "don't draw" (a pat jack or ten low is often the favorite).
- Confusing 2-7 Drawmaha with 2-7 Triple Draw (which has three draws and no board).

## Lore & reputation
- Kevin Haney (Card Player, Mar 2025) rates 2-7 as the best of the Dramaha family (9/10), saying first-timers tend to grasp it quickly and it produces more action than other Dramaha forms; he says roughly 1% of five-card hands are dealt an 8-or-better low.
- Counting Outs says deuce-to-seven version is "becoming more popular" than the high version and that both are often spread in Las Vegas especially while the WSOP is in town.
- Appears in Phenom Poker's online "Circus" and "Standard" mixes as "Dramaha 2-7" and in Coach's $8-$16 mix at Resorts World as "Deuce to Seven Drawmaha" (Card Player, 2023).
- The base game originated in a home game (LOB, Limit Omaha Badugi, credited to Todd Flannagan per Red Chip Poker); Red Chip Poker updated its post in 2016 to say Nevada Gaming had approved the game for the WSOP and that it was renamed Drawmaha.
