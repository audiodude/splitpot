## Deal & structure
- Played exactly like regular Badugi (Card Player Lifestyle: "played exactly the same as the original"): button + two blinds, four cards face down, **three draws**, four betting rounds (pre-draw and after each draw). Limit is the usual structure in the rooms that spread it (the Coach's Game at Resorts World was an $8-$16 fixed-limit mix; Card Player Lifestyle's rule sheet is for "low-stakes, fixed limit cash game formats").
- Each draw lets you replace 0-4 cards. No other deal changes.
- The difference is only in who wins: the pot is split in two halves, a **low badugi** and a **high badugi**.

## Showdown / what wins
- **Low half:** the normal Badugi winner. Four different suits and ranks beats three-card hands etc.; lowest wins; best is A-2-3-4 rainbow (Haney; Card Player Lifestyle).
- **High half ("high badugi"):** you still need a valid badugi (four cards, all suits different, all ranks different). The best high badugi is the one whose **lowest card is highest**; compare the lowest card first, then the next-lowest, and so on - Haney: "you simply read the hand backwards". So the best high badugi is K-Q-J-10 rainbow (compare lowest cards: 10 beats anything lower). Card Player Lifestyle phrases it as "the player holding the highest low card wins".
- **Ace is both:** the ace plays as the lowest card for the low half and as the **highest** card for the high half (Haney: it "plays both high and low, and is thus 'invisible'"; Card Player Lifestyle: "aces are counted as both low and high").
- Worked examples from the sources:
  - J♣ 8♠ 7♦ 5♥ is a "jack-low" and "five-high". Q♠ 8♥ 5♦ 4♣ is "queen-low" and "four-high". The first wins both halves (Haney): jack-low beats queen-low for low (J is lower than Q); 5-high beats 4-high for high (lowest card 5 > 4).
  - K-Q-5-3 vs J-10-5-7 (Card Player Lifestyle): the second is the better hand both ways: low: J-high beats K-high; high: lowest card 5 beats 3.
  - A♠ 8♥ 7♦ 6♣ is an eight-low (8-7-6-A) and a six-high (compare from the bottom: 6,7,8,A with ace high). Heads-up it can't be scooped, but in a 3-way pot against K-Q-10-9 and 7-6-5-3 it gets nothing: K-Q-10-9 wins high (lowest card 9 > 6) and 7-6-5-3 wins low (seven-low) (Haney).
- Because a very strong low (A-2-3-4) has the *worst* high, and a strong high (K-Q-J-10) has the worst low, scoops are rare and "middle" hands (such as 5-6-7-8 / 6-7-8-9) can win half but struggle to win both (paraphrasing Haney/the 2+2 thread - see Sources).
- Unspecified in anything I could read: how the high half compares three-card (non-badugi) hands, odd-chip rule, and whether a player holding a low badugi can also claim high with the same four cards (obviously yes - same hand, both reads). The 2+2 poster presumes "any badugi still beats all tris" for the high side too, but that's an assumption. [INFERENCE: use badugi card-count-first rule for both halves; confirm with floor]

## Rooms disagree
- I found no real conflict, but sourcing is thin: only Haney (Card Player magazine, about the Resorts World Coach's Game), Card Player Lifestyle's Mixed Game Festival rule sheet, and a 2+2 forum thread that I could only see through search-engine snippets (page returned HTTP 403). The three descriptions agree with each other.
- Naming: "Badugi High-Low" (Haney's Coach's Game list), "Badugi Hi-Low" (Card Player Lifestyle), "Badugi hi/lo" (2+2 thread title).
- Labeling slip: Card Player Lifestyle calls J-10-7-5 "Jack-low and 7-high", whereas Haney's rule (judge the high side by the lowest card) would call it five-high. The ruling in that example (J-10-5-7 scoops K-Q-5-3) comes out the same either way, so treat the "7-high" label as loose wording.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Thinking "high badugi" means the highest-ranking top card. It is judged from the bottom up (Haney: "the highest lowest card").
- Calling A-2-3-4 a great hand: it's the nut low but the worst possible high badugi; a hand that is nutty for one half is nearly dead for the other. Haney: this is "the variant that causes the most angst for new players."
- Forgetting the ace plays high in the high half and low in the low half. A-8-7-6 is a decent hand in both directions but still can lose both in multiway pots.
- Ignoring the multiway-pot trap: a hand that cannot be scooped heads-up can still win nothing at a three-way showdown.

## Lore & reputation
- Spread in the Resorts World "Coach's Game" mix (Las Vegas, run by Donald "Coach" Shiflett Jr.) as one of its 17 games, per Haney (Card Player, Jan 2023). Haney: "a ton of fun, and many players who were initially turned off by it now rate it as one of their favorite in the mix." Resorts World's poker room closed 30 March 2026 per a Vegas room-review site (search-engine summary of vegasadvantage.com, not opened directly).
- Also in the Mixed Game Festival rule sheet (Card Player Lifestyle). No source found for who invented it. Not on the WSOP Dealer's Choice menu.
- A 2+2 poster called the split-pot concept hard to see a scoop in and said reading the high hand "feels like Calvinball" (search snippet of the 2+2 thread; opinion).
