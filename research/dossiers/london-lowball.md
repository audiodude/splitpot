## Deal & structure
- Dealt exactly like razz: ante; 2 down + 1 up; three more up cards and one final down card; betting after every card (five rounds).
- **Bring-in:** the HIGHEST door card, ace low, ties by suit with K♠ highest (BARGE 2.38 — "the high card on the board by rank first and then by suit brings it in").
- Later streets: the player showing the LOWEST hand (ace-to-six ranking) acts first (BARGE).
- **Traditionally pot-limit:** BARGE: "For all rounds, the minimum bet will be equal to the size of the bring-in and the minimum raise is the size of the last bet or raise. The maximum wager is the size of the pot." Pagat: "Normally a pot limit betting structure is used." Wikipedia: "its canonical version is played at pot limit. Some variations are played at no limit, but the name 'London lowball' is typically reserved for the pot-limit version."
- BARGE's rule sheet is written "assuming the game is played pot-limit, which is traditional for this game as described in Pot-Limit & No-Limit Poker by Stewart Reuben and Bob Ciaffone."

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand wins the whole pot, no qualifier, ranked **ace-to-six**: the ace is LOW, but **straights and flushes count against you** (as in deuce-to-seven). Best hand: **6-4-3-2-A, not suited** (BARGE; Wikipedia). 5-4-3-2-A is a straight here (bad). BARGE's ranked list of A-6 hands: 6-4-3-2-A, 6-5-3-2-A, 6-5-4-2-A, 6-5-4-3-A, 7-4-3-2-A, 7-5-3-2-A, ...
- Pairs are bad, as in all lowball. No joker/bug (BARGE).
- Ties split; odd chip by stud rules.

## Rooms disagree
- **Where it's played:** Wikipedia: "almost exclusively in Europe." BARGE: "seldom seen in games played in American casinos" (and it debuted at BARGE only in 2018). A book review of Stewart Reuben's *Poker 24/7* describes it as popular in London poker clubs and says this ranking method "has never been used in U.S. casinos." No US room rulebook (WSOP, TDA, Robert's) has it.
- **Structure:** pot-limit in every source (Wikipedia, Pagat, BARGE); BARGE and Wikipedia mention no-limit variations.
- **Ranking nuance:** Wikipedia's "A-6" description and BARGE agree on 6-4-3-2-A; Wikipedia's Five-card stud article notes the UK also ranks ace-to-six lows in five-card stud hi-lo.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming straights and flushes don't count (they do — that's what distinguishes it from razz).
- Thinking A-2-3-4-5 is the nuts (it's a five-high straight; the nuts is 6-4-3-2-A).
- Playing it with fixed limits and razz raise caps — the traditional game is pot-limit with min bet = bring-in.
- Confusing it with draw ace-to-six lowball (Pagat has a separate entry for the draw game).

## Lore & reputation
- Stewart Reuben (British chess organizer and professional poker player, co-author with Bob Ciaffone of *Pot-Limit & No-Limit Poker*) played in a London scene where pot-limit 7-card stud and London Lowball were popular; a review of his memoir *Poker 24/7* says the game "led to a great deal of money changing hands" and that he did not hold it in high regard (via the poker-vibe.com review; I did not read the book itself).
- BARGE's rule page treats it as a rarity: first played at BARGE in 2018.
- Keep out of "casino mixed game night" copy except as a historical/European curiosity.
