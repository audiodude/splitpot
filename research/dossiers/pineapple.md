## Deal & structure
- Hold'em, but each player is dealt **three** hole cards and must **discard one** before the flop. After the discard it is plain Hold'em (two hole cards, five board cards, any combination).
- WSOP: "each participant must discard one of the three down cards after the first round of betting is complete and before the flop is exposed" - i.e. pre-flop betting is done with three cards, then everyone discards, then the flop.
- All other Hold'em rules (blinds, button, burns, betting structure) apply. Any betting structure (limit, pot-limit, no-limit).
- The discarded card is dead (not shown, not reusable).

## Showdown / what wins
- High hand only, best five of the two remaining hole cards plus five board cards; may use zero, one or two hole cards (plays the board allowed).
- Standard rankings, ties split, odd chip by position from the button.
- Nothing is qualified or split; Hi-Lo variants are a different game (see Crazy Pineapple Hi-Lo in `crazy-pineapple`).

## Rooms disagree
- **When the discard happens.** WSOP: after the pre-flop betting round, before the flop. Pagat, Wikipedia ("pre-flop, before the first betting round"), Wizard of Odds ("immediately discards one, before the first round of betting") and BeatTheFish ("before the pre-flop betting round") all put the discard **before any betting**. WSOP is the outlier, but it is the canonical live-room text. This is a genuine disagreement about the game's defining step; announce which version is being called.
- Terminology: some sources use "Pineapple" for any three-card hold'em variant (Crazy, Lazy/Tahoe, Super Hold'em); room rule sheets such as WSOP's keep the plain name for the pre-flop-discard game.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Discarding too early or too late: in the WSOP version you discard after betting but before the flop; pitching the card before betting (the Pagat/Wikipedia version) changes pre-flop values.
- Treating three hole cards as "better hands to flop": everyone's starting hands are stronger, so top pair and two pair are overvalued (BeatTheFish; same general effect noted for Crazy Pineapple).
- Mistaking Pineapple for Omaha-style "must use two": you still play any combination of your two remaining cards, including none.
- Confusing with OFC Pineapple (Open-Face Chinese variant, excluded from this project; the Wizard of Vegas thread shows people confuse the two).

## Lore & reputation
- Plain Pineapple is the least-played of the three-card hold'em family; Crazy Pineapple is the version most often spread. Wizard of Odds' forum host (Wizard of Vegas thread, 2024) says he had never heard of a casino offering Pineapple and sees it as a home/dealer's-choice game; one poster there recalls seeing it dealt in a downtown Las Vegas casino 25-30 years earlier, and another sat in a Foxwoods 2/5 PLO/Pineapple mixed game. These are anecdotes, not room rules.
- WSOP's current live-action rules still define Pineapple and Crazy Pineapple under "Hold'em variations", which is why it can be called in a dealer's-choice live game.
- A claim that Pineapple appeared in California cardrooms in the 1980s and "spread online in the early 2000s" appears on poker-content sites (e.g. Pokerology) with a citation to Sklansky I could not verify; treat as unsourced.
