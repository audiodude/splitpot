## Deal & structure
This is a **structure**, not a hand-ranking game. A bomb pot overlays a different pre-flop on whatever flop game the table is playing (NLHE, PLO, PLO5, hi-lo, double board...).
- **WSOP definition (rule 31):** an agreement by all players to ante the same amount, then hole cards are dealt, the flop is put out, and play proceeds normally from there: "every player having seen the flop with totally random hands". There is **no pre-flop betting round**. Post-flop betting begins left of the button (WSOP 5-card double board entry: "action starts left of the button after the flop").
- In WSOP live games a bomb pot is tied to **time collection**: in time-raked games players may opt to play a time-pot, time-flop or bomb pot; the half-hour's entire time charge is collected from the bomb pot on the first hand dealt after the 30-minute announcement. A player may opt out and pay only their own time, but then is not dealt in for that hand; **the button does not advance** in a bomb-pot hand.
- If players agree to a bomb pot but not on the ante, WSOP's default is $20 per player in $1-$2-$5 games and $25 in $5-$5 and larger games (rule 31, as worded: "limit type games").
- **Ante size elsewhere is set by the table or room** (casino.org: usually a few times the big blind; PLOMastermind: typically 3 to 10 big blinds; The Lodge example $15 ante in a $1/$3 game).
- Frequency is not standardized: hourly, every dealer change, every orbit, or table-agreed (casino.org, The Lodge).
- **Double-board form** (most common live in streamed PLO): two flops, two turns, two rivers; see `double-board-omaha`. **5-card double board bomb pot PLO** is WSOP's named game; **Derailment** uses three boards; see those dossiers.
- WSOP bomb-pot dealing quirks (from the 5-card double board bomb pot rule): **no burn for the flop** because there is no pre-flop action; with eight players dealt in the last card of the deck completes the river; if the river is dealt prematurely with two cards left in the stub, the last card, the premature card, the burns and the muck are reshuffled; if a player fails to play the worst position (first left of the button) the button passes to the next player who posted on the previous hand.
- **The Lodge (Austin) house version** (their rules page): a special bomb-pot button rotates; its holder sits "on the button" for the bomb pot, picks NLHE or PLO (in NLHE games), and the bomb pot happens at each dealer change in $1/$2-and-up games; all Lodge bomb pots are double-board. Players may sit the hand out. Example game: $1/$3 NLHE, $15 ante, $500 cap on the bomb pot.

## Showdown / what wins
- Whatever the base game's showdown is. In single-board NLHE/PLO, the best hand wins the pot; in double-board bomb pots the pot is split between the best hand on each board (see `double-board-omaha` for scoop/quarter outcomes).
- The ante is dead money in the pot; there is no blind or live pre-flop bet. Odd chip rules as in the base game (WSOP: in mixed games the odd chip goes to the worst position in button games).

## Rooms disagree
- **Whether you can opt out.** WSOP lets a player skip the bomb pot (and pay only their own time). The Lodge says players can sit the hand out. PokerListings' definition describes "every player at the table agrees" to the ante; its FAQ says a player can still fold before the flop. Treat opt-out as a room/table decision.
- **Who decides the game.** At The Lodge the bomb-pot button holder chooses NLHE vs PLO (in NLHE-posted tables; PLO tables always play PLO). WSOP only speaks about the agreement and ante.
- **What triggers it.** WSOP: agreement in time-raked games, used to pay time. Other rooms: schedule (dealer change/orbit/hour). Online: random trigger (casino.org).
- **Ante cap/stack cap.** The Lodge example caps each player's bomb-pot stake at $500; WSOP states no cap.
- Bomb pots are described as new/rapidly growing (PLOMastermind, The Lodge: "relatively new addition"), with no TDA rule found for them.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating it as a normal flop with the ante as "free"; the ante is already in the pot so the hand is played multiway with random ranges (casino.org: top pair in a bomb pot is often behind; the ante is a sunk cost).
- Forgetting to confirm the format (single vs double board, NLHE vs PLO) before the ante goes in (casino.org: ask what the ante and format are).
- In double-board versions, ignoring the second board or forgetting that a split of one board can leave you with a quarter pot.
- Assuming a player who opts out of a WSOP bomb pot is dealt in; they sit the hand out and the button does not move.

## Lore & reputation
- Described in several explainers as "wild", popularised by streamed cash games (Hustler Casino Live per casino.org) and by Texas card rooms like The Lodge. All sources I read are explainers or room blog posts; no deeper history source was found. [INFERENCE] The WSOP rule shows bomb pots are an established live-room device (tied to time collection), not only a streaming gimmick.
- casino.org notes online rooms may trigger bomb pots at random intervals.
- "Circus games" (Derailment, 3D, etc.) are a home-game/live extension of the bomb-pot idea (Poker Chip Forum posters), with WSOP naming Derailment explicitly.
