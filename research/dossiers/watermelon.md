## Deal & structure
Per Cardplayer Lifestyle: a 5-card Omaha variant played with blinds-style flop-game betting. Three complete boards (each with a flop, turn and river) are dealt, with the usual betting rounds between streets. No burn procedure, number of players, or betting limit is given in the source. Dealing a single hand with three simultaneous boards follows the same procedure as other multi-board games (see double-board-omaha, squid-game) [INFERENCE: not stated for Watermelon].

## Showdown / what wins
- Omaha rule per board [INFERENCE from "5-card Omaha variant"; the source states only that each board is a full Omaha-style board]: exactly two of five hole cards plus three cards of that board.
- Each player holding the best hand on a board earns one point; the three boards are worth three points in total.
- If two players tie for a board, each gets half a point.
- The player with the most points wins the whole pot. If players tie on points (for example three players on one point each, or two on 1.5 each), the pot is divided accordingly among those tied players. The source gives no odd-chip rule.
- Not stated: whether the same pair of hole cards must be used on every board. [INFERENCE] players may pick different pairs per board as in double-board Omaha.
- Not stated: what happens when no one holds more than one point and all points are different (e.g. three different board winners, 1 each, three-way tie, equal split).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Expecting to be paid per board: the pot is not divided by board; whoever collects the most points takes everything, so winning two boards beats winning one.
- Missing that three-way splits of points (1/1/1) produce a three-way chop of the entire pot.
