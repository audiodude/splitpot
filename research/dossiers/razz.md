## Deal & structure
- Dealt exactly like seven-card-stud: ante; 2 down + 1 up; then 4th, 5th, 6th up; 7th down; five betting rounds; best five of seven (see seven-card-stud for burns, the 8-player community-card fallback, raise caps).
- **Bring-in is the HIGHEST door card** (K highest, ace low). Ties are broken by suit: spades highest, then hearts, diamonds, clubs — so K♠ is a guaranteed bring-in (WSOP 239; Robert's; PokerNews; Wikipedia). The bring-in player may complete to a full small bet.
- **Later streets:** the player whose up cards show the LOWEST hand acts first. A pair among your up cards makes your board worse (pairs count against you); an unpaired board beats a paired board. If the low boards tie, WSOP and Robert's give first action to the tied player closest to the dealer's left; TDA 2019 RP-10D (tournaments) gives it to the hand with the high card by suit; Pagat (home tradition) breaks ties by suit of the highest cards.
- Limit bet sizes: small bet on 3rd and 4th street, big bet on 5th, 6th, 7th. **An open pair on 4th street does NOT allow a big bet in Razz** (WSOP 237, 240; Robert's s.9 rule 3; Pagat).
- All other stud procedure (completions, raise caps, dead hands, short stub) is the seven-card-stud procedure (WSOP 237).
- Check-raise allowed (general rule). Dealers announce pairs when they first appear, but never announce pairs of face cards (Robert's s.9 rule 4).

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand wins the WHOLE pot; **no qualifier**: the lowest hand wins even if it is garbage (PokerNews; Wikipedia).
- Ace-to-five ranking ("California" low): the ace is always low; straights and flushes do NOT count against you; pairs/trips DO count against you ("two aces are the lowest pair" — WSOP intro). Best hand 5-4-3-2-A ("the wheel", also called "the bicycle" — Wikipedia). Next: 6-4-3-2-A, 6-5-3-2-A, 6-5-4-2-A, 6-5-4-3-A, 6-5-4-3-2, 7-4-3-2-A, ... Compare from the highest card down (7-6-4-3-A beats 7-6-5-2-A).
- You use the best five cards of your seven. If you hold a pair, trip, etc., you choose five unpaired cards if you can; only if you hold fewer than five distinct ranks do you have to play a pair.
- Ties split. Odd chip: WSOP 153 and TDA 20B — highest card by suit among the five cards in the winning hand (this applies "in all stud games," low included); Robert's s.3 (ties) — LOWEST card by suit in low games, using all cards held.
- Kansas City Razz in the WSOP text means 7-5-4-3-2 is best and straights/flushes count as high hands (WSOP 238) — treated as its own game here, see 2-7-razz.

## Rooms disagree
- **Raise cap:** WSOP bet + four raises (completion doesn't count); Robert's/Pagat bet + three.
- **Tied low boards on 4th+ streets:** WSOP/Robert's — position (first clockwise from dealer); TDA RP-10D — high card by suit; Pagat — suit of the highest cards (e.g., ♦6-♣2 vs ♣6-♠2: the ♣6 board is lower and starts the betting).
- **Odd-chip rule:** WSOP/TDA — highest card by suit; Robert's — lowest card by suit in low games.
- **Open pair on fourth street:** all sources agree it has no effect in Razz. (In Stud high some rooms double the bet; not Razz.)
- **Players/cards:** Wikipedia says razz is "usually" capped at eight players, with a community 7th card if all eight are still in; WSOP 232 and TDA RP-10H give the exact short-stub procedures.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Playing it like stud high-in-reverse and expecting straights/flushes to count — they don't. The wheel is the nuts, and a 7-low simply beats an 8-low, no matter the suits.
- Forgetting that the ace is always LOW (it never makes a high pair or a high card). A-A is the lowest pair, but it is still a pair.
- Playing hands with cards above an 8 from the first round in a full ring (PokerNews notes experienced players fold three-card hands containing a card higher than an 8 in 8-handed games).
- Ignoring the dead cards: the cards your opponents show are your outs-in-the-deck problem. A 3-4-5 board with several 3s, 4s and 5s out is stronger than the same cards when "make" cards are live (Wikipedia).
- Overvaluing the bring-in: the forced bet is a king/queen door card, usually a hand you want to fold (PokerNews).
- Failing to complete the bet in position when the bring-in is high and everyone folds — stealing the ante and bring-in is a standard Razz play (PokerNews, Wikipedia).

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: Razz is among the oldest poker forms, played since the early 1900s; it has been a WSOP bracelet event since 1971 (first winner Jimmy Casella, $10,000) and run every year except 1972. In 2004 ESPN televised the event (won by T.J. Cloutier), which Wikipedia credits with kicking off a razz online boom (Full Tilt first, then others, PokerStars in 2006).
- PokerNews: razz's popularity waned in the 1990s/early 2000s; the rise of mixed games and HORSE brought it back.
- Haney (Card Player, 2026): 30 years ago razz "was considered a dying game," then HORSE rotations revived it; $1,500 WSOP razz now draws about as many players as a $1,500 limit hold'em event; he describes the vibe as "kind of lame but it's cool," and says it is *not* very popular in home games or low/medium-stakes mixed cash games, where Razzdeucey/Razzdugi are preferred.
- A frequent criticism, from Greg Raymer, is that the bring-in is almost always dead money, which led to Action Razz (see action-razz).
