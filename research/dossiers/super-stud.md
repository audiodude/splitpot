## Deal & structure
- Ante, then every player gets FOUR cards face down and ONE face up (five cards total). First betting round (3rd street) happens with all five cards.
- After that round, every player must discard TWO of the four down cards (never the up card) before 4th street is dealt. A player who fails to discard before the first 4th-street card is dealt has a dead hand (WSOP 251, 252). Each player is now back to the normal stud position of 2 down + 1 up.
- Then three more up cards (4th, 5th, 6th street) and a last down card (7th), with a betting round after each: five betting rounds in total; small bet on 3rd and 4th street, big bet on 5th-7th (WSOP).
- **Open pair on 4th street:** in Super Stud HIGH, any player may choose the small or big bet when a pair shows (WSOP: "Super Stud High Only"); not in the hi-lo/low versions.
- **Bring-in (high version):** the WSOP says "all rules for Seven Card Stud ... apply" and contrasts that only Super Stud 8 is brought in by the high card (WSOP 250), which implies Super Stud High is brought in by the LOW card as in regular stud. BARGE says for "Stud" and "Stud 8" supers the LOW card (2♣ lowest) brings it in and for Razz-type supers the HIGH card brings it in.
- Players: WSOP says "as many as seven." Badger (strategy site): six-handed is most common, seven plays with occasional reshuffling.
- **Cards run out faster** (every player is dealt 5 initial cards): WSOP 253 — before 6th street the dealer counts players + 2; if the stub is short, the stub, burns and the muck (including the discards) are reshuffled; the same check before the 7th-street burn. WSOP: "The dealer will never use a community card in Super Stud since the muck is used in the re-shuffle." CountingOuts reports the opposite common practice (community card on 7th). See Rooms disagree.
- Timing of the discard: after the first betting round is standard (WSOP; Badger; "in Las Vegas almost always after the round of betting on 3rd street" — CountingOuts). Some Atlantic City games let you discard concurrently while acting (CountingOuts); the dealer often pauses and checks, but "the onus is on each player" (Haney).
- "Roll your own" variant (some home/mixed games): all five cards are dealt down and each player chooses which card to turn up (Haney).

## Showdown / what wins
- Best five-card high hand from the seven cards you end up with (same as seven-card-stud). Ties split; odd chip to the highest card by suit among the five playing cards (WSOP intro).
- No special ranking changes. The difference is only that your first five cards give you a chance to choose the best three: rolled-up trips are far likelier (see super-stud-8).

## Rooms disagree
- **Bring-in card:** WSOP 250 (implied low card in Super Stud High, high card in Super Stud 8); BARGE 2.60 says LOW card brings it in for Stud and Stud 8 supers (and HIGH for Razz supers) — so BARGE disagrees with WSOP, Haney and CountingOuts on the Super Stud 8 bring-in.
- **Cards running out:** WSOP 253 — reshuffle stub + burns + muck, never use a community card. CountingOuts — "as in normal stud games a community card should be used on 7th street as opposed to a reshuffling of the burn cards." TDA RP-10H has only the normal-stud short-stub procedure.
- **Max players:** WSOP 7; Badger six-handed typical.
- **Discard timing:** after 3rd-street betting (Las Vegas, WSOP) vs concurrently (some Atlantic City games, CountingOuts).
- **Is "Super Stud" high-only actually spread?** WSOP and BARGE define Super Stud (high); the strategy/evidence pieces I found (Badger, CountingOuts, Haney) all concentrate on Super Stud 8. WSOP names "Super Stud" in its sample mixes alongside Stud 8 variants.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- **Forgetting to discard:** your hand is dead. Haney: "avoid being that guy at all costs." CountingOuts tells of a player who tabled "a nine card straight" with no discard and forfeited the pot.
- Discarding your up card: you can only discard down cards.
- Treating it like normal stud: trips/rolled-up hands are much more frequent (Haney: about five times more likely to be dealt rolled up trips; CountingOuts calculates ~1.3% vs ~0.24%).
- Playing too many hands because everyone "gets five cards" — Badger's whole thesis is that Super Stud 8 should be played tighter than regular Stud 8.
- Not tracking the extra information: your own two discards are dead cards you know about.

## Lore & reputation
- WSOP's mixed-game rosters name "Super Stud" and "Super Stud 8/O" as games in $20-$40 mixes.
- Haney (Card Player 2025): "increasingly more Stud 8 is being played as 'Super'" to co-exist with the exotic action games in home and casino mixes.
- CountingOuts: "the most common version spread in Las Vegas and Atlantic City" plays with 4 down + 1 up.
- BARGE's rulebook treats "Super" as a modifier that can be applied to Stud, Stud 8, or Razz. Haney says both Razzdugi and Razzdeucey are usually played as "super" in low/mid-stakes mixes.
