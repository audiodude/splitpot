## Deal & structure
- No blinds, no button advantage. Every player antes (small: roughly 5-20% of the small bet depending on source/room). Because the ante is the "dead" money, the first-round forced bet is a bring-in, not a blind.
- Deal clockwise, one card at a time: 2 down, 1 up. The up card is the "door card". One card is burned before each later street.
- Streets: 3rd (2 down + 1 up), 4th (up), 5th (up), 6th (up), 7th/river (down) = five betting rounds. Final hand = best five of the seven cards. A player who stays to the end has 3 hidden + 4 visible cards.
- Limit bet sizes: small bet on 3rd and 4th street, big bet (2x) on 5th, 6th, 7th (WSOP, Robert's, PokerNews, Pagat). Some California rooms use the small bet on the first three rounds (Wikipedia, "Stud poker" article) [uncertain which rooms, article is unsourced on that point].
- Bring-in (3rd street): the player showing the LOWEST door card, by rank and then suit, must post the bring-in (a forced partial bet, commonly about half the small bet — PokerNews). Suit order low-to-high: clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades (alphabetical). The ace is a HIGH card here, so the lowest possible bring-in card is the 2♣ (BARGE; WSOP stud-8 and Robert's say it explicitly for Stud 8).
- The bring-in player may instead "complete" to a full small bet (WSOP 220). Completing the bring-in to the small bet is NOT a raise; after a completion, raises are still available (WSOP 223).
- Action then goes clockwise from the bring-in. Raise cap in limit: WSOP says one bet plus four raises, with a completion not counting as a raise (rule 130/223). Robert's, Pagat and PokerNews say bet plus three raises. Heads-up play removes the cap in cash games.
- 4th street onward: the player whose UP cards make the best poker hand (pairs, trips, quads count in the normal poker order; straights/flushes cannot show with 4 or fewer cards) acts first, and may check or bet. If best hands tie, see Rooms disagree.
- Open pair on 4th street: in the "classic" rule, if any player's two up cards are a pair, anyone may bet or raise either the small bet or the big bet on that round (WSOP 224, Robert's, PokerNews, Pagat). If a $5 bet is made in $5-$10, others may raise by $5 or $10; after a big raise, all later raises are big.
- Folded hands: seats that fold with no wager outstanding keep receiving cards until a bet kills the hand, so the fold doesn't change who gets which card (WSOP 226).
- Running out of cards: with 8 players still in, 7th street needs more cards than the stub holds. WSOP 232: dealer counts (players remaining + 2); if the stub is short but stub + the three burn cards is enough, scramble those and deal; otherwise burn one and turn one community card face up in the middle that everyone uses as their seventh card. PokerNews and Wikipedia describe the same community-card fallback; TDA RP-10H gives a tournament version.

## Showdown / what wins
- Best five-card hand (standard high ranking, ace high/low in straights) from the seven cards wins the whole pot. Cards speak.
- Last bettor/aggressor on 7th street normally shows first (PokerNews). If a player is all-in, hands go face up and everyone's cards are live (TDA 16).
- Dead hands: a hand with more than 7 cards is dead; one with fewer than 6 cards at showdown is dead (WSOP 235). Facing a wager, picking up/turning over your up cards is a fold (WSOP 227, TDA 66).
- Exact ties split the pot; odd chip goes to the highest card by suit in the winning five cards (WSOP 153: "highest card by suit in all stud games," five playing cards only; TDA 20B same; Robert's uses all seven cards — see Rooms disagree).
- Dealer announces the bring-in card, then the high hand, raises and pairs each street (WSOP 229).
- Deliberately re-ordering your up cards is improper and can get the hand ruled dead (WSOP intro to Section IX; Robert's).

## Rooms disagree
- **Raise cap:** WSOP four raises after a completed bring-in (rule 130/223); Robert's Rules, Pagat and PokerNews three raises.
- **Who acts first when the best board hands tie on 4th street and later:** WSOP 219 and Robert's — position: the tied player who received cards first (closest to the dealer's left) acts first. TDA 2019 RP-10D (tournaments) — the tied hand with the high card by suit starts the betting. Pagat (home tradition) — compare suits of the highest cards.
- **Paired door/up cards on 4th street:** WSOP, Robert's, PokerNews, Pagat allow the big bet. BARGE notes many card rooms no longer allow the double bet and that the current TDA does not (TDA RP-10F: bets are not doubled on 4th street for a pair showing). For high-low and low stud (Stud 8, Razz, etc.) the pair rule is normally off everywhere (WSOP 237, 241; Robert's).
- **Odd chip tie-break:** WSOP/TDA — highest card by suit among the five cards that make the hand; Robert's — highest card by suit using all cards held.
- **Ante/bring-in sizing:** PokerNews gives ~5% of big bet for ante in one article and ~1/5 of low limit in the Stud 8 article; Upswing's $2/$4 example uses $0.30 (7.5% of small bet). Treat as "small fraction", varies by room.
- **Dealer announcing straights/flushes:** WSOP 229 — never announced. Robert's — not announced "except for specified low-stakes games".
- **Short-stub fallback:** WSOP 232 (burn cards can be mixed back first) vs TDA RP-10H (similar order: scramble with prior three burns if that reaches the needed number, else community card).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating position like Hold'em: in stud the first-to-act seat is determined by the up cards each round and changes. The bring-in is forced on the lowest door card, NOT the dealer's left.
- Not knowing the ace is high for the bring-in and for ranking the board (A♠ is not the "lowest" card in 7-card stud high).
- Ignoring dead cards: every visible up card (including those in folded hands) tells you which outs are gone; PokerNews stresses watching opponents' up cards and blockers.
- Misreading what a visible pair means: an open pair on 4th street (where permitted) lets the big bet in.
- Mucking or turning cards wrong: turning your up cards over when facing a bet = fold.
- Assuming straights/flushes show on the board: with at most 4 up cards you cannot see a made straight or flush.
- Re-ordering/hiding the order of up cards (an etiquette and dead-hand issue).

## Lore & reputation
- Before the 2000s hold'em boom, 7-card stud was one of the most-played poker games in US home games and in casinos in the eastern US; it is "not as common in casinos today" (Wikipedia, Seven-card stud).
- It is the "S" in HORSE and in 8-game mixes (Wikipedia; Pagat). Fixed-limit with an ante and bring-in is "the normal rule in formal games hosted by American casinos" (Pagat).
- Alternate names "Seven-Toed Pete" and "Down the River" are listed in Wikipedia.
- Wikipedia mentions a nod in Tennessee Williams' *A Streetcar Named Desire* ("This game is seven-card stud"). Light trivia only.
- BARGE describes it as "a dominant game for many decades."
