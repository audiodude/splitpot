## Deal & structure
Played "just like regular Archie" (Cardplayer Lifestyle), i.e. the triple-draw structure from the WSOP Archie text:
- Button + blinds (some structures allow the big blind to be called; others require opening at 2x BB); each player gets five cards face down.
- Betting round before the draw, then up to three draws, each followed by a betting round (four rounds total). Limit game; the bet size doubles after the draw (WSOP's usual limit structure for Archie).
- Reshuffle rule (WSOP Archie): when the stub runs low, shuffle old discards and mucks, but do not use the current round's discards; only use the very last stub card if it completes all remaining draws.
What changes is how hands are scored (see below).

## Showdown / what wins
Cards are scored by pips, as in `drawmaha-49` / `drawmaha-zero`: ace = 1; two through ten = face value; jack, queen, king = 0 (Cardplayer Lifestyle).
- Cardplayer Lifestyle version: the pot splits between the hand with the LOWEST point total (best = 0) and the hand with the HIGHEST (best = 49), "typically" with qualifiers for both sides — its example is 35 (high) and 8 (low).
- A Poker Chip Forum post (Oct 2024, passing on what a player learned at the Bellagio Mixed Game Festival) describes it differently: "49" = points hand qualifying at 35+ points, split with "the best poker hand"; "Zero" = points hand qualifying at 10 points or fewer, also split with the best poker hand. I.e. two separate games (Archie 49 and Archie Zero), each pairing a pip count against a conventional high hand.
- [UNVERIFIED] Which description is the standard one is unresolved; the sources conflict. For the website, treat Archie 49/0 as a festival-only novelty with unsettled rules and say "ask the dealer for the qualifiers".
- No-qualifier fallbacks (does the high side take the whole pot, or is the pot chopped?) are not documented for the 49/0 version. In regular Archie, WSOP says: no qualifying high -> best low scoops; no low -> qualifying high scoops; neither qualifies -> the pot is split among all remaining players [from WSOP Archie rules 293-295]. Whether this carries over is [INFERENCE].

## Rooms disagree
- See above: "49 vs 0 points" (Cardplayer Lifestyle) vs "points qualifier vs conventional poker hand" (forum report).
- Qualifier for the low-points side: 8 (Cardplayer Lifestyle) vs 10 (forum report). High-points qualifier: 35 in both.
- Regular Archie's high qualifier also varies (pair of 9s per WSOP and Haney; pair of 6s in "Ari" and in some rooms per Haney) — likely applies here too [INFERENCE].
- Not in the WSOP Live-Action rules at all (WSOP names only Archie and Ari).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating tens as zero points (they are 10) — same mistake Haney flags in Drawmaha Zero.
- Valuing face cards for the high side: for the 49 side you want tens, nines and eights, not kings.
- Assuming this is a poker-hand game: for the points side only pip totals matter, suits and pairs do not.
- Keeping cards "that correlate": a forum player notes that in Zero, the cards you keep for points (faces and aces) are exactly the ones you'd keep for a high hand, so the draw strategy is straightforward.

## Lore & reputation
- Archie itself: Haney says it originated in the Phoenix area and was named for a player who had a bad run when it was first introduced; he rates it 8/10 (Card Player, Oct 2025).
- Archie 49/0 appears only on the Mixed Game Festival rule sheet, and a Poker Chip Forum poster says a friend learned the variants at the Bellagio Mixed Game Festival. The same poster's group later tried Archie Zero and Archie 49 and reported both were "duds" not kept in rotation. The 2024 post compares Archie 49 to Draw2maha 49.
- Haney's 2025 Archie article covers a different variant ("Archie 777") and does not mention 49/0.
- Coach's Resorts World mix lists plain Archie, Drawmaha 49 and Drawmaha Zero but not Archie 49/0.
