## Deal & structure
- Dealt and bet exactly like razz: ante; 2 down + 1 up; then three more up cards and a final down card; five betting rounds; small bet on 3rd and 4th street, big bet on 5th, 6th and 7th street (WSOP intro).
- Bring-in: the HIGHEST door card, ace low, ties broken by suit with K♠ highest (BARGE 2.50). The WSOP text just says it is "dealt like seven card razz."
- 3rd-street action begins with the bring-in; on later streets the player whose up cards show the lowest Razz-style hand acts first (BARGE; The Festival).
- Open pair on 4th street does not change the limit (razz structure; BARGE structure is "same as Razz").
- Check-raise allowed (WSOP 329). Stud misdeal rules apply (WSOP 328). WSOP allows up to 8 players (WSOP 330).
- "Super" form: 4 hole cards and 1 up, betting, then discard two before 4th street (like super-stud). Haney (Card Player, 2026) says both Razzdugi and Razzdeucey are "usually played as 'super'" in low/medium-stakes mixed games; the card-room text I found does not give an official "Super Razzdugi" rule. [See super-stud for the shared discard procedure.]

## Showdown / what wins
- Two halves, no qualifier on either:
  - **Razz half:** best five-card A-5 low (ace low, straights/flushes ignored; wheel is best).
  - **Badugi half:** best four-card Badugi from your seven cards: four cards of four different ranks AND four different suits; ace low; lower is better; best is A-2-3-4 all different suits. If nobody has a four-card Badugi, the best three-card badugi (then two-card, then one-card) wins the Badugi half, so the pot is always split between two halves (Card Player Lifestyle; The Festival; BARGE).
  - You may use different cards for each half: five for Razz, any four for Badugi from the same seven.
- A scoop needs both: e.g., A-2-3-4 in four suits plus a 5 of any suit scoops (WSOP 326).
- "Most commonly the pot will be split between the best Badugi hand and the best Razz hand" (WSOP 327).
- **Odd chip: goes to the Razz hand** "since there is no true five-card hand" in Badugi (WSOP 331, 153); BARGE treats the five-card hand as the "high" side the same way.
- Ties within a half split that half (BARGE odd-chip convention); see seven-card-stud for the stud odd-chip rules.

## Rooms disagree
- **Which Badugi?** Razzdugi uses A-low badugi (A-2-3-4 nuts). Do NOT confuse with Razzdeucey (aces high; 2-7 low). WSOP's "Razzduci (Razzdeucey)" paragraph is internally contradictory (see razzdeucey).
- **Who's on the bring-in:** BARGE spells out K♠; WSOP doesn't say (assumes razz).
- **Naming:** Haney calls A-5 Razz+Badugi "Razzdacey" and says it used to be called "Super Razzdugi"; WSOP, BARGE, Card Player Lifestyle and The Festival use "Razzdugi".
- **Variants:** "Action Razzdugi" (BARGE 2.3): same as Razzdugi but the Razz half uses Action Razz's face-card qualifier (Jack/Queen/King among your seven cards, any qualifier beats a non-qualifier; the Badugi half doesn't need a face card). "2-7 Razzdugi" = Razzdeucey (aces high, straights/flushes bad on the 5-card half) — separate dossier.
- **How many players:** WSOP says up to 8; Wikipedia's generic stud/razz pages say 8 may trigger the community-card fallback (not specific to this game).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Thinking suits are irrelevant (they are for plain razz): in Razzdugi a repeated suit kills your Badugi even though it's fine for Razz; a pair hurts Razz but doesn't have to hurt Badugi (The Festival).
- Assuming the pot goes to one winner: it's always two halves. Even a "junk" 3-card badugi can win its half.
- Forgetting a hand strong in only one half can be worth playing for half the pot, and it can still lose half (The Festival: "you don't need to win both sides").
- Over-valuing hands like 3-2-2-A (great for Razz, poor Badugi because of suits) — and the reverse.
- Mixing it up with Badacey/Badeucey (draw games) or with Razzdeucey (aces high).

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, 2026): Razz itself is "not very popular" in low/medium-stakes mixed games, where "the most favored stud lowball variants are Razz-deucey and Razz-dacey"; both are usually played "super".
- The Festival (Estonia/European series) says Razzdugi makes its first-ever appearance on The Festival Series schedule at Malta 2026 (a stand-alone event), as an article published July 31, 2026.
- Appears in the WSOP cardroom "mix" examples (must-move examples name "Razzdugi" and "Razzduci"), BARGE's 65-game rulebook, and Card Player Lifestyle's Mixed Game Festival list.
