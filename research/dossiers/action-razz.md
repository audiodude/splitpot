## Deal & structure
- Played exactly like razz (ante; 2 down + 1 up; up/up/up; last card down; five rounds; small bet 3rd/4th street, big bet 5th-7th; low hand showing acts first on later streets). Only the showdown changes.
- **Bring-in:** BARGE and Raymer: the HIGHEST door card (K♠ highest) brings it in, as in razz.
- Origin: invented at the summer 2020 BARGE by Kevin Un and other BARGErs (Raymer, Card Player 2021); the BARGE rulebook lists it as "a creation of Kevin Un" and credits Cliff Matthews with the terms "Face" and "Butt" (the latter "believed", by the rulebook).

## Showdown / what wins
- **Qualifier (face card):** at showdown, a hand "qualifies" only if at least one of the player's SEVEN cards is a Jack, Queen or King. The paint card does NOT have to be in the five-card low hand you play (BARGE; Raymer).
- A qualifying hand ("Face") beats any non-qualifying hand ("Butt"), regardless of how good the low is. Example (BARGE): A-2-3-4-5-6-7 with no paint (the nut razz hand) LOSES to K-K-K-K-Q-Q-Q (queens full of kings), because the second hand holds paint.
- Among qualifiers, the winner is the best five-card A-5 low hand (ace low, no straights/flushes) from their seven cards. Non-qualifiers are excluded.
- If NO player at showdown qualifies, or if ALL qualify, ordinary razz rules decide (BARGE; Raymer).
- BARGE example: P1 2♥2♦3♠3♣4♦5♠7♣ (no paint), P2 A♥A♦2♠4♣4♥6♠8♣ (no paint), P3 3♥5♦7♠8♦9♥Q♠K♣ (paint): P3 wins with 9-8-7-5-3. If there were a side pot between P1 and P2 only, P1's seven-low (7-5-4-3-2) beats P2's eight-low.
- Ties split, odd chip by stud rules.

## Rooms disagree
- **Nobody qualifies:** BARGE and Raymer — fall back to normal razz. Card Player Lifestyle's mixed-game rules page says "if no player's hand qualifies at showdown, the pot is split among the players who made it to showdown" (a different rule, apparently an error or a house version).
- **Bring-in card:** BARGE/Raymer: highest door card (K♠). Haney (Card Player, Jan 2026): "The highest card starting with the 10♠ brings in the action as it is considered the worst card to have" — i.e., Haney describes the ten as the bring-in; the sources are not reconcilable from text alone.
- **Super version:** Haney advises playing it with two down cards only ("otherwise it's too easy to get a face card"); he says it "can be played as 'super'". BARGE has an "Action Razzdugi" version (Razzdugi with an Action Razz five-card half; the Badugi half has no face-card requirement).
- **How widely spread:** Haney: "Action Razz isn't that widely known." It is a festival/home-game invention; it is in BARGE's rulebook and Card Player Lifestyle's mixed-game festival list but not in WSOP, Robert's or TDA rulebooks.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Believing a wheel always wins: a wheel with no J/Q/K LOSES to any qualifying hand (even a terrible one).
- Thinking the paint card must be part of your five low cards. It only has to be among your seven (e.g., A-3-J-4-5-7-9 is a qualified seven-low).
- Folding when you are the bring-in with a jack up: unlike plain razz the bring-in can be a good hand (Raymer: about one-third of the time opponents never catch a paint card).
- Calling down heads-up on 7th with "garbage" when you hold paint and your opponent shows none (Raymer's example: you can beat a wheel with K-high low or a pair for low).

## Lore & reputation
- Raymer (2004 WSOP Main Event winner; Card Player, Jan 2021): the point of the game is that the bring-in in razz is "essentially dead money," and Kevin Un wanted a stud-lowball game where the bring-in can have a good hand. He called for it to be a staple and joked about an eventual bracelet.
- Haney (Card Player, 2026) says he played it a few times: "it certainly lived up to its name and was a lot of fun," and notes about 70% of A-2-4 starters will find a paint card by 7th street. He recommends it for "an adventurous and easy-going crew."
- Inclusion note: this is a notable variant (invented 2020), not a room staple; mention only as a "variants" blurb under razz.
