## Deal & structure
Same as `drawmaha`: 5 hole cards, blinds, betting, 3-card flop, betting, ONE draw (WSOP 1-3 cards or stand pat), turn + betting, river + betting. Limit is the usual form; WSOP allows pot/no-limit too.

## Showdown / what wins
Pot splits in half, no qualifier.
1. **Omaha-high half:** exactly 2 hole cards + exactly 3 board cards, standard high ranking.
2. **High-dugi half:** the best four-card "dugi" from your five hole cards (board does not play). A dugi = four cards all of DIFFERENT SUITS (ranks may repeat). Ranking is normal poker high ranking applied to those four cards, with aces high:
   - Best possible is A♣A♠A♦A♥ (four aces, one of each suit) — NOT A-K-Q-J rainbow. Haney explicitly points this out as a common surprise.
   - Order (Cardplayer Lifestyle): four-of-a-kind dugi, then three-of-a-kind dugi, two-pair dugi, one-pair dugi, no-pair dugi (four different ranks and suits; this is the lowest kind of dugi), then non-dugi hands after that (e.g. three of a kind with a repeated suit).
   - Practical result (Haney): the winning hand is usually a single high pair with four suits, e.g. K♣K♠10♦4♥; players should assume that is what the draw side looks like unless action says otherwise.
   - "Any four-card dugi beats any hand that is not a dugi" (Cardplayer Lifestyle). Details of ordering among non-dugi hands (3-card dugis etc.) are not spelled out in the sources I read [UNVERIFIED].
3. A single player can scoop. Ties split that half. Odd chip: by position clockwise from the button (WSOP).

## Rooms disagree
- **Name.** WSOP: "Badugi High". Coach's Resorts World mix: "Drawmaha High-Dugi". Cardplayer Lifestyle: "Drawmaha Hi-dugi". Haney: "Dramaha High-Dugi". Same game.
- **Detail gap:** WSOP's text only says the Badugi game "can also be played in a high variation. The best hand being As, Ah, Ad, Ac"; it does not list the ordering. The detail above is from Card Player writers, not the rule sheet.
- Draw cap and turn-before-draw dealing as in `drawmaha`.
- Not to be confused with `badugi-hi-lo` (Badugi Hi-Low, where the pot is split between lowest badugi and "highest low card" badugi) — that is a separate Coach's-mix game, not Drawmaha.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Thinking the nut hand is a high rainbow like A-K-Q-J: it's A-A-A-A (all four suits).
- Forgetting that the dugi still needs four different suits, so a pair of kings with only three suits (e.g. K♣K♠10♦4♦) is NOT a dugi.
- Believing a trips dugi is a monster: Haney points out trips dugi (e.g. 7♦7♥7♣3♠) is frustrating because it has little Omaha value and is hard to scoop with.
- Chasing the Omaha half without a good dugi hand: Haney says many players are "appropriately fearful of running into a superior Draw hand" and says a pair of eights dugi is usually behind.

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, Apr 2025) rates Dramaha High-Dugi 3/10: "many detractors", new players have trouble grasping what wins in the draw half, and there is less action. He still includes it as a real variant.
- Coach's $8-$16 mix at Resorts World spread it in 2023 (Card Player). It also appears in the Cardplayer Lifestyle Mixed Game Festival rule sheet and a Poker: All the Games podcast episode (May 2024) devoted to "Dramaha Hi-Dugi" (audio only; not transcribed).
- Haney also mentions a Minnesota home-game cousin, "Dramaha Dugi 2.0", which is a three-way split (Omaha high, low dugi, high dugi) — home-only, not documented in any room rule sheet.
