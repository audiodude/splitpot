## Deal & structure
- Played **exactly like Archie** (see `archie`): five-card triple-draw high-low split, button and blinds, three draws with a betting round after each, usually fixed limit - with one change.
- **The change:** a single **community card** is put in the middle of the table "at the end of the initial deal" (WSOP). Card Player Lifestyle says it is dealt "before action begins". The WSOP text doesn't say whether it is exposed; a board card only works face up, but that is not stated. [INFERENCE: face up]
- Each player still holds five hole cards and draws up to five at each of three draws; the community card never changes.

## Showdown / what wins
- **High half:** the best five-card high hand that can be made from the player's five cards plus the community card (six cards available, best five play). Card Player Lifestyle's example: a player holds A-2-3-4-K and the community card is a 5 - he has a wheel (straight) for high but no qualifying low.
- **Qualifier for high is a pair of sixes or better** (WSOP: "6's or better will be the qualifier for the high hand"). I read "6's or better" as a pair of sixes, since Archie's alternative qualifier is also a pair of sixes (BARGE). [INFERENCE: the WSOP phrasing doesn't say "pair"]
- **Low half:** unchanged from Archie: ace-to-five low from the player's five hole cards only, "The low hand won't be using the community card"; eight-high or better qualifies.
- No-qualifier outcomes: WSOP says "All the rules of Archie apply" except the listed changes, so by that rule: no qualifying high -> best qualifying low wins all; no qualifying low -> best qualifying high wins all; neither -> pot split among all remaining players (Archie rules 293-295). [INFERENCE that the carry-over of these rules is intended]
- Example: you hold A♥ 2♦ 3♠ 4♣ K♦ and the board is 5♥. High: wheel straight 5-4-3-2-A using the board 5 (qualifies; beats all pairs). Low: your five cards only, A-2-3-4-K, king-high, does not qualify for low.
- Odd chip rule is not given in the Ari text; see Archie for the WSOP conflict (high gets odd chip in a high-low split vs worst position in mixed games).

## Rooms disagree
- **High qualifier:** WSOP says sixes for Ari. Card Player Lifestyle's rule sheet says "Qualifiers for both high and low remain the same" as its Archie entry (whose qualifier there is a pair of nines). So the WSOP says 6s while the festival rule sheet implies 9s - a real conflict. Ask before playing.
- **Where the board card goes:** most sources just say "community card"; none I read describe whether it is dealt face up before or after the first betting round beyond "at the end of the initial deal" (WSOP) / "before action begins" (Card Player Lifestyle).
- **Is it a real mix game?** Evidence is thin: the WSOP Live-Action rules and Card Player Lifestyle's Mixed Game Festival rule sheet list it; Haney mentions the same idea as an optional add-on to Archie ("A community card can also be deployed for use only on the high side"). I did not find a poker-room game list naming it. Treat as a niche/organizer-specific variant.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming the community card helps the low hand. It does not; it only plays for high.
- Assuming the 6-pair qualifier is the same as regular Archie's nines (and vice versa) - see disagreement.
- Not valuing the board card as a straight-draw completer: Haney's example: with the board card, a low draw like 3-4-5 has 16 community cards (aces, deuces, sixes, sevens) that give a straight draw, which makes low draws more able to scoop than in plain Archie.
- Counting the board card in the low hand.

## Lore & reputation
- No sourced origin. Haney (Card Player) describes the community-card version only as an alternative that "can greatly benefit the low draws".
