## Deal & structure
- **Button game with blinds** (sometimes an ante is added): Robert's/WSOP: "normally played with one or more blinds, sometimes with an ante added." Pagat says it is possible with an ante but "in formal games it is more often played with blinds".
- Each player gets **five cards face down**, then betting; **you must open with a bet or fold** (no checking pre-draw to see a free draw) - Robert's/WSOP: "Players are required to open with a bet or fold." Some structures let the big blind be called; others require a minimum open of double the big blind.
- **One draw**, then a second betting round. After the draw, a player may discard 0-5 (Robert's: up to four consecutive; the fifth card after everyone else has drawn; WSOP: up to five consecutive). Exposed cards at the draw are replaced after everyone else has drawn (Robert's #9).
- **Limit** is the traditional structure and doubles after the draw ("In limit poker, the usual structure has the limit double after the draw"; Robert's notes Northern California as an exception, i.e. same size both rounds there). Pagat: "some play that the size of the bet is doubled after the draw and others play that it remains the same."
- **The joker** ("fitter" or "bug"): traditionally added to the deck; it plays as the **lowest card not already in your hand** (so 8-6-4-A-joker is 8-6-4-2-A) (Pagat, Robert's #1, WSOP #281, BARGE, Wikipedia).
- **Kill pots:** the usual way to get dealt in right away as a new player is to kill the pot (post double the big blind) instead of waiting for the big blind (Robert's rule 3; WSOP #268). A kill doubles the limits (Robert's Section 13).
- Check-raise: **not allowed** in limit A-5 unless players are told otherwise (Robert's; BARGE "no check-and-raise allowed"). WSOP Live-Action #282 says check-raise **is permitted**.
- The **Sevens Rule** (limit): see below.
- Hand must be exactly five cards after the draw; more or fewer = a fouled (dead) hand (Robert's #12, WSOP #275). Before the draw a short hand is live (you get the missing cards).

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand wins the whole pot. **Ace-to-five**: aces are always low; **straights and flushes do not count against you**; any hand with no pair beats any hand with a pair. **Best hand: 5-4-3-2-A (the wheel or "bicycle"), any suits.** Next: 6-4-3-2-A, 6-5-3-2-A, 6-5-4-2-A, 6-5-4-3-A, 6-5-4-3-2, 7-4-3-2-A... (Wikipedia, Pagat, BARGE).
- Compare the highest card first, then next: 7-5-4-3-2 beats 7-6-3-2-A (Pagat). With pairs: A-A beats 2-2 (Robert's), and lower remaining cards win ties: AA654 beats AA732 (BARGE). Any no-pair hand beats any pair hand, so K-J-8-6-4 beats A-A-2-3-4.
- Hands are spoken by their top card(s): "a seven" = seven-high, "eight-six" etc.; smooth (low remaining cards) vs rough.
- BARGE count: in A-5 there is 1 wheel, 5 sixes and 15 sevens - 21 hands that are seven-or-better - and 35 eights, 56 hands that are eight-or-better.
- **Cards speak** (you read your hand off the table), but you may not claim a better hand than you hold; if you miscall and cause another player to foul, your hand is dead (Robert's #15, WSOP #279). A player spreading a hand containing a pair **must announce "pair"** or risk losing the pot if another player folds/fouls because of it (Robert's #16, WSOP #280).
- **Sevens rule** (Robert's, limit only; assumed in use unless posted otherwise): after the draw, if you check a hand of **seven-high or better** and it is the best hand, all post-draw action is void for you - you can win only what was in the pot before the draw. If you check and the hand is beaten, you lose the pot and any calls. Pagat's version: if you check a 7-or-better and it wins, you only win the pre-draw pot; chips bet after the draw go to a separate pot you cannot win; betting, calling or raising makes you eligible for everything provided you did not check first. BARGE's Appendix B (from Michael Wiesenberg's Card Player column): "you must bet a 7 or better whenever you are either first to bet, or those ahead of you have passed... In some clubs you forfeit the entire pot." There is a half-bet all-in wrinkle (Robert's, BARGE): if a short all-in bet is less than a full bet, a 7-or-better must complete to a full bet, not just call.
  - Illustrative example (my own, applying the Robert's/BARGE wording): after the draw you hold 6-4-3-2-A and are first to act. You check. If everyone checks, you win only the pot as it stood at the draw. If an opponent then bets and you call: should your hand be best, his post-draw bet is returned to him and you still win only the pre-draw pot; should his hand be better, you lose your call as well (BARGE/Wiesenberg). The correct play is to bet it.
- In **no-limit/pot-limit** California lowball there is **no sevens rule** and check-raise is allowed (Robert's NL/PL #4-5; BARGE).
- Ties split the pot. Odd chip: see WSOP #152-153 (button game: first hand clockwise from the button; Mixed-Games simplification: worst position).

## Rooms disagree
- **Exposed card before the draw (limit):** Robert's: an exposed card of seven or under **must be taken**; a card above seven is replaced after the deal (the first exposed card burns). WSOP Live-Action #283: **all exposed cards are replaced** after the deal. In NL/PL: Robert's - must take an exposed A, 2, 3, 4 or 5, replace any other; WSOP #290: all exposed cards replaced.
- **Joker default:** Robert's: the joker "is assumed to be in use unless the contrary is posted." WSOP: "Jokers are not used unless their inclusion is specifically posted." BARGE: "usually played with a joker/bug." Pagat: "often played with a joker added".
- **Check-raise:** Robert's/BARGE: no check-raise in limit; WSOP #282: permitted.
- **Draw size:** Robert's four consecutive then the fifth after others vs WSOP five consecutive vs BARGE zero to five.
- **Blind structure:** Pagat describes "The dealer places a small blind and the next two players to the left of the dealer each place a big blind equal to the minimum bet" (i.e. a dealer small blind plus two big blinds). Robert's/WSOP say "one or more blinds" (single-blind and multiple-blind games both exist; rules for each are given). Rooms vary.
- **Sevens rule penalty:** void post-draw winnings only (Robert's, Pagat) vs "forfeit the entire pot" in some clubs (BARGE/Wiesenberg).
- **Bet doubling after the draw:** doubles (Robert's usual, Pagat "some") vs same size (Northern California per Robert's).
- **Max players:** WSOP says as many as six.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating straights/flushes as bad (that's 2-7). Here 5-4-3-2-A is the nut and A-2-3-4-6 beats any 7-high.
- Checking a seven-or-better after the draw in a limit game (sevens rule).
- Forgetting you must open or fold pre-draw; limping is only possible if the big blind can be called in that structure.
- Spreading a pair without saying "pair".
- Thinking the joker is a wild card: it can only be the lowest card not in your hand (it can never pair you).
- Thinking you must worry about straights/flushes: you don't. Only pairs and high cards count against you.

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: "the primary poker game played in California during the heyday of Gardena in the 1970s"; popularity has lessened since stud and community-card games arrived.
- BARGE: "This was the big game in California, until someone realized Hold'em wasn't illegal." BARGE spread a NL California lowball bounty tournament in 2021.
- WSOP history: limit ace-to-five draw was a bracelet staple from 1971 (Johnny Moss) to 2004 (Norm Ketchum; $1,500 limit A-5, 184 entries); no-limit A-5 events with and without the joker ran in the 1980s (Billy Baxter 1982, David Angel 1983 with joker, Mike Cox 1986 with joker and rebuys) (PokerNews "Defunct bracelet events"; note: that article also wrongly says "straights and flushes count" in A-5 - they don't, per every rules source).
