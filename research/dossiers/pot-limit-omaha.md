## Deal & structure
- Button + small blind + big blind (WSOP: one blind, several blinds, antes or a mix are also allowed). Each player gets **four** face-down hole cards. Betting round, burn, 3-card flop, betting, burn, turn, betting, burn, river, final betting. Same street structure as Hold'em; action post-flop starts with the first live player left of the button.
- Pot-limit betting: the maximum bet is the size of the pot; the maximum raise is the amount that would be in the pot after you call (WSOP). WSOP worked example: pot is $100, someone bets $50; the next player calls $50 and can raise by $200, a total wager of $250. PokerNews gives the same method (pot $10, bet $5, raise-pot to $25 total). Minimum bet is the big blind.
- Live-room conventions from WSOP: the small blind is treated as a full big blind when computing pot size until the big blind has acted, so a $5/$10 game can open to a maximum of $40 (4x BB); pots may be rounded up to the next chip unit; an oversized chip put in without comment as a first bet counts as a pot-sized bet, and as a call if there is already a bet; an over-pot bet is corrected with the surplus returned unless action has already completed.
- WSOP's 3-blind live variant ($1-$2-$5 "3 Blind PLO/Big O"): the blinds are rounded up for the pot calculation; the quoted first-action options are $10, $15, $20 or pot ($25). A missed-blind player can post everything missed or wait for the big blind; only the $5 straddle part is live.
- Seats: PokerNews says 2-10 players. Wikipedia says up to eleven can be dealt in (4 x 11 + 3 burns + 5 board = 52), though six- and nine-handed are the norm.
- Fixed-limit Omaha high and no-limit Omaha high exist but pot-limit dominates (Pagat, Wikipedia: Omaha high "is often played with a pot limit betting structure").

## Showdown / what wins
- High hand only. Each player must use **exactly two of the four hole cards and exactly three of the five board cards** (WSOP, Wikipedia, Pagat, PokerNews all agree). You cannot play the board, play one hole card, or use three hole cards.
- Standard five-card poker rankings; aces high or low for straights (A-2-3-4-5 is a wheel). Flushes, straights, full houses and quads must be built from the 2-from-hand/3-from-board rule (e.g. one heart in hand plus four hearts on board is NOT a flush).
- Ties split evenly; in button games the odd chip goes to the first tied hand clockwise from the button (WSOP rule 153). In mixed-game rotations WSOP simplifies the odd chip to the worst position in button games.
- Side pots are split separately, last side pot first (WSOP).

## Rooms disagree
- **Pot-size rounding / small-blind treatment.** WSOP rounds the SB up to the BB (and the pot to chip units) for max-open calculations. The Poker TDA rules use exact pot math: their example is PLO, 100-200 blinds, pot-limit first bet for the first player to act = 700 (call 200 plus the 500 that would be in the pot), and a dead or short blind does not change that calculation (TDA assumes full blinds were posted). A 2+2 poster calls SB-rounding a common live tweak but not universal.
- **Max open size.** Exact pot math with $1/$2 blinds gives an open to $7; WSOP's rounding gives $8 (4 x BB).
- **Pot count and tabling.** TDA entitles players to a pot count in pot-limit only; proper tabling in Omaha means turning all four hole cards face up so the dealer and players can read the hand (TDA rule on tabling).
- **Blind structure.** Some live games (WSOP lists $1-$2-$5) use three blinds, others plain SB/BB; straddles are optional and rooms cap them (WSOP: one live straddle in 1-2-5 games, capped at $10).
- Whether PLO is Hi-only or Hi-Lo (PLO8) is posted per game; "Omaha" with no qualifier is ambiguous in North America vs Europe (Wikipedia: in Europe "Omaha" normally means high).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Using one hole card and four board cards for a flush or straight (the single most common error per PokerNews; their example: A♥Q♣7♦6♦ on 9♥4♥2♣J♥Q♥ does NOT have an ace-high flush, only a pair of queens).
- Overvaluing top set / two pair / non-nut flushes (PokerNews: two pair is "the most overplayed hand" by Hold'em converts; second-best straights and flushes lose most of the money).
- Miscalculating pot-sized raises. Tell the dealer "pot" and let them count.
- Forgetting that four hole cards means six two-card combinations and that wraps can have up to 20 outs (Wikipedia example J♥10♠7♦6♣ on 9♥8♠2♦).

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: casino executive Robert Turner brought Omaha into a casino setting by introducing it to Bill Boyd, who spread it at the Golden Nugget in Las Vegas under the name "Nugget Hold'em". Origin beyond that is "unknown" per Wikipedia. Pagat: introduced in the 1980s and quickly popular with casino and tournament players.
- Wikipedia: PLO is popular in Europe, online, and in high-stakes mixed games played in some American casinos; PokerNews calls it an "action game" and says it is a popular alternative to NLHE.
- It is the pot-limit Omaha slot in the WSOP Poker Players Championship: that event's 10-game mix has included pot-limit Omaha since 2015 (Wikipedia); H.O.R.S.E.H.A.T. (the PokerStars 8-game-style mix) also includes it (Wikipedia, H.O.R.S.E.).
- Room-menu evidence (from search-result snippets; the pages themselves were blocked or thin): Commerce Casino lists PLO as a game; Aria is described as the Strip's busiest PLO room; Wynn 2/5 PLO and Venetian 2/5 PLO "occasionally" appear in third-party room guides. Hustler Casino (Gardena) runs 5/5 and 5/10 PLO per PokerAtlas listing (snippet).
