## Deal & structure
- Button game. Tournaments (WSOP) use small blind + big blind AND an ante (WSOP 2025 rule 36 names "Deuce-to-Seven No-Limit Draw" as a blinds-plus-antes event). Cash games: blinds, sometimes with an ante; some structures require the opener to come in for at least 2x the big blind (Robert's Rules / WSOP Live-Action lowball intro).
- Each player gets 5 cards face down.
- Betting round 1 (pre-draw): starts left of the big blind. Players open with a bet (raise) or fold.
- The draw: one draw only. Starting with first active player left of the button, each player discards 0–5 and is dealt replacements; "pat" = keep all 5.
- Betting round 2 (post-draw): starts with first active player left of the button. Then showdown.
- No-limit is the standard (WSOP: "No-Limit draw games are generally played with a single draw"). Pot-limit and limit versions exist (Robert's Rules covers "No-limit and pot-limit lowball"; in limit lowball the bet usually doubles after the draw).

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand by deuce-to-seven rules wins the entire pot. Same ranking as 2-7 Triple Draw:
  - Aces are high only; straights and flushes count against you; pairs are bad.
  - Best hand 7-5-4-3-2 not all one suit; then 7-6-4-3-2, 7-6-5-3-2, 7-6-5-4-2, 8-5-4-3-2...
  - A-2-3-4-5 is ace-high, not a straight; it loses to any king-high no-pair hand.
- Compare from the highest card down; identical hands split.
- After the draw a player may check any hand without penalty — the lowball "sevens rule" does not apply in no-limit/pot-limit lowball (Robert's NL/PL #4; WSOP Live-Action #291). Check-raise allowed (Robert's; WSOP Live-Action #292).
- Cards speak, but miscalling your hand in a way that causes an opponent to foul can make your hand dead (Robert's #15).

## Rooms disagree
- Exposed card on the deal: Robert's Rules (NL/PL deuce-to-seven) — player must keep an exposed 2, 3, 4, 5 or 7; any other card, including a 6, is replaced (the logic: you're not entitled to learn an opponent lacks the nuts). WSOP tournament and WSOP Live-Action — every exposed card is replaced. After the draw, any exposed card is replaced (Robert's NL/PL #3).
- Ante: WSOP tournaments use blinds + ante; Robert's says lowball is "normally" blinds, "sometimes with an ante added".
- Betting: WSOP & PokerNews treat it as a no-limit game; Robert's and Wikipedia note limit forms (Kansas City lowball has been played limit too; Robert's says limit lowball doubles after the draw except in Northern California).
- Player max: WSOP no-limit single draw 7; WSOP Live-Action caps deuce-to-seven lowball at 6.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Thinking A-2-3-4-5 is the nuts (it's the A-5 nuts; in 2-7 it's ace-high garbage).
- Drawing two or more cards routinely — PokerNews strategy guidance says never play two-card draws in NL single draw.
- Forgetting there's only ONE draw: a one-card draw that misses is usually a bluff-or-fold spot.
- Breaking a pat hand that only improves by a tiny amount (PokerNews example: don't break 10-9-8-7-2-type hands).
- Assuming a "seven or better must bet" rule like in limit California lowball — not used in NL 2-7.

## Lore & reputation
- One of the oldest WSOP games: 2-7 lowball debuted at the 1973 WSOP; first champion Aubrey Day (WSOP.com). PokerNews: at least one event at every WSOP except one (1999 not held) since 1973; $10,000 championship buy-in since 2009, plus a $1,500 event since 2010.
- "Billy Baxter Lowball": Billy Baxter won the deuce-to-seven draw title in 1975, 1978, 1982, 1987 and 1993 per PokerNews' winners table; WSOP.com says all seven of his bracelets came in lowball forms and he holds 16 WSOP lowball cashes.
- WSOP.com: the game was added because it was a favorite of high-stakes cash players, and is rarely spread in public card rooms — it lives mostly in high-stakes games and the WSOP.
- Other notable champions in PokerNews' table: Stu Ungar (1981), Doyle Brunson (1976), Erik Seidel (1998, 2007), Jason Mercier (2016), Brian Rast (2018).
- "Kansas City" name: Wikipedia and Robert's both use it for deuce-to-seven; origin of the name not documented in sources consulted.
