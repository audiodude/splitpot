## Deal & structure
- Button game with a small blind and big blind (tournaments start with SB/BB; WSOP 2025 rules). No community cards, no antes in the standard limit game.
- Each player gets 5 cards face down, one at a time, starting left of the button.
- Betting round 1 (pre-draw): first to act is the player left of the big blind; normal blind-game options (call, raise, fold; BB has the option).
- Draw 1: starting with the first active player left of the button and going clockwise, each player discards 0–5 cards face down and receives the same number. Drawing none = "standing pat". At WSOP, players place all discards first; the dealer then announces each player's draw count before dealing, which locks the numbers in (WSOP 2025). A player taking 5 new cards gets all five consecutively at WSOP (Robert's Rules for single-draw lowball instead deals 4, then the 5th after everyone else).
- Betting round 2: starts with first active player left of the button (i.e., the small blind if still in).
- Draw 2, betting round 3, draw 3, betting round 4 (same order each time), then showdown.
- Limit bet sizing: small bet on rounds 1 and 2 (pre-draw and after first draw), big bet (2x) on rounds 3 and 4 (after second and third draws). Limit play allows a bet and four raises multiway (Robert's/WSOP Live-Action rule 267). WSOP lists a Pot-Limit 2-7 Triple Draw in Dealer's Choice; same mechanics, pot-limit betting.
- Running out of cards: if the stub can't complete a draw, the dealer reshuffles the earlier discards (plus burns/muck) but NOT the discards from the round in progress; the last stub card is only used if it completes the round (WSOP 2025; WSOP Live-Action Section X).
- A card is burned before each draw round even if everyone stands pat (WSOP 2025).
- Max 6 players at WSOP because a full table runs the deck out.

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand wins the whole pot; you must play all five of your cards.
- Deuce-to-seven ranking = normal poker ranking turned upside down: aces are HIGH only; straights and flushes COUNT (they are bad, ranked as straights/flushes, not "fouled"); pairs are bad.
- Best hand: 7-5-4-3-2 not all one suit ("number one", sometimes called the "wheel" in 2-7). Next: 7-6-4-3-2, 7-6-5-3-2, 7-6-5-4-2, 8-5-4-3-2, 8-6-4-3-2...
- A-2-3-4-5 is NOT a straight in 2-7: it is just an ace-high no-pair hand (beats a pair and other ace-high hands, but loses to any king-high). A pair of aces is the highest (worst) pair.
- 7-6-5-4-3 is a straight and loses to any no-pair hand. Any five suited cards = flush = bad.
- Compare no-pair hands from the top card down (8-6-x beats 8-7-x). Exact ties split.
- Cards speak, but you may not claim a better hand than you hold; a verbal miscall that causes another player to foul (muck) can make your hand dead (Robert's Rules Section 11 #15; WSOP Live-Action #279). Spreading a paired hand without saying "pair" risks the pot if it causes someone else to foul (Robert's #16; WSOP Live #280).
- Odd chip (tournaments): first seat left of the button (WSOP 2025 rule 73). APT house rule: odd chip to worst position.

## Rooms disagree
- Exposed card before the draw: Robert's Rules (deuce-to-seven) — a 2, 3, 4, 5 or 7 exposed on the deal must be kept; any other card (incl. a 6) is replaced. WSOP 2025 tournament rules and WSOP Live-Action rules — ALL exposed cards on the initial deal are replaced; a second exposed card on the initial deal = misdeal (tournament).
- Five-card draws: Robert's — four cards dealt, fifth after everyone else draws; WSOP — all five consecutively.
- Changing your draw: Robert's/WSOP Live-Action allow changing the number until a card is dealt or someone acts on your number; WSOP tournament rules say the number can't change once discards are placed, though you may swap which cards you discard before the round is dealt.
- Max players: WSOP 6; PokerNews says typically 2–7.
- Betting structure: almost always limit (PokerNews, Pagat); WSOP Dealer's Choice also lists pot-limit 2-7 TD.
- New-player entry in lowball (WSOP Live-Action #268): wait for BB, post BB, or kill the pot for 2x BB; Robert's lists only wait or kill.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating the ace as low. In 2-7 an ace is the worst card; A-2-3-4-5 is ace-high trash, not a wheel.
- Forgetting straights/flushes: 6-5-4-3-2 is a straight (bad); a five-card one-suit low is a flush (bad).
- Expecting draw order/action to start left of the big blind after the draw; post-draw rounds start with the first active player left of the button.
- Not tracking the betting double: the big bet starts after the SECOND draw, not after the first.
- Calling a hand wrong out loud (miscalling) and causing an opponent to muck — can kill your hand.
- Hold'em instincts on hand values: smooth 8-lows are near the top of the ranking list (8-5-4-3-2 is the 5th-best hand possible per PokerNews' table).

## Lore & reputation
- 2-7 lowball itself has been a WSOP game since 1973 in its single-draw form (first champion Aubrey Day per WSOP.com).
- Triple draw origins are disputed (WSOP.com 2011 article): David Sklansky credited limit "double-draw" lowball at Vegas World in the early 1980s; others point to "ten-handed triple-draw lowball" at Amarillo Slim's Super Bowl of Poker (Reno/Lake Tahoe, ~1979–1984).
- A $1,500 limit "Triple Draw" event ran at WSOP 2002 and 2003 (listed in PokerNews' ace-to-five history table — likely ace-to-five triple draw; [uncertain which ranking]). PokerNews says 2-7 Triple Draw itself was introduced in 2004 and has run every WSOP since 2007; Wikipedia's Draw poker article agrees (2004 event; every WSOP from 2007).
- PokerNews: a $10,000 2-7 Triple Draw Championship was added in 2014; Phil Ivey won it in 2024.
- Part of the $50K Poker Players Championship rotation since 2010 (Wikipedia, Draw poker).
- Reputation: the flagship draw game of mixed games; "snowing" (standing pat on garbage as a bluff) is a famous concept.
