## Deal & structure
- Exactly PLO, but every player gets **five** hole cards instead of four. Blinds, burn cards, flop/turn/river and the four betting rounds are unchanged. Pot-limit betting as in `pot-limit-omaha`.
- Hand-making rule is unchanged: exactly two hole cards plus exactly three board cards. Five hole cards give ten two-card combinations (versus six in 4-card PLO).
- Table size: WSOP caps its 5-card Omaha games (Big O, Big Easy, double board) at **8 players**. [INFERENCE, my arithmetic] 9 x 5 = 45 hole cards leaves 7 cards, fewer than the 8 needed for 3 burns + 5 board cards, so 8 is the natural live maximum.
- WSOP run-it-twice note: in 5-card PLO variants with 8 players dealt in, only 2 burn cards are used when running it twice pre-flop (one burn per board) because of the deck size.
- Fixed-limit versions exist and are spread live (PokerAtlas listing for Hustler Casino Gardena: 6/12 limit 5-Card Omaha Hi Only, "runs always"; search-result snippet, page itself blocked 403). Pot-limit is the usual online structure.

## Showdown / what wins
- High hand only (some rooms also spread the Hi-Lo version, which belongs under Big O / 5-card Omaha 8; see Related games).
- Best five-card high hand using exactly two of five hole cards + three of five board cards. Standard rankings, aces high or low in straights, ties split, odd chip by position clockwise from the button.
- Because players hold more combinations, the average winner is stronger than in 4-card PLO (CardPlayer: winning hands are stronger, marginal holdings generate small value; premium-vs-premium equities run closer, e.g. roughly 55/45 vs 65/35 per PLO365 snippet; treat the exact numbers as vendor estimates).

## Rooms disagree
- **"Big O" naming.** WSOP uses "The Big O" for 5-card Omaha **Hi-Lo** (8 or better), and the Commerce Casino game page describes Big O as a variation on Omaha Hi/Lo with five hole cards. Wikipedia and Pagat use "Big O"/"five-card Omaha" for the high-only game as well and say there are five-card versions of both Omaha High and Omaha/8. Always confirm "Big O" means hi-lo and "PLO5"/"5-card Omaha Hi" means high only before calling a game.
- High-only 5-card Omaha is usually pot-limit online but limit in at least one big SoCal room (Hustler 6/12).
- 6-card Omaha, Courchevel, and "bomb pot" 5-card double-board variants are separate games; some rooms (e.g. Aspers 888/ Hippodrome in London per 2+2 snippets) let the button choose 4/5/6 cards (see `6-card-plo`).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming the exactly-two rule relaxes with more cards (it does not; CardPlayer, PLO365, CanadianPoker, Wikipedia all restate it).
- Playing PLO4 hand charts unchanged: nut potential matters more, two pair/top set/non-nut flush are weaker, more multiway pots (CardPlayer, PLO365 snippets).
- Failing to realise more of your hand blocks opponents' draws and that dangling cards that connect to nothing are dead weight.

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: five-card Omaha ("Big O") began appearing in Southern California in 2008 and had spread to most of the card rooms in the area by the end of 2008 (marked [citation needed] on Wikipedia, so treat as unverified); smaller card rooms in particular liked the extra action. The Poker Wiki (fandom, snippet) repeats the same text.
- Wikipedia also calls five-card Omaha "very popular in the Southeastern United States as a home game" and says some online rooms offer five-card Omaha, six-card Omaha and Courchevel.
- Live evidence it is a current high-stakes/streamed staple: PokerAtlas lists a daily 6/12 limit 5-card Omaha Hi game at Hustler Casino Gardena; WSOP's 2026 live-action rules give 5-card PLO its own run-it-twice rule and a 5-card double-board bomb-pot game (see `5-card-double-board-bomb-pot-plo`).
