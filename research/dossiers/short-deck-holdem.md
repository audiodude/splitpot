## Deal & structure
- Hold'em played with a **36-card deck**: all 2s, 3s, 4s and 5s removed (WSOP, Wikipedia, PokerNews, PokerListings agree). Sixes are the lowest cards.
- Two hole cards, five board cards, standard Hold'em streets (flop, turn, river with burns). Pot-limit or no-limit; the "Short Deck Ante-Only" form is the usual one.
- **Antes, not blinds** (WSOP, PokerNews, PokerCoaching): every player posts an ante and the button posts a larger one, normally double. WSOP example: in a $200/$400 NLHE game all players post $200 and the button posts $400. Action starts to the left of the button (Triton ante-only rules per PokerCoaching/search result: button acts last, button never dead).
- WSOP also lists a **very restricted pre-flop rule**: pre-flop a player may only fold or put in exactly three times the big blind; **no raising, limping or straddling pre-flop**. Post-flop three rounds are pot-limit or no-limit as usual. This is stricter than what online rooms and Triton-style games play (open raise sizing, limping allowed). [Flag: WSOP wording says "big blind" although the game uses an ante structure; read as the button ante amount.]
- WSOP caps Hold'em short deck at **9 players** and short-deck **PLO at 6 players** (4 hole cards).

## Showdown / what wins
- High hand only, best five of seven cards (Hold'em: any combination of hole and board cards, including playing the board).
- **Ace plays high or low:** A-6-7-8-9 is the lowest straight; A-K-Q-J-10 the highest. 9-8-7-6-A is a straight (WSOP rule 366).
- **Hand ranking changes (WSOP):** Royal Flush, Straight Flush, Four of a Kind, **Flush**, Full House, Straight, Three of a Kind, Two Pair, One Pair, High Card. i.e. **flush beats full house**, straight beats three of a kind (WSOP list order).
- Why: with 36 cards each suit has 9 cards, so flushes are harder than full houses (PokerListings: with suited hole cards + two suited board cards a flush has 9 outs in a full deck but only 5 in short deck).
- Ties split; odd chip by position from the button in button games (WSOP rule 153).

## Rooms disagree
- **Three of a kind vs straight.** WSOP, PokerNews, Wikipedia ("in most games"), and PokerCoaching (calling it the Triton/online standard) rank **straight above trips**. PokerListings states that the official 6+ ranking is Flush > Full House > **Three of a Kind > Straight**, and says some casinos instead value the straight higher; Wikipedia footnotes that the theoretical-probability ordering would put trips above a straight and that some rooms use normal Hold'em rankings. **Always confirm at the table** (PokerNews explicitly notes some variations rank trips higher).
- **Flush above full house.** All sources agree on this one in the usual game; Wikipedia notes some variants use standard Hold'em rankings entirely.
- **Betting structure.** WSOP: fold-or-3x pre-flop only; online/Triton: ante + button ante with normal NL raising, limping allowed (PokerCoaching says limping is common and cheap).
- **Name overlap with Manila.** Wikipedia's 6+ article says short deck is also known as Manila, while the Community-card-poker article defines Manila as a 32-card game (7s and up) where exactly two hole cards must be used. Don't merge the two without checking.
- Max seats: WSOP 9 (Hold'em) / 6 (PLO). 36-card arithmetic would allow more PLO seats, but WSOP caps at 6.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Forgetting flush > full house (PokerCoaching/PokerNews both call this the biggest beginner error); also forgetting A-6-7-8-9 is a straight (PokerNews: a 9-8 holder may not notice a straight on 6-7-A).
- Overvaluing sets, straights and top pair; pairs are weaker, straight draws stronger (open-ended draw about 8 outs of fewer unseen cards; "rule of 3 and 6" instead of 2 and 4 per PokerListings/PokerCoaching).
- Treating ace-king as dominating: equities run close; pocket aces hold up far less than in full-deck Hold'em (PokerCoaching: KK is only about a 53% favourite over AKs).

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: 6+ is a variation of Manila (created in 2010 or earlier), "a once popular game in Australia and among high stakes gamblers in Asia"; in 2015 Phil Ivey and Tom Dwan promoted the game in an online video showing the variant frequently played in Macau, Hong Kong and Manila. Triton Poker's 2018 series hosted the first televised six-plus tournaments; the first Triton Super High Roller HK$250,000 short deck ante-only event was won by Phil Ivey (US$617,396 first prize).
- PokerListings: PokerStars, partypoker, 888/GG and iPoker skins have offered it online. WSOP's 2026 live-action rules include it as a posted game with its own Hold'em and PLO versions, so it can be called in a US dealer's-choice room, but it remains a niche/high-stakes game rather than a regular mixed-game fixture.
