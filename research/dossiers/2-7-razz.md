## Deal & structure
- Same deal and betting structure as razz (ante; 2 down + 1 up; 4th/5th/6th up; 7th down; small bet on 3rd/4th, big bet on 5th-7th). Only the way the hands are ranked (and therefore who brings it in) changes.
- **Bring-in is the HIGHEST door card with the ACE HIGH**: A♠ is the highest possible card (then A♥, A♦, A♣, K♠, ...). BARGE: "because the game is played using Kansas City Lowball rules, aces are always high, so an ace is a high card for bring-in purposes." Haney (Card Player 2026) likewise says A♠ is the automatic bring-in in the 2-7 stud-split game Razzdeucey.
- Later streets: the lowest board (best 2-7 low) acts first. Because only 4 cards are ever face up, straights and flushes cannot show on the board; only pairs and high cards matter when comparing boards.
- Raise caps, short-stub handling, dead hand rules: as in seven-card-stud / razz.

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand under deuce-to-seven ranking wins the whole pot. No qualifier.
- Deuce-to-seven rules: **aces are HIGH only**; **straights and flushes count against you** (they are ordinary poker straights/flushes, i.e., bad); pairs are bad. A-2-3-4-5 is NOT a straight in this game (the ace is high, so it is just an ace-high hand).
- You pick the best five of your seven cards, avoiding straights/flushes and pairs when you can. Best possible hand: 7-5-4-3-2 with at least two suits (WSOP 238; Haney: "2-3-4-5-7 without a flush"). Haney's example: holding 7♥ 3♠ 4♥ 6♠ 10♦ J♥ 5♥, the best low is 3-4-5-6-10 (a ten-low), not a seven-low, because 3-4-5-6-7 would be a straight.
- Ties split; odd chip by stud rule (highest card by suit among the five cards; WSOP/TDA).

## Rooms disagree
- **Name:** WSOP calls it "Kansas City Razz" (rule 238); BARGE calls it "Deuce-to-Seven Razz" and says it uses Kansas City Lowball rules; Haney/Card Player calls it "2-7 Razz". All three describe the same ranking (ace high, flush/straight = bad, nuts 7-5-4-3-2). Note that "Kansas City" in other contexts (Kansas City lowball) is the draw game; make sure site copy says "stud" version.
- **Is it standard?** Haney: it has "been around awhile in mixed games where the game choices are more exotic, but has recently become more prevalent in all games" (2022). WSOP lists it only as a note on the Razz rule, not as a standalone section; it isn't in the PokerNews Dealer's Choice list. Treat it as a notable variant.
- **Pair/open pair on 4th street:** BARGE and WSOP give no pair-double rule for razz-type games; assume none (as in A-5 Razz).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating the ace as low. The ace is the worst card in this game, and the best door card to HAVE is a deuce.
- Forgetting straights: 3-4-5-6-7 is a straight, and a straight is a strong poker hand, so it is a terrible low (Haney's example above). A pair is a "better" low than a straight or flush.
- Playing "condensed" starting hands like 4-5-6 or 5-6-7: Haney says hands that can make straights are worse than "stretch" hands like 2-3-7, 2-5-7, 2-4-8 (Card Player).
- Playing aces: Haney notes players "play hands with aces more than one would think" because the ace is easy to overlook as a high card; an ace door card is the worst possible (and will be the bring-in).

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (2022): "truly what Seven Card Stud Lowball is supposed to be if making the worst possible high hand is the goal"; he predicted it might "make inroads, possibly even replacing the A-5 version" wherever razz is spread in cash games and tournaments. This is an opinion piece, not a record of rooms.
- Wikipedia's Razz article simply notes that "Deuce-to-seven Razz is also sometimes played" (best hand 2-3-4-5-7).
- Haney's starting-hand frequency table: three-to-7 starting hands 5.50%, three-to-8 4.34%, three-to-9 6.08% (total 15.93%) — roughly the same share of playable starting hands as A-5 razz (three to an 8, 16.2%).
