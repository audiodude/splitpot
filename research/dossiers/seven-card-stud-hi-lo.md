## Deal & structure
- Identical deal to seven-card-stud: ante, 2 down + 1 up, then up/up/up, then one down on 7th; five betting rounds; best five of seven for each half. See seven-card-stud for antes, burn cards, the community-card fallback at 8 players, and raise caps.
- Limit bet sizes: small bet on 3rd and 4th street, big bet on 5th, 6th, 7th (WSOP 246, Robert's).
- **No double bet for an open pair on 4th street.** In Stud 8 a paired board never allows the big bet, even in rooms that allow it in Stud high (WSOP 241; Robert's s.10 rule 6; BARGE).
- **Bring-in:** player showing the lowest door card by rank and suit (suit order low to high: clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades). The **ace counts as a HIGH card for the bring-in**, so the lowest possible bring-in is 2♣ (WSOP 244; Robert's s.10 rule 4; BARGE: "2♣ lowest").
- **Later streets:** the player whose up cards make the best HIGH hand acts first. If the high hands tie, the tied player first clockwise from the dealer acts first (WSOP 244; Robert's) [TDA 2019 RP-10D instead gives it to the high card by suit — see Rooms disagree]. If the high hand showing is all-in, action starts as though that player had checked (WSOP 244).
- Max players: 8 (standard stud); 8 handed usually triggers the 7th-street community card.

## Showdown / what wins
- The pot is cut in two halves: HIGH and LOW. Each player independently chooses the best five cards for high and the best five (the same or different) for low from their seven cards (WSOP 242; Robert's s.10 rule 2). Cards speak; splitting is determined only by the cards, never by player agreement (WSOP 247).
- **Low qualifier (8-or-better):** to win low you need five cards of five DIFFERENT ranks, each an 8 or lower. Ace plays both high and low (WSOP 243: "An ace is the highest card and the lowest card"). Straights and flushes never hurt a low (WSOP 245).
- **Low ranking (ace-to-five):** best low is 5-4-3-2-A (the wheel). Compare from the highest card down: any 7-low beats any 8-low (8-6-4-2-A is "an eight low" and loses to 7-5-4-3-2). Highest possible qualifying low is 8-7-6-5-4.
- **No qualifying low:** the best high hand wins the whole pot.
- **Scoop:** one player can win both halves (e.g., a wheel is both a straight for high and the nut low).
- **Ties / quartering:** players who tie for a half split that half "as evenly as possible" (WSOP 248; a two-way tie for low, with someone else winning high, is a "quarter"). Only the five cards used count when assigning the odd chip (WSOP).
- **Odd chip:** the odd chip in the TOTAL pot goes to the HIGH hand (WSOP 248; Robert's; TDA 20C). Within a tied half: WSOP 248 and TDA 20B give it to the highest card by suit in the five-card playing hand; Robert's s.10 rule 9 gives the low half's odd chip to the LOW card by suit. If two players have identical hands for both high and low, the pot is simply split as evenly as possible.
- Dead hands, short stub, misdeal and 7th-street rules: as in seven-card-stud.

## Rooms disagree
- **Bring-in ties/ace:** PokerNews' Stud 8 page has the lowest card bring it in and, on a rank tie, the tied player closest to the dealer's left — i.e., no suit tie-break and no mention of the ace — while its own seven-card stud page uses suit ranking. WSOP, Robert's, TDA and BARGE use rank then suit, ace high. Upswing's Stud 8 page uses the suit tie-break (clubs lowest) but never says how an ace door card is treated.
- **Tied best-high boards on later streets:** WSOP/Robert's use position (first clockwise from dealer); TDA 2019 RP-10D (tournaments) uses the high card by suit.
- **Odd chip for tied low half:** WSOP/TDA — highest card by suit in the five cards; Robert's — lowest card by suit (and Robert's counts all cards held, not just five).
- **Raise cap:** WSOP bet + four raises (completion doesn't count); Robert's/Pagat/PokerNews bet + three.
- **Qualifier wording:** Upswing's article says low must be "five cards below 8" but its own showdown example treats 8-7-6-5-3 as a qualifying low; PokerNews/WSOP/Robert's define it correctly as 8 or lower. PokerNews' own raise-cap line says three raises.
- **"Three Cards Down" format:** the Resorts World $8-16 mix list (Haney, Card Player, 2023) names "Stud 8/OB (Three Cards Down)". Haney elsewhere says stud lowball games can be played "as Pineapple (three cards down and discard one) or just plain old two down" — so this is probably a 3-hole-card-discard-one version, but I found no article fully spelling out that room's version [INFERENCE; unverified].
- **Max seats for super version:** see super-stud-8.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Playing like Stud high: big pairs are hard to play, because low hands get free rolls and no one can be run out of the low half. [CardPlayer's Haney notes high pairs were "mostly unplayable" in the no-qualifier version; the 8-or-better qualifier exists specifically to make high hands more playable.]
- Thinking a 9 can be in a low: it cannot. Five cards, all ≤ 8, no pairs.
- Thinking the high and low must use the same five cards (they don't, but each is still exactly five cards).
- Forgetting the ace is HIGH when bringing in: an ace door card is the highest, so it never brings in; a 2♣ is the bring-in card.
- Thinking you win half by default if no one else qualifies: a lone qualifier gets half; when NO ONE qualifies, high takes it all.
- Forgetting "quartering": two players with the same best low = each wins a quarter of the pot; the high winner keeps the other half.
- Expecting a big bet when you see an open pair on 4th street (that rule is OFF in Stud 8).

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, 2025): the earliest kitchen-table hi-lo stud was played with NO low qualifier; the eight-or-better qualifier was introduced "to provide balance and make high hands more playable", then "flourished" in HORSE rotations (the "E" in HORSE) and 8-game mixes. Haney rates Stud 8 only 4/10 for action ("grading on a curve").
- Pagat: "In formal games there is often a qualification rule" — this variant is called Stud 8 and is the formal-game standard for hi-lo stud.
- It is commonly named in WSOP cardroom mix rosters (e.g., "$20-$40 MIX ... Stud 8/O" in WSOP rules) and in the WSOP's Dealer's Choice event roster (PokerNews).
