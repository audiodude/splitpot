## Deal & structure
- Hold'em with **three** hole cards. Per WSOP, Pagat, Wikibooks and BeatTheFish you keep all three through the pre-flop **and flop** betting rounds and discard one **after flop betting is finished, before the turn card is exposed**. After the discard it plays as normal two-card Hold'em on turn and river. (Wizard of Odds disagrees on timing; see Rooms disagree.)
- Everything else (blinds/button, burn cards, structure) is as in Hold'em. Structure can be limit, no-limit or pot-limit.
- The discard is dead; players must discard exactly one card. A player who discards too soon or too late is subject to the room's irregularity rules (WSOP gives none specific to Pineapple; general misdeal/premature-card rules apply).

## Showdown / what wins
- **High hand only** in the version WSOP rules define. Best five of two remaining hole cards + five board cards, any combination (play the board allowed). Ties split; odd chip by position from the button.
- The flop betting round is played with all three cards, so the discard is made after seeing the flop. Because everyone holds three cards, hands run stronger than in Hold'em (BeatTheFish: two pair rarely holds up, aim for the nuts or a draw to the nuts).
- **Hi-Lo version (8-or-better)** is commonly spread too (Pagat describes it: same deal and discard timing, but at showdown each player also makes a low hand from the seven cards, using any cards; the usual 8-low qualifier; pot split high/low). WSOP does not list it. It changes what wins, so treat it as its own game if the site covers it; owned by the BoardSplit research list.

## Rooms disagree
- **High vs Hi-Lo.** WSOP rule text = high only. Pagat says Crazy Pineapple is "often played" hi-lo 8-or-better. Confirm per table.
- **When the discard happens relative to flop betting.** WSOP: after the second betting round (flop betting) is complete, before the turn. Pagat, Wikibooks, BeatTheFish agree. Wizard of Odds' rules list the discard **immediately after the flop and before the flop betting round**. Wikipedia only says "after the flop, before the fourth community card". Check which version a room spreads; it changes how much information the flop bettor has when deciding.
- **Lazy Pineapple / Tahoe / Wichita Hold'em** (keep three cards to showdown, use at most two) and **Super Hold'em** (may use all three) are different games under the same family (Pagat, Wikipedia); not the same as Crazy.
- Structure: limit, no-limit and pot-limit are all possible; the anecdotes in the Wizard of Vegas thread describe limit-era Florida cardrooms ($2 bet games) and a small no-limit-style tournament, not a standard structure.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Discarding too early (before the flop, which is plain Pineapple in most sources) or waiting until the turn card is dealt (too late; WSOP says discard before the turn is exposed).
- Throwing away the card that connected best with the flop because it "looks worse" pre-flop; the discard is the key decision (BeatTheFish).
- Overvaluing top pair/two pair; everyone sees the flop with three cards and connects more often.
- Forgetting the discard is irreversible and that others do not see it (so tell-based inference matters).

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia (H.O.R.S.E. article): **C.H.O.R.S.E.** adds Chowaha "or Crazy Pineapple" to H.O.R.S.E. to give an even split of community-card and stud games, so Crazy Pineapple is a documented mixed-game rotation slot (the article does not say how often it is run).
- Wizard of Vegas thread (June 2024, anecdotes): players recall Crazy Pineapple as "the very popular and raucous" high-action game in Florida limit cardrooms in the early/mid 2000s, where all 9-10 players saw the flop for $2 bets; it reportedly "completely disappeared once card rooms went no-limit circa 2009". Another poster saw a Sahara (Las Vegas) $60 Crazy Pineapple tournament, and another saw a 2/5 PLO/Pineapple mixed game at Foxwoods. All anecdotal.
- PokerAtlas has a "No Limit Crazy Pineapple Poker Cash Game - WSOP Poker Room - Las Vegas" listing page (search-result snippet only; the page itself returned 403), suggesting it has been posted at least once at the Las Vegas WSOP room.
- Third-party venue directories claim two dozen US venues list it, but those are low-quality aggregator claims; I did not rely on them.
