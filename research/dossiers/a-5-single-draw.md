## Deal & structure
- Same skeleton as `california-lowball` (five cards, button, blinds with optional ante, open-or-fold pre-draw, one draw, one post-draw betting round) but with **no-limit or pot-limit** betting. WSOP: "Single draw is generally played Pot Limit or No Limit. Limit, Pot Limit and No Limit rules apply."
- All normal no-limit/pot-limit rules apply (unlimited raises, min-raise = size of last raise) - WSOP #289; Robert's NL/PL lowball #1.
- Minimum open: WSOP/Robert's: pre-draw "Players are required to open with a bet or fold"; some structures let the big blind be called, others require the open to be double the big blind. BARGE says (for its California Lowball) the NL form runs "with no Sevens Rule and with check-and-raise allowed."
- Draw: 0-5 cards. Dealer procedure at the WSOP: push-and-pull the draw cards to the player; if the player asks the dealer to pitch them, any exposed card stays with the player (#290). Robert's: up to four consecutive then the fifth after others; WSOP lowball: up to five consecutive (#274).
- Joker: depends on what is posted. WSOP: jokers are not used unless posted (#281); Robert's: joker is assumed in use unless posted otherwise. When used it is the lowest card not already in your hand.
- After the draw: **any hand may be checked without penalty** - the Sevens Rule is *not* used in NL/PL (WSOP #291; Robert's NL/PL #4). Check-raise is allowed (WSOP #282; Robert's NL/PL #5).
- Max six players (WSOP #284).

## Showdown / what wins
- Identical to `california-lowball`: ace-to-five low, aces low, straights/flushes ignored, **best hand 5-4-3-2-A any suits**, lowest wins the whole pot. Pairs are bad; any no-pair hand beats any pair; compare highest card down; A-A beats 2-2 for pair hands; joker = lowest card not present.
- Ties split. Cards speak but you may not overclaim; spreading a hand with a pair requires announcing "pair" or you risk losing the pot if it causes another player to foul (WSOP #279-280).
- Five cards exactly after the draw or the hand is fouled (WSOP #275).
- Example: P1 holds 7-5-4-2-A (a "seven"), P2 holds 8-6-3-2-A (an "eight") and P3 holds 6-5-4-3-2 (a "six"; it would be a straight in high poker but that is irrelevant here). Six-high beats seven-high beats eight-high, so P3 wins. If P3 were not there, P1 beats P2 by the top card (7 < 8).

## Rooms disagree
- **Exposed cards before the draw (NL/PL):** Robert's: a player must take an exposed A, 2, 3, 4 or 5 (because a player isn't entitled to know an opponent lacks the best hand) and replace any other; WSOP #290: every exposed card is replaced, procedure being push-and-pull.
- **Joker default:** Robert's - in use unless posted otherwise; WSOP - not in use unless posted. BARGE just says the NL game "is also sometimes played" without describing the joker; PokerNews' bracelet table marks the 1983 and 1986 NL A-5 events "w/ joker" and does not mark the 1982 one.
- **Draw size:** four consecutive (Robert's) vs five consecutive (WSOP).
- **Min open / blind:** Robert's/WSOP: both structures exist (big blind callable or double-BB open).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Using 2-7 instincts: A-2-3-4-5 is the nuts here, straights and flushes are fine, and an ace is your best card.
- Expecting the limit-lowball Sevens Rule in a no-limit game: it does not apply.
- Forgetting the open-or-fold rule pre-draw.
- Believing exposed cards are always replaced: check the house rule (Robert's vs WSOP).
- Drawing two or more as a routine: Pagat's lowball intro says that drawing two makes a pair about half the time and that "your chance of getting a 9-high or better is never more than 25%". (General lowball advice, not A-5-specific.)

## Lore & reputation
- Thin modern evidence. It is **not** on the PokerNews/PokerListings 20-game WSOP Dealer's Choice menu - that menu has only the 2-7 single draw (see `2-7-single-draw`). The WSOP Live-Action rule book still has a full set of no-limit/pot-limit lowball rules covering ace-to-five (#289-291).
- BARGE ran "the no-limit format" of California lowball as a **bounty tournament at BARGE 2021**.
- WSOP bracelet history (PokerNews): $2,500 No-Limit Ace to Five Draw 1982 (Billy Baxter, 39 entries); 1983 with joker (David Angel, 37); 1986 $5,000 with joker and rebuys (Mike Cox, 32). These sit alongside the limit A-5 draw bracelets that ran 1971-2004.
- Wikipedia's Draw poker article says Kansas City (2-7) lowball is "often played no-limit"; its California lowball entry mentions no-limit/pot-limit only to note that the check-raise restriction is never used there. Big-bet A-5 is a niche that survives mostly in historic WSOP results, WSOP Live-Action rules and the occasional special event.
