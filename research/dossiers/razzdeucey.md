## Deal & structure
- Dealt and bet like razz: ante; 2 down + 1 up; three more up cards and a final down card; five betting rounds; small bet 3rd and 4th street, big bet 5th, 6th, 7th (WSOP intro).
- **Aces are HIGH.** The bring-in is the highest door card, so **A♠ is the automatic bring-in** (Haney 2026). Card Player Lifestyle says "the player showing the highest card must post the bring-in" and "Aces always play high."
- Later streets: lowest board (by 2-7 rules; no straights/flushes can show on four cards) acts first (extension of razz structure).
- Check-raise allowed, stud misdeal rules, up to 8 players (WSOP 334-336).
- "Super" form: 4 hole cards, 1 up, betting round, discard 2 before 4th street (Haney: both Razzdeucey and Razzdugi are "usually played as 'super'"). Other forms mentioned: "Pineapple" (3 down, discard 1) or plain 2-down (Haney). The Resorts World $8-16 mix lists "Razzdeucey (Three Cards Down)" [meaning not spelled out — likely the 3-hole-cards-discard-one form; unverified].

## Showdown / what wins
- Two halves (no qualifier on either), per Haney and Card Player Lifestyle:
  - **2-7 half:** best five-card deuce-to-seven low from the seven cards: aces high, straights and flushes count against you, pairs bad; nuts 7-5-4-3-2 (not suited). WSOP 332 states straights and flushes count against you on the 2-7 portion.
  - **Badugi half:** best four-card Badugi, aces HIGH — nut badugi is 2-3-4-5 in four suits (Haney). Four distinct ranks and four distinct suits; fewer cards (three-card, two-card) win if nobody has a full badugi.
- A scoop is possible but, with ace high, needs e.g. 2-3-4-5 rainbow plus a 7 (WSOP 332's example is garbled; see below).
- **Odd chip:** to the five-card ("razz-side") hand — WSOP rule 153 lists "Razzdugi/Duci, odd chip will go to the razz hand"; BARGE's generic rule treats the five-card hand as the high side in all 5-card+4-card split games.

## Rooms disagree
- **WSOP's own text contradicts itself.** The paragraph says the pot is split between the best Badugi hand and the best Deuce-to-Seven hand (matches Haney and Card Player Lifestyle). But rule 333 says "the pot will be split between the best Deuce to Seven hand and the best Razz hand," and rule 332 says "the best hand would be A, 2, 3, 4, 5 of different suits with a 7 of any suit," which only makes sense if it were copied from Razzdugi and half-edited. Treat the paragraph + Haney + Card Player Lifestyle (Badugi + 2-7) as authoritative and flag the rule-sheet wording to Main.
- **Name:** WSOP "Razzduci (Razzdeucey)"; Card Player Lifestyle and Haney "Razzdeucey"; 2+2 thread (search snippet only) "Razzdeucy".
- **Is the badugi half ace-high?** Haney/Card Player Lifestyle: yes ("aces always play high"; nuts 2-3-4-5). WSOP's Baduci (draw) paragraph does exclude the ace from the badugi side; WSOP's Razzduci text doesn't say so explicitly.
- **Bring-in:** Haney: A♠. Card Player Lifestyle: "highest card" (same thing with ace high). BARGE has no Razzdeucey page.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Hearing "Razz" in the name and playing A-5 low. Haney: "New players understandably get confused at the name Razz-deucey because Razz makes them think they want to make an ace-to-five low, and there's also no mention of a badugi." It's Badeucey (2-7 + Badugi) dealt stud-style.
- Mis-announcing the game: Haney describes nearly playing a full round of Razzdeucey while everyone else played Razzdacey/Razzdugi (easy to misread the plaque).
- Treating an ace door card as a good card: it's the worst card and the automatic bring-in.
- Forgetting straights/flushes count against your 2-7 hand.
- Haney's advice: in the 'super' version start with three rainbow cards; three-to-an-eight is good, nines are playable if the worst card is hidden.

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, Jan 2026): "the most favored stud lowball variants" in low/medium-stakes mixed cash games are Razz-deucey and Razz-dacey; he suggests Razz-deucey might better be called "Super Stud Badeucey," and that Razz-dacey was once called "Super Razzdugi."
- Listed in Coach's Resorts World $8-16 mix (Las Vegas) as "Razzdeucey (Three Cards Down)" and in Card Player Lifestyle's Mixed Game Festival rules; WSOP lists "Razzduci" in must-move mix examples.
