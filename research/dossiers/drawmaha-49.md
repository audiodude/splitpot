## Deal & structure
Same as `drawmaha`: 5 hole cards, blinds, betting, 3-card flop, betting, ONE draw after flop betting, turn + betting, river + betting. Limit: small bet first two rounds, big bet last two (BARGE). WSOP: draw 1-3 or stand pat; BARGE: draw 0-3.

## Showdown / what wins
Pot splits in half; no qualifier, so it always splits two ways (Poker Chip Forum thread).
1. **Omaha-high half:** exactly 2 hole + exactly 3 board cards.
2. **49 (points) half:** use all five hole cards; add up their point values; the HIGHEST total wins. Point values (WSOP, BARGE, Haney, Cardplayer Lifestyle all agree):
   - Ace = 1.
   - Two through ten = their face value (a ten is worth 10).
   - Jack, Queen, King = 0.
   - Maximum is 10-10-10-10-9 = 49 (hence the name).
   - No poker hand rankings matter on this half: only the sum. Suits don't matter.
   - Ties on the points split that half (Haney's blocker discussion treats equal totals as ties; no card-count tiebreak is documented for 49 itself).
3. One player can scoop. Odd chip: by position clockwise from the button (WSOP).

## Rooms disagree
- **Face-card value.** BARGE notes that "some online sources" say face cards are worth 1 point, but the BARGE standard (and WSOP, Haney, Cardplayer Lifestyle) is 0. Confirm before starting.
- **Draw cap.** WSOP 1-3 / stand pat; BARGE 0-3; the popular home variant "Draw2maha 49" caps the draw at 2 (Poker Chip Forum, MrCatPants).
- **Limit vs pot-limit.** WSOP: any. Poker Chip Forum posters note the pot-limit version is the most common in their games, but one poster says it "plays much better fixed limit" because big-bet play leads to "nut peddling". BARGE writes it up as a limit game.
- **Also spelled** Dramaha 49 (BARGE, Card Player) vs Drawmaha 49 (WSOP, Coach's mix list).
- Turn/draw order: as in parent `drawmaha`.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Valuing high cards: kings, queens and jacks are worth ZERO. The cards you want are tens and nines (and eights, sevens) — the opposite of normal poker.
- Thinking A is worth 11 or 14: it is 1.
- Playing hands because they are strong for Omaha: Poker Chip Forum's analysis says the cards that make a good 49 hand (T/9) are not the cards that make the strongest Omaha hands (Aces & faces), so scoops are harder than in Drawmaha Zero (a poster's view).
- Not keeping score: Haney says the average card is worth about 4.2 points over a 52-card deck (220 points total), so ~21 points is a typical dealt total and 33 is "way above average". A forum poster says winning totals are commonly in the mid-30s or better.
- Assuming 49 is the unbeatable nuts: Haney points out lesser totals can be unbeatable because they block the cards needed to beat them (his example: holding 10-10-10-9-8 = 47 means no opponent can exceed 45).
- Drawing more than two cards: forum guidance is to treat a 3-card draw as a likely fold.

## Lore & reputation
- Spread in real rooms: it appears on the WSOP's Drawmaha rule text, on BARGE's rulebook, and in Coach's $8-$16 mix at Resorts World (Card Player, Jan 2023).
- Haney (Card Player, May 2025) rates it only 5/10: people don't like counting points, and there aren't enough middling cards to go around (only four tens and four nines), which "curbs the action".
- Poker Chip Forum is polarised: some call it "one of the best games ever" or prefer it to Zero; others find big-bet versions nit-ish. Treat as opinion, not fact.
