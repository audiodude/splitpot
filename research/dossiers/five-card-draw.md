## Deal & structure
- Each player gets **five cards face down**, one betting round, **one draw**, a second betting round, showdown.
- **Posting:** the casino/online norm is a button with small and big blind (PokerNews: "most common on online poker sites and in casinos"); older and home games use an ante with no blinds (Pagat; Robert's Rules: "a button and an ante"). WSOP Dealer's Choice/tournaments use blinds plus antes in NL play (as for NL 2-7; see `2-7-single-draw`).
- **Betting structure:** in rooms it is spread mostly **no-limit or pot-limit**: WSOP Dealer's Choice lists "No-Limit 5-Card Draw High" (PokerNews, PokerListings); PokerListings says the first DC event in 2014 included "Pot-Limit Five Card Draw"; Card Player Lifestyle: "best and most commonly played as either a pot limit or no limit game with one draw". Fixed limit exists: in limit the bet doubles after the draw (Robert's, Pagat).
- **Draw:** starting left of the button, each live player says how many cards to discard (usually up to three), discards face down and gets replacements; keeping all five is "standing pat". After the draw there is a second betting round. Post-draw action:
  - Blind games: first live player left of the button (Wikipedia: "betting always begins with the player to the dealer's left"; casino-style).
  - Ante games (Robert's Rules): action starts with the opener, or the next player clockwise if the opener folded.
- **Open options (pre-draw):** "Guts" (open on anything) or **Jacks or Better** (see below). Robert's Rules: "Some draw high games allow a player to open on anything; others require the opener to have a pair of jacks or better."
- If you draw lots of cards, the deck can run out; the standard fix is to shuffle the muck/discards of players already drawn (Pagat). Rooms keep the table to six or fewer (Pagat: six is the sensible max, 2-8 possible).

## Showdown / what wins
- Standard high-poker ranking: royal/straight flush, quads, full house, flush, straight, trips, two pair, pair, high card. Best five-card hand wins the whole pot (PokerNews, Pagat). No qualifier.
- Cards speak; ties split the pot.
- **Jacks or Better (Jackpots) additions** (Pagat, Wikipedia, Robert's): in the first betting round a player may open only with a pair of jacks or better. The opener does not need to hold actual jacks - any hand that beats a pair of tens (e.g. 5-5-3-3-6) will do. If the opener discards cards that made the opening hand ("splitting openers") the discards are kept separate and shown at the end to prove the opener qualified; if the opener cannot prove it the hand is dead. If nobody opens, everyone re-antes and redeals (progressive versions raise the requirement to queens, kings, aces). Pagat also describes "Trips to Win" (need three of a kind to win; if nobody has it the pot carries over).
- **Joker ("bug")**: Robert's Rules (Draw High, "The Joker"): the joker may be used only as an ace or to complete a straight, flush or straight flush; in a flush it is the highest card of that suit not already in the hand; five aces (four aces plus joker) is the best possible hand. Pagat (bug): same; "9-9-7-7-joker is two pair with an ace kicker", and a flush with the bug uses the highest missing card (A-K-J-4-2 hearts beats A-10-9-8-joker spades because the bug acts as the king).
- Example: H1 = K♥ K♦ 8♠ 5♣ 2♥ (pair of kings) vs H2 = A♠ J♠ 9♠ 6♠ 3♠ (ace-high flush): flush wins; if both are on the same level (e.g. both pair of kings), kickers decide.

## Rooms disagree
- **How many cards may be drawn:** Pagat: normally up to three (some allow four if the fifth card is an ace, some allow four or five); PokerNews: "most variations" up to three; Robert's Rules: up to four consecutive cards (a five-card draw is dealt as four now and the fifth after everyone else has drawn; the last player burns a card first); WSOP Live-Action *lowball* text: a player may draw up to five consecutive cards. WSOP Live-Action has no separate Draw-High section I could find; the Drawmaha text says "all rules of single draw rules apply to the draw portion".
- **Exposed cards before the draw:** Robert's Rules (Draw High): any card exposed by the dealer before the draw must be kept; (lowball sections of Robert's and WSOP: exposed cards are replaced).
- **Check-raise:** Robert's Rules: permitted before and after the draw in draw high. Some limit lowball rules ban check-raise (not draw high).
- **Bug/joker:** Robert's: players are told whether the joker is in use; BARGE: draw "is played with a joker/bug... sometimes played without". WSOP lowball: jokers are not used unless posted. Pagat: bug is optional ("some players add a joker").
- **Opening requirement:** BARGE: no requirement ("Guts to Open") vs Jacks or Better; Pagat calls Jacks or Better "probably the most popular version" of five-card draw and the no-restriction game "California Draw or Guts"; Wikipedia says the Gardena jackpots version was the primary form in California 1930s-70s and "Guts to open" became the retronym for plain draw.
- **Single vs multiple draws:** Pagat: some play two draws (up to three cards, then one or two); Card Player Lifestyle: "can be played as a limit triple draw game, but is best and most commonly played as either pot limit or no limit with one draw".
- **Variants that change who wins (not given their own dossier here):** (1) *California high/low split* (Wikipedia only): five-card draw with a bug, best high and best A-5 low split the pot, low must be 8-or-better, and if nobody qualifies low the high takes it all; cards speak; wheels can "scoop". I found no room or WSOP source spreading it. [INFERENCE: `archie` is the room-spread relative - it adds a high qualifier and three draws - but no source says it descends from this game.] (2) *High-low with declare* (Wikipedia: simultaneous chip declaration, "common in home games... rarely found in casinos today") - excluded as a home game. (3) *Jacks Back / Johnson*, deuces wild, wild widow, Diablo, trips to win (Pagat) - home variants. (4) *Seven-card draw*, *Spit in the Ocean*, *Anaconda*, *Shotgun* (Wikipedia "home games") - excluded.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating five-card draw like hold'em preflop: PokerNews: playing far too many hands, especially draws to a straight or flush. Four-to-a-flush/straight is only about a 4-to-1 underdog with one card to come.
- Always drawing three to a pair (statistically right per PokerNews) so opponents read you; PokerNews says mix it up sometimes.
- Forgetting jacks-or-better opening rules when that variant is posted: the opener may be forced to prove the opening hand.
- Assuming nobody can have a strong hand after one draw. Card Player Lifestyle: players "will be quite apparent to observant players" from the number drawn: one card = two pair or four to a straight/flush; two = trips; three = pair; four/five usually only from the big blind.
- Playing it with a bug without knowing the bug is not wild (it's an ace or straight/flush filler only).

## Lore & reputation
- "Often the first poker variant learned" (Wikipedia) but now "rare in casino and tournament play" (Wikipedia); Card Player Lifestyle: "Nowadays, the game is virtually impossible to find save for mixed cash games, WSOP Dealers Choice or Big Bet Mix events, and a spattering of online offerings."
- WSOP history: four limit "Draw High" bracelet events between 1978 and 1982 (Lakewood Louie 1978 and 1979, Pat Callihan 1980, David Sklansky 1982) (PokerNews). Not offered as a standalone bracelet event since; survives inside Dealer's Choice.
- Gardena, California: jacks-to-open draw was the main game there from the 1930s to 1970s, always secondary to lowball (Wikipedia); the term "jackpot" comes from the huge pots this created when openers passed out (Wikipedia).
- Guts-to-open: R.F. Foster (1897) and J. Scarne (1949) quotes about jackpots are reproduced on Pagat, with opposite opinions.
