## Deal & structure
Identical to `drawmaha` (high) with one extra rule:
- Blinds; 5 hole cards each; betting; 3-card flop; betting.
- **One draw after the flop betting**; then turn + betting, river + betting, showdown.
- Replacements for two or more discarded cards are dealt face down (PokerNews).
- **One-card-open rule:** if you exchange exactly ONE card, the replacement is dealt FACE UP first. You may keep it, or reject it; if you reject it, it is discarded and you receive the next card face down, which you must keep (forced second card is stated by PokerNews; PokerListings and Cardplayer Lifestyle describe the keep-or-reject choice without stating that the second card is forced).
- Generally pot-limit; fixed-limit also played (PokerNews).

## Showdown / what wins
Two halves, no qualifier, same as Drawmaha High:
1. Best five-card draw hand (all 5 hole cards, ordinary high ranking) takes 50%.
2. Best Omaha hand (exactly 2 hole + exactly 3 board) takes 50%.
3. One player holding both scoops. Ties split the half; odd chip per house/WSOP convention [not stated by PokerNews].

## Rooms disagree
- **Origin story.** PokerNews: devised by Anders "Bengan" Bengtsson in 2004 in a short-handed mixed game in Sweden. SwC Poker (as read by the Drawmaha dossier author): invented at a Stockholm club in summer 2006. Dates conflict; treat as unsettled.
- **Naming.** Some sources treat Sviten as the original name of Drawmaha (SwC), others as a Drawmaha variant (Cardplayer Lifestyle). In the Baltics/Nordics "Sviten" is what the game is called; WSOP Live-Action does not mention the one-card-open rule.
- Draw cap and turn/draw order: as in `drawmaha`.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming the face-up card is optional both ways: once you reject it, the replacement is forced.
- Forgetting the open card is shown to the table, so a one-card draw is more transparent (and an opponent sees whether you rejected).
- Applying the one-card-open rule to multi-card draws: it applies only when exactly one card is drawn.

## Lore & reputation
- PokerNews says it spread across Sweden, Finland and Scandinavia, then Europe, with a strong Baltic following; OlyBet festivals in Tallinn feature it regularly.
- In 2025 the €555 Sviten Special at WSOP Circuit Tallinn was reported as the first standalone Sviten tournament to award a WSOP Circuit gold ring (PokerNews). 2026 OlyBet Showdown Tallinn ran a six-handed PL Sviten tournament; Vilnius ran a €200 PL Sviten; The Festival Weekend 2026 editions in Vilnius and Tallinn included Sviten events.
- Not part of US Dealer's Choice lists; this is a European festival game.
