## Deal & structure
- Identical procedure to 2-7 Triple Draw; only the hand ranking changes.
- Small blind + big blind; 5 cards each face down.
- Round 1 betting starts left of the big blind. Then draw 1 (0–5 cards, starting with first active player left of the button), round 2 betting, draw 2, round 3, draw 3, round 4, showdown. Post-draw betting rounds start with first active player left of the button.
- Limit: small bet for the first two rounds, big bet (double) for the last two (after the 2nd and 3rd draws) (Pagat triple draw; PokerNews/Cardplayer Lifestyle).
- Reshuffle procedure, burn-every-round and lock-in-your-draw-count procedures as in 2-7 TD (WSOP 2025).
- WSOP: max 6 players.

## Showdown / what wins
- Lowest five-card hand wins the whole pot, ace-to-five ranking:
  - Aces are LOW (best card).
  - Straights and flushes are IGNORED — they do not hurt a low.
  - Pairs are bad: any no-pair hand beats any paired hand; among paired hands, lower pair is better (A-A beats 2-2).
- Best hand: 5-4-3-2-A ("wheel" or "bicycle"), any suits, even all one suit. Next: 6-4-3-2-A, 6-5-3-2-A, 6-5-4-2-A, 6-5-4-3-A, 6-5-4-3-2, 7-4-3-2-A...
- Compare highest card first, then next (7-5-4-3-2 beats 7-6-3-2-A).
- No qualifier; any hand can win.
- Ties split; tournament odd chip to first seat left of button (WSOP rule 73).

## Rooms disagree
- Check-raise: WSOP Live-Action (#282) — check-raise permitted in ace-to-five. Robert's Rules (limit ace-to-five) — check-raise NOT permitted unless players are told otherwise. (Robert's lowball rules were written mainly with single-draw California lowball in mind; how rooms apply this to triple draw is not stated.)
- Sevens rule: Robert's (limit A-5) assumes the "sevens rule" (a checked seven-or-better forfeits post-draw action). WSOP Live-Action does not list a sevens rule for A-5. [Not documented specifically for triple draw in any source I opened.]
- Joker: Robert's says a joker is assumed in use for A-5 lowball unless posted otherwise; WSOP Live-Action says jokers are NOT used unless specifically posted. Mixed-game A-5 TD (WSOP Dealer's Choice) is a 52-card game.
- Exposed card on the deal: Robert's (limit A-5) — exposed 7-or-lower must be kept, higher replaced; WSOP — all exposed cards replaced.
- Pagat describes A-5 TD as "also sometimes played", with 2-7 TD the more popular form.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Carrying 2-7 habits: in A-5 an ace is your best card, and a 6-5-4-3-2 "straight" is a great low.
- Thinking a flush ruins your low — it doesn't.
- Confusing A-5 TD with Razz-style hand reading: same ranking (A-5), but here you have exactly five cards and must use all five.
- Mixing up the doubling point of the limit (after the 2nd draw).

## Lore & reputation
- Limit ace-to-five draw (single draw) was a WSOP fixture from 1971 (Johnny Moss) to 2004 (Norm Ketchum) per PokerNews' "History in Spades". The same table lists "$1,500 Limit Triple Draw" events in 2002 (John Juanda) and 2003 (Men Nguyen) inside its ace-to-five section [ranking of those events not explicitly stated].
- PokerNews (2014): ace-to-five returned to the WSOP in 2014 via the Dealer's Choice event after a nine-year absence. It remains one of WSOP Dealer's Choice's triple draw games (PokerNews DC guide).
- Roots in California lowball (see a-5-single-draw).
