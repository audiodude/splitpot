## Deal & structure
- Four hole cards each, blinds, preflop betting (CPL: limit Omaha Hi-Lo, up to eight players; example $4/$8 with $2/$4 blinds).
- Flop: dealer burns, then deals **three separate flops** (three rows of three cards = nine cards). Betting (small-bet street in limit).
- Turn: burn, then **two turn cards**, each placed in its own spot (in the CPL/PokerNews photos the three flops are in rows and the two turns sit alongside; the touching rule below says the middle row is adjacent to both turns). Big-bet street in limit.
- River: **one** river card. Big-bet street. Then showdown. Total board cards: 9 + 2 + 1 = 12.
- Burn procedure is given by CPL as "the dealer burns, and puts out three flops" and "burns and puts out two turns" (one burn per street). A river burn is not stated explicitly. [INFERENCE: one burn before the river, as in normal Omaha.]

## Showdown / what wins
- Hi-lo split: half the pot to the best Omaha high hand, half to the best qualifying low (five cards, eight-or-better) — both built as exactly two hole cards plus exactly three board cards. If a player has both, they scoop; if no low qualifies, high wins everything (CPL).
- CPL rule list: players "must utilize any flop + turn + river combo of three cards along with two of their hole cards". Reading: a player picks **one** of the three flops and **one** of the two turns, adds the single river, which yields a normal five-card Omaha board (3 x 2 = 6 possible five-card boards); the hand is exactly two hole cards plus exactly three cards of that board. CPL's article says the same ("any combination of a flop, turn and river") and its showdown example plays the nut low off "the bottom flop/turn".
- PokerNews on the PokerStars version agrees: players choose which flop to use, choose one of the two turns, and share the one river. (Sources agree; the wording "three cards" in the CPL list is read as the Omaha three-board-card requirement, not three cards from one street.)
- Because the choice is free, the high hand and the low hand may use different flops/turns and different hole-card pairs.
- **"Touching" rule (optional house variant per CPL):** you may only combine boards that are touching, i.e. middle flop can pair with either turn, but the top flop cannot use the bottom turn and vice versa. This is described as an "added challenge", not as the default.
- Ties, odd chip, ace treatment: as Omaha Hi-Lo / WSOP: ace plays low for low; straights and flushes don't count against low; worst qualifier 8-7-6-5-4; high hand gets the odd chip in a hi/lo split (WSOP general rule 153, if played under WSOP procedures — WSOP's rule sheet does not list 3-2-1).
- Same player may be both high and low winner (scoop), per CPL.

## Rooms disagree
- **Pot structure:** PokerStars/EPT Prague 2016 played **PLO 3-2-1 (high only, pot-limit)** per PokerNews; CPL plays an **Omaha 8 (hi-lo), limit** version at its Mixed Game Festival. Same board structure, different winners/betting.
- **Board combination:** default "any flop + any turn + the river" (CPL list, PokerNews) vs optional "touching boards only" house rule (CPL home-game article).
- CPL's two pages describe the rule slightly differently: the festival rule list says "any flop + turn + river combo"; the home-game article repeats it and adds the touching rule.
- **Touching rule is standard in BARGE's Chowaha write-up.** BARGE Rulebook ("Four Card Chowaha High/Low") calls this exact game (4 hole cards, three flops stacked in a 3x3 square, two turns to the right, one river) by its original name Chowaha, created by Mike Chow, name dating to the 1990s, "also called 3-2-1 Omaha". Its rule: the flop, turn and river you use must be contiguous (top flop row cannot be combined with the bottom turn card or vice versa; the middle flop works with either turn), giving four playable boards (confirmed by Bill Rini's 2005 diagram write-up: "you can play four different boards"). Cardplayer Lifestyle's festival rule list gives the free any-flop/any-turn version and calls the touching rule an optional "added challenge". Ask which version is dealt.
- **Two-card Chowaha (variant, not its own dossier).** BARGE's plain "Chowaha" deals two hole cards (Hold'em-style) onto the same 3-flop/2-turn/1-river board with the same contiguity rule; the high hand and the low hand can use different hole cards/boards, but each hand must use exactly two hole cards plus three board cards (BARGE text). BARGE says it "became two-card when played at BARGE" to deal fewer cards; "Casino Chowaha" is the high-only version (Bill Rini). Bill Rini (2005) wrote that Chowaha was a BARGE/RGP favorite but "you likely won't find Chowaha spread at any casino".

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating the nine flop cards as one big board; you still choose just one flop, one turn, and the lone river for a given five-card board.
- Never folding the flop because there are "nine cards" — CPL jokes about this and warns that you often play for half a pot at best.
- Forgetting a low still needs five distinct cards ≤ 8 from your chosen five-card board plus two hole cards.
- Playing with the touching rule when the table does not.

## Lore & reputation
- CPL author Alec Rome calls it a Mixed Game Festival ("Westgate chips" and a CPL tweet of 5 Oct 2021: "Where else can you play 3-2-1?") staple; CPL's rules page lists it among Mixed Game Festival flop games (the festival has run at Resorts World, Bellagio, Westgate).
- PokerNews (6 Jan 2017) reports PokerStars introduced PLO 3-2-1 at the final EPT Prague stop; winner Matias Knaapinen, 3rd Benny Glaser. So it is a real tournament format, if niche.
- BARGE Rulebook carries both the four-card Chowaha and two-card Chowaha chapters, and BARGE events spread the two-card version (BARGE text). Chowaha also featured in a Poker: All the Games podcast episode (Feb 2024; audio only).
- No evidence found of it in WSOP live-action rules (grep of 2026 text: no "3-2-1" match), Bellagio or Commerce Dealer's Choice lists; evidence of play is festival/Stars events only.
