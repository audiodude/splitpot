## Deal & structure
- Button and blinds. Each player gets six hole cards face down.
- BARGE (limit structure): pre-flop betting at the small bet. Then each player still in splits the six cards into a two-card hand (Hold'em) and a four-card hand (Omaha High). Flop (three cards), betting at the small bet; turn, betting at the big bet; river, betting at the big bet; showdown.
- Cardplayer Lifestyle: split happens before the flop; the split is locked, the two hands must stay visibly separated until showdown, and cards cannot be swapped between them.
- Kevin Haney (Card Player, 2025): split is made during the first betting round by anyone contesting the pot; it must be clear and unchanged all hand; chips or card protectors can mark the two hands. He also notes that cheating by altering the split is feasible in a casual game.
- One shared five-card board is used by both hands (a single flop/turn/river, not separate boards).

## Showdown / what wins
- Pot splits in two halves, both for high: the best Hold'em hand and the best Omaha hand.
- Hold'em half (BARGE): best five-card hand using zero, one or both of the player's two Hold'em cards with the board.
- Omaha half: exactly two cards from the player's four-card hand plus exactly three board cards.
- A single player can win both halves and scoop; each half is its own comparison across all players still in. [INFERENCE]
- Ties within a half: split that half; odd-chip handling is not stated in sources. [INFERENCE: use room rule.]
- Not stated: whether a player may use one card of their Hold'em pair in the Omaha hand; the sources say the two groups are separate, so no.

## Rooms disagree
- Timing of the split: BARGE puts it after pre-flop betting; Cardplayer Lifestyle says before the flop; Haney says it happens during the first betting round. Different procedures for when cards are separated.
- BARGE describes a limit game (small bet through the flop, big bet from the turn); the other sources do not state a limit. 2+2 index text for Jim Donahue's SOHE book (seen only in search snippet, page returned 403) says pot-limit.
- No WSOP or casino rule sheet found: the WSOP 2026 live-action rules contain no SOHE entry (grep found none).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating the Hold'em hand as part of the Omaha hand: they are disjoint.
- Keeping aces intact automatically; Haney says broken pairs often play better in a six-card split for large fields.
- Mis-splitting: pairs in the Omaha part and a weak Hold'em two-card hand are a common trap; Haney calls marginal two-way hands the biggest trap.
- Moving cards after the flop is revealed: the split is locked.
- Slow play: Haney warns the game runs slow.

## Lore & reputation
Haney rates it 6/10, line-up dependent, and writes that it is "another flop game" to balance draw-heavy mixes; he describes it as home-game fare ("Spice Up Your Home Game"). BARGE's rulebook includes it, and Jim Donahue published a book "SOHE: Simultaneous Omaha and Hold'em" (Aug 2017, from search listings; not opened). Cardplayer Lifestyle lists it as "Hold'em-Omaha (a.k.a. SOHE)" among flop games on its Mixed Game Festival rules page. No room, WSOP or festival event specifically running SOHE was found, so the live-room evidence is the festival rule list and BARGE only.
