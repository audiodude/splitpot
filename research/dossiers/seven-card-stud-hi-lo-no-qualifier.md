## Deal & structure
- Deal and betting are seven-card-stud (ante, 2 down + 1 up, up/up/up, final down; five betting rounds; small bet 3rd/4th street, big bet 5th-7th; same raise caps and dead-hand rules).
- **Razz-style order of action** (this is the key structural change vs Stud 8): the HIGH card brings it in on 3rd street, K♠ the automatic bring-in, ace low (BARGE: "as in Razz"); and the LOW hand showing initiates the action on each later street (WSOP 249; BARGE "Action starts with the lowest hand showing (as in Razz)").
- **No double bet for an open pair on 4th street:** BARGE: "even if there is an open pair on fourth street, all bets on this street will always be at the small bet limit"; WSOP 249 imports Stud 8 rules, which also have no pair double bet (WSOP 241).
- Players: up to 8, community card on 7th if cards run out.

## Showdown / what wins
- Pot is cut in two equal halves: best five-card HIGH hand and best five-card A-5 LOW hand (ace low; straights and flushes don't hurt; pairs DO hurt). **There is no 8-or-better qualifier**: someone always wins the low half, even with a 10-low, a K-low, or a pair (Wikipedia, "Stud poker"; PokerNews dealer's choice roster lists it as "Seven Card Stud Hi-Lo (Regular)").
- Use any five cards for high and any five (same or different) for low. One player can win both halves (scoop). Ties split the half between tied players.
- **Odd chip:** the high hand gets the odd chip in the total pot (WSOP 248-249, 153); within tied halves it follows the stud rules (highest card by suit among the five-card hand per WSOP/TDA; Robert's gives the tied low half's chip to the low card by suit).
- Splits are determined only by the cards, never by agreement (WSOP 247).

## Rooms disagree
- **Name:** WSOP and PokerNews "Stud Hi-Lo Regular"; BARGE and the Resorts World mix "Stud High/Low No Qualifier"; Haney "Stud NQ"; Wikipedia "Q".
- **Who acts first on later streets:** WSOP 249 and BARGE both say the LOW hand showing acts first (Razz-style). In Stud 8 (and in most online descriptions of generic stud hi-lo) the HIGH hand acts first — be sure to state which the site uses. I found no source giving a different rule for no-qualifier.
- **How common it is:** Wikipedia: "much less common than stud eight, and is generally played at higher limits." Haney: the original kitchen-table version of hi-lo stud, now in rotation only in some mixes. WSOP's Dealer's Choice event roster includes it ("Seven Card Stud Hi-Lo (Regular)"), and the Resorts World $8-16 mix lists "Stud High-Low No Qualifier (Three Cards Down)".
- **Odd chip for tied low half:** WSOP/TDA — highest card by suit in the five; Robert's — low card by suit.
- "Three Cards Down" variant (Resorts World): see seven-card-stud-hi-lo.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming you need an 8-or-better low. A pair or a ten-high low wins half if everyone else is worse; Haney: "a ten low, a small pair, or occasionally an even worse low holding could drag half the pot."
- Playing high-card hands like Stud 8: Haney says high pairs were "mostly unplayable" in NQ (a low card showing can make others fold and half the pot goes to the best low no matter what); even rolled-up trips can be negative unless multiway or you show a low card.
- Expecting the high hand to act first like Stud 8: it's the LOW board that acts first here.
- Forgetting the high bring-in: with the highest door card you're forced in, as in razz.
- Thinking the whole pot goes to the high hand when nobody has a low: the low half still awards to the best low hand.

## Lore & reputation
- Haney (Card Player, 2025): "One of the original kitchen table homes games was Seven Card Stud High-Low Regular (Stud NQ)," in which the low half could be dragged with a ten-low or a small pair. The eight-or-better qualifier was introduced "to provide balance and make high hands more playable." Haney grades Stud NQ 3/10 for action (vs 4/10 for Stud 8) — his opinion.
- Wikipedia notes it is generally played at higher limits. WSOP's rules give it its own page (rule 249) and its Dealer's Choice roster includes it.
