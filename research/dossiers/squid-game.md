## Deal & structure
Decision: Squid Game and WSOP "Derailment" are the same core mechanic under two names (three Omaha boards; after the river, the board with the lowest river card is removed; the pot is split between the best hands on the two surviving boards). They differ only in packaging: Squid Game is described with ordinary blinds and a final betting round after the elimination; Derailment is defined by WSOP as a variant of the double-board bomb pot (ante, no pre-flop action). One dossier, Derailment as a variant.

Squid Game (Cardplayer Lifestyle, Upswing): plays like Omaha High, but the dealer puts out three flops, then three turns, then three rivers, with normal betting rounds between streets. After the rivers are dealt, the board holding the lowest river card is taken out of play, then one more betting round occurs before showdown. A hole-card count other than Omaha's four is not stated in those sources; Upswing says "Omaha Hi" and the Lodge list says "Omaha Hi with three boards". The Lodge list calls it "Limit Squid Game", so it was spread there as a limit game.

Derailment (WSOP 2026 live-action rules, 8 max): "all the rules of double board bomb pot apply" with changes, namely three boards until the river, and the lowest card by suit on the river removes that whole board; the pot is chopped between the best hand on each of the two remaining boards. The double-board bomb pot entry in the same document is a 5-card PLO game: everyone antes, no blinds, no action pre-flop, action starts left of the button after the flop, no burn before the flop because there is no pre-flop action, last card of the deck used for the river with 8 players, premature river with two cards left means reshuffling. [INFERENCE] Derailment therefore uses five hole cards, pot-limit, ante and no pre-flop betting, unless the floor says otherwise; the WSOP text does not restate this.

## Showdown / what wins
- Omaha rule on each surviving board: exactly two hole cards and exactly three cards from that single board (WSOP text for double-board games: no mixing cards from different boards in one hand).
- Two boards survive, so the pot is split in two halves, one to the best hand on each remaining board. A player who has the best hand on both surviving boards scoops (Upswing).
- Players may use different hole-card pairs on different boards (WSOP double-board text: "exactly two of their ... hole cards and exactly three cards from one of the two boards"; Cardplayer Lifestyle's double-board entry says players can use different two-card combinations per board). [INFERENCE] same applies here.
- Ties for lowest river (Cardplayer Lifestyle): if two boards share the same lowest river rank, both are eliminated and the winner of the one remaining board scoops the whole pot. If all three rivers are the same rank, all boards are eliminated and, per that source, the pot goes to the dealer. That last result is odd and unverified elsewhere; do not present it as a standard rule.
- WSOP wording differs: "lowest card by suit". This can be read as a suit tiebreak when river ranks tie (so exactly one board always dies), but the sentence does not say that and no source defines a suit order. Mark as unresolved; ask the floor.
- Ace is presumably high when ranking river cards, since sources say "lowest" without exception [INFERENCE]. A pokergamedatabase.com page for a game it names "Oklahoma" states 2 low, ace high; that site is not an authoritative rule source and the description it gives (Hi-Lo, 8 qualifier) conflicts with the Lodge list's different definition of "Oklahoma", so do not rely on it.
- Hi-only: no low half is mentioned in any source. [Not Hi-Lo.]

## Rooms disagree
- Tie for lowest river: Cardplayer Lifestyle eliminates all tied boards; WSOP's "by suit" suggests a suit tiebreak (or a different reading). Unresolved.
- Betting structure: Cardplayer Lifestyle/Upswing imply blinds and a normal Omaha structure; Lodge says limit; WSOP Derailment inherits ante/no-pre-flop-action bomb-pot structure.
- All-three-tied outcome (pot to the dealer) appears in only one source.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Playing for a scoop on all three boards: only two boards get paid, and which board dies is known only at the river, so a "nut" on the board that gets eliminated is worth nothing.
- Forgetting that the board elimination occurs before the final betting round (Cardplayer Lifestyle, Upswing), so the final bet is made knowing which two boards survive.
- Combining cards across boards.
- Assuming a low half exists.

## Lore & reputation
Upswing's article credits exposure to Doug Polk's vlog at The Lodge Poker Club (Texas), where players in a dealer's-choice game could call any game for an orbit; a VIP-Grinders post about that video lists "Limit Squid Game" among the games called. Cardplayer Lifestyle lists Squid Game among variants played at its Mixed Game Festivals (the article says the "overwhelming majority" of the listed games appear there; it does not individually confirm Squid Game's use). The name is presumably borrowed from the Netflix series [INFERENCE]. Derailment appears in the WSOP 2026 live-action rule document as a named game, but no 2026 WSOP event was found for it; the one double-board tournament event was Event #83 $1,500 Double Board Bomb Pot PLO.
