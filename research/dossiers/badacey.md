## Deal & structure
- Button + small blind + big blind (WSOP: "A button and blind structure will be used"; BARGE: "button and blinds"). Fixed limit is how it is spread (Card Player Lifestyle: "usually limit, and usually capped at 6-handed because of the number of cards in play and discarded").
- Every player gets **five** cards face down. No community cards.
- Four betting rounds with three draws between: pre-draw (left of big blind starts), then after each draw (first live player left of the button starts). Each draw a player may discard 0-5 and gets replacements (WSOP: "entitled to draw up to five cards on each round"; "may receive five consecutive cards off the deck").
- Limit sizing: small bet pre-draw and after draw 1; big bet (double) after draws 2 and 3 (BARGE's Badacey page: small, small, big, big).
- Draw-order/exposed card/"push and pull" delivery and the "may change number of cards drawn until a card is dealt or someone has acted on it" rules are the same as the other WSOP lowball draw games (#321, #324).
- Entering mid-game: wait for the big blind or post a full big blind (WSOP #320).

## Showdown / what wins
Two independent halves, both read from the same five cards. A player can win both (scoop). If everyone else folds, the last player takes the whole pot.
- **Half 1 - A-5 lowball (all five cards).** Aces are LOW; straights and flushes do not count; pairs are bad. Best hand is A-2-3-4-5, any suits. Compare from the highest card down. **No qualifier** - BARGE explicitly says Badacey does not use the eight-or-better rule, and none of the WSOP text mentions one.
- **Half 2 - Badugi (best four-or-fewer-card subset).** A badugi is four cards of four different suits and four different ranks; with five cards you drop whichever card(s) leaves the best set. Aces are LOW here too, so A-2-3-4 in four suits is the best badugi. Any 4-card badugi beats any 3-card hand, which beats any 2-card hand, which beats 1 card. Among equal-length hands compare the highest card first, lower wins. If no one has a 4-card badugi, the best 3-card hand wins that half, and so on (Wizard of Odds, Wikipedia/Badugi, BARGE).
- WSOP #316: the "best hand" is A, 2, 3, 4 of different suits plus a 5 of any suit - i.e. the one hand that is unbeatable in both halves.
- Example (suits s/h/d/c): you hold A♠ 2♥ 3♦ 4♣ 5♠. A-5 low: the wheel A-2-3-4-5 (nuts). Badugi: A♠ 2♥ 3♦ 4♣ rainbow (nuts). You scoop.
- Example: you hold 2♥ 4♥ 6♥ 7♥ A♣. A-5 low: 7-6-4-2-A is a very good low (no qualifier needed). Badugi: only one heart can count, so best is A♣ + one heart: a two-card hand A-2 (or the heart you choose) - any 4-card badugi beats it. You are a one-way hand.
- Ties: each half splits independently, so you can be quartered. Odd chips: WSOP's low-low split rule gives the odd chip by position clockwise from the button (#153), but its Mixed Games simplification says the odd chip goes to the worst position in all button games. ## Rooms disagree
- Ante vs blinds: WSOP, BARGE, Card Player Lifestyle say blinds; Wizard of Odds' summary says "All players make an ante bet." Blinds is the standard in rooms.
- Spelling: Badacey (most explainers, BARGE, PokerNews DC list), Badacy (PokerListings, Wikipedia), Badaci (WSOP Live-Action 2026).
- Draw limit: WSOP says up to five; Wikipedia's Baduci article (the same page documents Badacy as a variant) says "up to a maximum of three cards" in one paragraph while saying zero to five in its intro. Treat five as the rule.
- Max players: WSOP 6; Card Player Lifestyle "usually capped at 6". No conflicting figure found.
- Is it a separate game from Badeucy? Yes: Badeucy pairs the badugi with a deuce-to-seven low and makes the badugi aces-high; Badacey pairs it with ace-to-five and aces-low badugi (Wikipedia: "The only difference is in hand evaluation"; PokerNews DC list treats them as separate entries; see the `badeucy` dossier).

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Confusing the two halves' ace treatment with Badeucy. In Badacey the ace is low in both halves.
- Chasing a pat 8-or-worse badugi: with five cards dealt, showdown badugis are much stronger, so CountingOuts advises discarding any notion of holding an eight-badugi in a multiway pot; aim for at least a seven badugi and seven low.
- Thinking a made low means safety: a hand like 2♥4♥6♥7♥A♣ has a great low but is a one-way hand; someone drawing to a badugi can freeroll you.
- Forgetting that cards like 2♥4♥6♥ don't help the badugi but are still too valuable for the low to throw away (CountingOuts).
- Believing there is an 8-or-better requirement. There isn't.
- Playing it 7-8 handed: with five-card hands and three draws the deck runs out; rooms cap at six.

## Lore & reputation
- Considered a mixed-game staple: CountingOuts says it is "almost always included in any mixed rotation that includes draw variants", slightly less prevalent than Badeucy, and most games are higher stakes.
- On the WSOP Dealer's Choice menu (PokerNews and PokerListings 20-game lists), and Wikipedia says Badacey and Badeucy were both offered in the 2022 $10,000 Dealer's Choice championship. PokerListings says the first WSOP Dealer's Choice (2014) described Badeucy/Badacy as "rarely played (at the time)".
- Also in the Coach's Game at Resorts World's 17-game list (Card Player, 2023) and in the Mixed Game Festival rule sheet (Card Player Lifestyle).
- Card Player Lifestyle: hard to find unless near a casino spreading a regular mix; no online offerings (as of its 2021 guide). CountingOuts' page promotes PhenomPoker as one low-stakes online option (affiliate text; treat lightly).
- Who invented it and when: not documented in sources read.
