## Deal & structure
- Crazy Pineapple (see crazy-pineapple) with a low half added. Each player gets **three** hole cards, blinds/button as in hold'em. Betting round on the three cards, burn + three-card flop, flop betting, then **every active player discards one hole card face down** (folded players don't), then burn + turn / betting / burn + river / betting. From the turn on it is ordinary two-card hold'em.
- Pagat: "The deal and betting are exactly as in Crazy Pineapple." Deuces Wild Casino (rules posted on the California Attorney General's gambling-game site): three down cards, bet, flop, bet, "Players please discard", turn, river; fixed-limit with doubled limits on the turn and river, check-raise allowed, bet plus three raises in multi-way pots, **up to 11 players**; a failure to discard fouls the hand.
- Structure: Deuces Wild's table-fee schedule lists limit games ($2-$4 up to $10-$20), and its rules say the game may also be flex-limit (pot-limit or no-limit). Pagat treats it as a limit game in the hi-lo form [limit is "usual" - source describes it, doesn't say what is most common].

## Showdown / what wins
- After the final round, each player uses **any five cards from the seven available** (two surviving hole cards + five board cards) - there is *no* "exactly two" rule as in Omaha, and **playing the board is allowed**. Pagat: each player makes their best high hand and also "the lowest possible hand from their seven cards"; high and low may use different five-card combinations.
- Pot is split in half: best high, and best **8-or-better low** (five different ranks, all 8 or lower, aces low, straights/flushes don't count; best low is 5-4-3-2-A). If no low qualifies the best high takes the whole pot. One player can scoop.
- No low is possible when the board shows three or more cards higher than 8 (Pagat); you then hold at most 4 low cards (2 hole cards + the 2 low board cards).
- **Odd chip** (Deuces Wild's posted rules): between high and low halves the high hand gets it; between tied highs or tied lows, the first tied player left of the button. WSOP's general hi-lo rule is also high-gets-odd-chip.

## Rooms disagree
- **When the discard happens.** Pagat ("kept until after the second betting round... discard immediately before the turn") and Deuces Wild (flop, *flop betting*, then discard) agree: **discard after flop betting**. Wizard of Odds describes Crazy Pineapple as discarding right after the flop and "before the second round of betting", i.e. before flop betting. If a room/dealer doesn't say, ask.
- **Is Crazy Pineapple hi-lo or high?** WSOP's 2026 live-action rules list Pineapple and Crazy Pineapple as high-only (no hi-lo version). Pagat describes the hi-lo form (and says Crazy Pineapple is "often played" that way); Deuces Wild's Crazy Pineapple rule *is* a hi-lo 8-or-better game.
- **Qualifier:** 8-or-better in both sources.
- **Max players:** Deuces Wild 11; no WSOP cap listed for Pineapple.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Discarding too early (before flop betting is finished) or forgetting to discard at all - the hand can be declared fouled (Deuces Wild).
- Treating it like Omaha: you may use 0, 1 or 2 hole cards and can play the board for high or low.
- Thinking a low is available on any board: three board cards above 8 make a low impossible.
- Treating the discard as automatic: with a split pot, which card you throw away depends on whether you are going for high, low or both; decide after seeing the flop and the flop betting.
- Forgetting the odd chip goes to the high hand in a high/low split.

## Lore & reputation
- Mixed-game rotation use of Crazy Pineapple (e.g. C.H.O.R.S.E.) is covered in the crazy-pineapple dossier; I did not verify whether the hi-lo form is the one used there.
- The hold'em-based hi-lo game is otherwise very rare: searches for stand-alone Hold'em hi-lo/eight-or-better turned up no room, WSOP or mixed-game listing, so Crazy Pineapple Hi-Lo is effectively the only hold'em-family hi-lo game seen in rooms (a California approved-games listing, Pagat). This is an observation from search results, not a sourced statement about popularity.
