## Deal & structure
- Ante, then each player gets 1 card face down (the hole card) and 1 face up (the door card). Four betting rounds in total: after the 2nd card, then after each of 3 more face-up cards (so final hand = 1 hidden + 4 visible cards).
- Limit structure: small bet on the first two rounds, big bet on the last two (BARGE; Upswing: round 1 small, round 2 small with the big bet allowed if a pair is showing, rounds 3-4 big). Raise cap: Upswing says bet + three raises. Fixed-limit and spread-limit games are common; no-limit/pot-limit also played because there are only four rounds (Wikipedia, Stud poker/Five-card stud).
- **Bring-in:** with a bring-in structure, the player with the LOWEST door card by rank and suit (ace high; 2♣ lowest, suits clubs < diamonds < hearts < spades) posts the bring-in and may complete to a full small bet (BARGE; Upswing; Wikipedia).
- **No-bring-in version:** the first round opens with the player showing the HIGHEST card, who may check; no suit tie-break, ties go to the tied player closest clockwise to the dealer (Wikipedia).
- **Later rounds:** the player showing the best high hand (pairs/trips count; there is no straight or flush on four cards) acts first (BARGE; Wikipedia; Upswing).

## Showdown / what wins
- Best five-card high hand wins; all five cards are your hand (no choosing among seven). The last aggressor usually shows the hole card first. Most hands end earlier: "most deals of five-card stud end early when a player bets and gets no calls" (Wikipedia).
- Standard high poker ranking; ties split; odd chip by the stud rule (highest card by suit — see seven-card-stud).
- Wikipedia notes that, in Finland, a variant called Sökö (Canadian/Scandinavian stud) counts 4-card straights and flushes as hands; the UK commonly ranks the low half of five-card stud hi-lo ace-to-six.

## Rooms disagree
- **Not in the modern casino rulebooks:** the WSOP 2026 Live-Action Rules have no Five-Card Stud section; Robert's Rules and TDA cover only seven-card stud/razz/stud 8. BARGE's rulebook (2.30) and Upswing describe it; BARGE gives it as a game that "has featured, or has been featured, at BARGE."
- **Pair-on-board bet doubling:** Upswing lets a pair showing allow the big bet on the second round; BARGE's five-card stud page has no such rule (small/small/big/big).
- **Raise cap:** Upswing three raises; others give none for five-card stud.
- **Betting structure:** typically limit; Wikipedia says it also suits no-limit/pot-limit because of the few rounds.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Assuming 7-card stud rules with a 7th street: there are only four streets, and a single hole card.
- Forgetting that the hole card is your whole bluff/value: most hands end with a bet nobody calls, so the up cards telegraph almost everything (Wikipedia notes transparency "cuts out much of the element of uncertainty").
- Playing the bring-in rule backwards: the low card still brings it in; the highest hand acts first thereafter.
- Treating the ace as the lowest card for the bring-in (it is high).

## Lore & reputation
- Wikipedia: "the earliest form of the card game stud poker, originating during the American Civil War" and "much played among soldiers on both sides"; seven-card stud later became more common.
- Five-card stud was a WSOP preliminary event in 1971-1974, all won by Bill Boyd (his only bracelets); it was dropped from 1975 on and has been absent from all later WSOP schedules (Wikipedia). It was "occasionally still offered in the 1990s" in Europe and North America and is "limited to small home games and small casino games until becoming available at some online poker cardrooms."
- Inclusion note for the site: it is NOT a staple of live mixed-game nights; keep it as a short "history/related" page.
