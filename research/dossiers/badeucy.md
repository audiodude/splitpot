## Deal & structure
- Button + small blind + big blind game (WSOP Live-Action: new players wait for the big blind or post a full big blind, same as other lowball button games). Fixed-limit, six-handed or fewer, is how it is actually spread: Bryan Devonshire (Card Player) says he has only ever seen it limit and that it must be dealt six-handed or less; PokerListings says the same ("most games run six-handed", fixed limit).
- Every player gets **five** cards face down. The same five cards are read two ways at showdown: a five-card deuce-to-seven low, and a four-card badugi (best four-or-fewer-card subset).
- Betting and drawing are identical to 2-7 triple draw: round 1 pre-draw (starts left of the big blind), then up to three draw rounds, each followed by a betting round - four betting rounds, three draws. Small bet pre-draw and after the first draw; big bet (double) after the second and third draws (Devonshire; PokerListings; PokerNews 2014 WSOP guide says it "plays exactly the same as triple draw lowball").
- Each draw: starting with the first live player left of the button, each player discards 0-5 and receives replacements ("pat" = keep all five). WSOP text says a player is entitled to draw up to five on each round. Draw order, announcing/counting draws, exposed-card replacement, and the reshuffle procedure follow the house lowball rules (WSOP: "push and pull" delivery of draw cards; one card burned per draw round even if everyone stands pat).
- Check-raise: WSOP 2026 Live-Action rule 312 says check-raise is allowed; WSOP 2025 Tournament Rules say "check-raising with a perfect hand is permitted" for Badeucey (and Badacey).
- Max players: WSOP 6 (tournament and Live-Action). Plain Badugi is allowed 8 at WSOP, Badeucy only 6.

## Showdown / what wins
Two independent halves; each half is won by the best hand of its type among players still in. One player can win both ("scoop"). If every other player folds, the last player wins the whole pot without showing.

**Half 1 - 2-7 low (five cards).** Standard deuce-to-seven: aces are **high only**, straights and flushes count against you (they rank as straights/flushes), pairs are bad. Best possible = **7-5-4-3-2 not all one suit**. A 6-5-4-3-2 straight is *not* a good low; it is worse than any pair. Compare highest card first, then down.

**Half 2 - Badugi (best four or fewer of your five cards), ACES HIGH.** A "badugi" is four cards of four different suits *and* four different ranks. Any four-card badugi beats any three-card badugi, which beats any two-card, which beats a one-card hand - regardless of how high the cards are. Among equal-length hands, compare the highest card first (lower is better), then the next, and so on. With five cards you pick whichever subset gives the best badugi (duplicates of rank or suit are simply ignored). The best possible badugi is **5-4-3-2 in four suits**; the nut-nut hand is 2-3-4-5 rainbow plus any 7 (Devonshire; Wikipedia Baduci).

**Ace ruling (see "Rooms disagree" for the evidence): the ace is the HIGHEST card in the badugi half too.** An A-2-3-4 rainbow is an ace-high badugi that loses to a 5-4-3-2 and to a king-high badugi.

**Ties/odd chip.** Tied halves split that half only (two players tied for best badugi each get a quarter of the pot). Odd chips: WSOP Live-Action rule 153 says in a button game the first hand clockwise from the button gets the odd chip, but its mixed-game simplification says "all button games, odd chip will go to the worst position" (the two statements differ; the mixed-game line is the one aimed at mixes like this). It names Razzdugi/Razzduci (odd chip to the razz hand) but not Badeucy, so Badeucy's odd-chip owner is not stated explicitly [INFERENCE: use the house button-game rule]. Identical hands split as evenly as possible.

### Tricky showdowns (exact cards, suits shown by letter: s h d c)
**1. Halves go to different players - a real hand (reported by Card Player).** WSOP 2019 $1,500 Dealer's Choice, final draw done. Jim Collopy shows 9h 7s 5s 3c 2d; Mike Ross shows 6s 4c 3d 2h Tc.
- 2-7: Collopy 9-7-5-3-2 (no pair, no straight, mixed suits) beats Ross 10-6-4-3-2 (ten-high). Collopy wins the 2-7 half.
- Badugi: Collopy's best badugi is 9h 5s 3c 2d (nine-high; the alternative 9-7-3-2 is higher, and he cannot use the 7s and 5s together). Ross's 6s 4c 3d 2h is a six-high rainbow badugi, lower, so Ross wins the badugi half.
- Result: pot chopped one half each (the article reports exactly this chop). Both players held a "good" hand and got only half.

**2. The ace is high in the badugi.** Player X: As 2h 3d 4c 9s. Player Y: 5s 4d 3c 2h 8h.
- Badugi: X's best is A-4-3-2 rainbow, which is an *ace-high* badugi (the ace plays above the king). Y holds 5-4-3-2 rainbow. Y's five-high beats X's ace-high. (If aces were low, as in plain Badugi/Badacey, X's A-2-3-4 would be the unbeatable nut and win this half. In Badeucy it loses.)
- 2-7: X is A-9-4-3-2 (ace-high), Y is 8-5-4-3-2 (eight-high). Y scoops.

**3. Nut 2-7 with a two-card badugi vs. a decent badugi with a weak low.** Player P: 2h 3h 4h 5h 7d. Player Q: 9s 8d 6c 3h 2s.
- 2-7: P has 7-5-4-3-2 (not all hearts because of the 7d) - the absolute nut low. Q is 9-8-6-3-2. P wins the low half.
- Badugi: P has one heart plus the 7d - only a two-card badugi (7-2). Q's best is 8d 6c 3h 2s, an eight-high four-suit badugi. Any four-card badugi beats a two-card one. Q wins the badugi half.
- This is Devonshire's point that a pat nut 2-7 with no badugi is only half a hand and can be "freerolled" by a player drawing to a badugi.

**4. A straight ruins the low but makes the nut badugi.** Player R: 2c 3d 4h 5s 6c. Player T: Ks Qd Jh 9c 8s.
- 2-7: R holds 6-5-4-3-2, a straight. In 2-7 a straight is worse than a pair, so R's low is terrible. T is K-Q-J-9-8, king-high with no pair/straight/flush, and wins the low.
- Badugi: R plays 2c 3d 4h 5s (5-4-3-2 rainbow, ignoring the 6c) - the best possible badugi. T's best is Ks Qd Jh 9c (K-Q-J-9, a four-card badugi). R wins the badugi half. R gets half even though R's five-card hand is a "wheel-looking" straight.

**5. Quartering.** Player A: 2s 3h 4d 5c Ks. Player B: 2h 3s 4c 5d 7d.
- Badugi: both have 5-4-3-2 rainbow - tie, that half is split.
- 2-7: B has 7-5-4-3-2 (suits h s c d d - not a flush - so it is the nut low); A has K-5-4-3-2. B wins the low outright.
- Payout: B gets all of the 2-7 half plus half of the badugi half (75% of the pot); A gets 25%.

**6. Nobody has a four-card badugi: three-card hands decide.** Player U: 2d 3h 4s 4c 4d. Player V: Kc 7h 5d 5s 2c.
- Badugi: U has only three distinct ranks, so the best U can do is 2d 3h 4s = 4-3-2 (three cards). V has four distinct ranks but no way to put four suits on them (the two 5s clash and 2c/Kc share a suit), so V's best is 2c 7h 5d = 7-5-2 (three cards). Same length, compare the high card: U's 4-3-2 beats V's 7-5-2. U wins the badugi half.
- 2-7: U holds three 4s (4-4-4-3-2, trips); V holds a pair of fives (K-7-5-5-2). In 2-7 a pair beats trips as a low hand, so V wins the 2-7 half. Neither player has a "good" hand; the pot is still split.

## Rooms disagree
**The ace in the badugi half - resolved: ACES ARE HIGH (best badugi 5-4-3-2). This is canonical.**
Sources saying ace is HIGH in the badugi half of Badeucy/Baduci:
- WSOP 2026 Live-Action Rules (rule 307 and intro): badugi half awarded to the best badugi "excluding the Ace since the Ace is considered as a high card"; best hand = 2-3-4-5 different suits with a 7; "Aces are considered high in this game."
- WSOP 2025 Tournament Rules: "best Badugi hand (excluding the Ace)"; best hand 2-3-4-5 of different suits with a 7; "The 'Ace' is always a HIGH CARD."
- Wikipedia (Baduci): best badugi five-high, 2-3-4-5; "differs from straight Badugi, in which aces are low... an ace plays as the highest card and loses to a king."
- PokerListings, CountingOuts, Card Player (Devonshire), Card Player Lifestyle (Mixed Game Poker Rules), PokerNews 2014 WSOP Dealer's Choice guide (nuts 2-3-4-5-7 with the first four in different suits), Randy Ohel interview (Card Player), Denexa (aces high in both evaluations).
Outliers:
- Wizard of Odds' Badeucy/Badacey page has a generic "Hand Evaluation in Badugi" section saying aces are low and the lowest badugi is A-2-3-4, then states aces are high only for the *lowball* half of Baducey. Read literally, that is an ace-low badugi in Badeucy. It never says the ace is low or high specifically for Badeucy's badugi half, and it contradicts WSOP/Wikipedia/Card Player; treat it as imprecise.
- Pokercine's Baduci page lists A-2-3-4 as the best badugi and also claims the best 2-7 hand is A-2-3-4-5 (wrong for 2-7), so it is unreliable.
Why aces-high is canonical: (1) it is the written rule in the most authoritative rule sheets (both WSOP documents) and in the dedicated explainers - seven independent sources agree, versus one ambiguous page and one unreliable page; (2) [INFERENCE] it makes the game coherent: with the ace high in both halves, a single hand like 2-3-4-5-7 (four suits) is the nuts for both halves, and the "ace" means the same thing in both evaluations of the same five cards. The ace-low-everything version is its own game, Badacey (A-5 low + ace-low badugi).
Residual ambiguity: the WSOP phrase "excluding the Ace" could be misread as "an ace cannot be part of a badugi". Both WSOP texts immediately say the ace is a high card, and Wikipedia says it loses to a king, so the standard reading is "ace is the top rank, not removed". The practical difference only shows in rare spots (an ace-high four-card badugi vs a three-card badugi).

**Is Badacey separate? Yes.** Badacey (WSOP spelling Badaci/Badacey) pairs the badugi with an ace-to-five low (aces low, straights/flushes don't count) and uses the ace-low badugi, best hand A-2-3-4 four suits plus a 5 (WSOP 2025 and 2026; Wikipedia: "The only difference is in hand evaluation"; PokerNews 2014). It changes what wins, so it deserves its own page; Badeucy and Badacey are always called as separate games in a dealer's-choice list (WSOP DC lists "badeucy, badacy" as separate games).

Other differences:
- Spelling: Baduci (WSOP Live-Action, Wikipedia title), Badeucey (WSOP Tournament Rules, Card Player), Badeucy (PokerNews, PokerListings), Baducy. Same game.
- Draw count: WSOP and PokerListings say a player may draw up to five; Wikipedia's "Play of the hand" paragraph says "up to a maximum of three cards" while its intro says zero to five. Treat five as the rule (WSOP).
- Check-raise: Live-Action 2026 "allowed"; 2025 Tournament Rules specifically "with a perfect hand is permitted." Some rooms' lowball rules limit check-raising (WSOP lowball intro "check-raise is allowed, on any round after any draw" for Badugi), so follow house rules.
- Betting structure: nearly everyone says fixed-limit; Wikipedia adds pot-limit, half-pot, no-limit are possible but rarer.

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Treating the ace as low in the badugi half (the most common one; see above). A-2-3-4 is NOT the nuts here.
- Treating the best 2-7 hand as A-2-3-4-5 or 6-5-4-3-2; the nut low is 7-5-4-3-2.
- Playing a pat deuce-to-seven hand with no badugi (e.g., 2h 3h 4h 5h 7d): Devonshire says players "get freerolled" by someone drawing to a badugi; he keeps a pat deuce hand only if it is super strong or has a strong badugi component.
- Assuming a king-high badugi can't win the badugi half. A four-card badugi of any rank beats every three-card badugi.
- Keeping an eight-or-worse made badugi unimproved with five cards in the deal. Because everyone gets five cards, the average showdown badugi is much stronger than in regular Badugi (CountingOuts: forget starting with a ten-high or worse badugi; usually discard the nine too).
- Forgetting the straight in 2-7: 6-5-4-3-2 is a straight, not a great low.
- Thinking a made half-hand is safe. The halves are independent: you can win one half and be chopped on the other, so a "pat nut" in one half can still lose the other.
- Drawing too wide: pros want smooth three-card starters in three different suits (e.g., 2-3-4 rainbow) - Devonshire calls 2-3-4, 2-3-5 and 2-4-5 rainbow premiums; Ohel says the playable upfront hands resemble triple-draw hands but with three different suits.

## Lore & reputation
- Devonshire (Card Player, 2013): Badeucey and Badacey "first showed up in Vegas around 2007", origin unknown, not available online at that time, "not solved"; blends triple draw, badugi and stud hi-lo ideas. CountingOuts calls it almost always included in mixed rotations that have draw games; many games are higher stakes.
- WSOP debut: the 2014 inaugural $1,500 six-handed Dealer's Choice event (PokerNews guide) listed badeucy and badacy, calling them high-stakes cash-game favorites unlikely to be called often there. Wikipedia says both were offered in the $10,000 dealer's-choice championship at the 2022 WSOP. A Badeucey hand from the 2019 $1,500 Dealer's Choice (Jim Collopy vs Mike Ross) is analysed by Randy Ohel at Card Player.
- Ken Lo's book "A Poker Player's Guide to Mixed Games" has a sizeable Badeucey chapter (per CountingOuts' review).
- Strategy flavour cited by pros: "freeroll them, don't get freerolled"; position matters somewhat less than in plain 2-7 because drawing fewer cards doesn't always mean you're ahead (Devonshire).
- Origin of the name: combination of Badugi + "deuce" (2-7); who coined it is not documented in the sources consulted.
