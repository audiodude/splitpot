## Deal & structure
Same as `drawmaha`; only the draw half's ranking is different.
- Blinds (WSOP also allows antes/multiple blinds), 5 hole cards each, betting round, 3-card flop, betting round.
- Single draw after the flop betting: declare 1-3 cards or stand pat (WSOP). Declared counts are final.
- Turn, betting; river, betting; showdown. WSOP allows limit, pot-limit or no-limit; max 7 players.

## Showdown / what wins
Pot splits in two halves, no qualifier.
1. **Omaha-high half:** best high hand from exactly 2 hole cards + exactly 3 board cards.
2. **A-5 low half:** best ace-to-five lowball hand from your five hole cards only (board cards do not play). Ranking (WSOP ace-to-five lowball rule):
   - Ace is always low.
   - Straights and flushes do NOT count against you.
   - Best hand is 5-4-3-2-A (the "wheel"). Pairs are bad: any no-pair hand beats any paired hand [standard lowball ranking; WSOP states only the wheel and the straight/flush rule].
   - Compare highest card first: 6-4-3-2-A beats 6-5-3-2-A.
3. A single player can scoop both halves. Odd chip: by position starting clockwise from the button (WSOP Drawmaha rule).

## Rooms disagree
- Draw cap and procedure disagreements are the same as in `drawmaha` (WSOP 1-3 cards, turn placed before draw cards are dealt; BARGE base Dramaha allows 0-5 and deals the draw before the turn).
- Naming: WSOP says "Ace-5", Cardplayer Lifestyle says "Drawmaha A-5", Haney uses "Ace-to-Five". Spelling Dramaha/Drawmaha is interchangeable.
- No source found that gives a separate A-5-specific qualifier, so treat it as no qualifier [INFERENCE from WSOP's general "best 5-card hand from hole cards" wording].

## Common newcomer mistakes
- Using the 2-7 rules (ace high, straights/flushes bad) instead of A-5 (ace low, straights/flushes ignored).
- Throwing away aces: Haney notes the ace is a key card for Omaha strength AND for the A-5 side, making it hard to know when to hold it.
- Playing a wheel draw as if it also wins the Omaha half; board connectivity is separate.
- Forgetting there is only one draw, so a one-card A-5 draw that misses leaves you with half a hand.

## Lore & reputation
- Kevin Haney (Card Player, 2025) says an A-5 version of Dramaha exists "but is far less popular", gives it 5/10 and says it plays second fiddle to the 2-7 version; the ace may be too important for Omaha, and starting-hand values are harder to judge. For a small rotation he suggests a badugi or point variant instead.
- WSOP rules and Cardplayer Lifestyle's Mixed Game Festival list name it; I found no real-room rotation list that features it by name (Coach's Resorts World mix and Phenom's mixes do not include it).
