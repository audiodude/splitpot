## Drawmaha with an [A-5 low](/glossary#a5-low) draw half

[Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha) skeleton: five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half the pot to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)), half to the best ace-to-five low from your own five cards, board ignored. [Limit](/glossary#limit) or [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit); WSOP cap is seven players.

## Cheat sheet

- Draw half: five final hole cards, ace low, straights and flushes ignored, lowest hand wins. Nuts: the [wheel](/glossary#wheel).
- Any unpaired hand beats any paired hand. Compare from the top card down.
- Omaha half: exactly two hole cards plus three board, high.
- No qualifier assumed (the sources never mention one). Both halves pay out, a player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop), and ties split their half.
- WSOP draw: 1 to 3 or [pat](/glossary#snow), locked once declared, turn dealt before the draw cards. BARGE and others allow 0 to 5 or deal the draw first.
- Odd chip: WSOP says first player clockwise from the button.

## Watch out for

- The ace is a premium card on both halves, so what to keep is rarely obvious. A one-card draw to a wheel that misses leaves you half a hand, and there is only one draw.
- A wheel does not win Omaha unless the board connects. A-2-3-4-5 is a [one-way hand](/glossary#one-way-hand) as often as not.
- Playing 2-7 instincts: here a pair of aces loses to K-high unpaired.
- Haney rates it 5/10 and "far less popular" than 2-7. Don't expect to see it often, and ask which rules the table uses.
