## [Badugi](/glossary#badugi) with a [hi-lo](/glossary#hi-lo) split: low badugi versus [high badugi](/glossary#high-badugi)

Same as Badugi: button and [blinds](/glossary#blinds), four cards, three [draws](/glossary#draw), [limit](/glossary#limit). Half the pot to the lowest badugi, half to the "highest", which is read bottom-up.

## Cheat sheet

- Low half: standard Badugi, A-2-3-4 [rainbow](/glossary#rainbow) is the nuts.
- High half: still needs a valid badugi (four suits, four ranks). Winner is the **highest lowest card**, then the next-lowest, and so on. Haney: "read the hand backwards".
- Ace is the lowest card for low and the top card for high.
- Examples: J-8-7-5 (jack-low, five-high) beats Q-8-5-4 (queen-low, four-high) both ways. K-Q-5-3 loses both ways to J-10-7-5. A-8-7-6 (eight-low, six-high) gets nothing against K-Q-10-9 (wins high) and 7-6-5-3 (wins low).
- A-K-Q-J rainbow has the highest low card (a jack) with ace on top, so it should be the best high badugi ([INFERENCE]; the sources don't name the nut).
- High half with three-card hands, and the [odd chip](/glossary#odd-chip): unspecified. Presumably card count first, as in Badugi ([INFERENCE]).
- Scoops are rare. Strong lows have weak highs and vice versa.

## Watch out for

- Judging high by the top card. It's the lowest card that counts.
- A-2-3-4 is the nut low and about the worst high there is.
- A hand that can't be scooped heads-up can still win nothing three-way.
- Card Player Lifestyle's "J-10-7-5 is jack-low and 7-high" is loose: by the bottom-up rule it's five-high. The ruling in their example doesn't change.
