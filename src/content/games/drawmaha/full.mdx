## It's Omaha with five cards and a draw in the middle

Think of it as two games stapled together. You get five cards. There's a flop, then everyone gets one chance to throw cards away and draw new ones, then a turn and a river. At the end the pot splits two ways:

- **Half** goes to the best Omaha hand: exactly two of your five cards plus exactly three from the board.
- **Half** goes to the best five-card high hand made from just *your own five cards*, board ignored.

That's why people call it the world's most confusing way to hold the same cards twice.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- Five hole cards each. Betting, then a three-card flop, then betting.
- Then **one draw**: everybody says how many cards (1 to 3 in WSOP rules, or standing pat) to throw away and gets replacements.
- Turn, betting, river, betting.
- Half the pot: best **Omaha high** (exactly 2 from your hand + 3 board).
- Half the pot: best **5-card poker hand from your own cards** only.
- Played pot-limit, limit or no-limit. Seven players max at the WSOP.

## Full rules

### The deal and first round
Button, small and big blind (WSOP rules let rooms use one blind, several, antes or a mix). Five cards face down each. Betting starts left of the big blind.

### The flop
Burn a card, deal three face-up community cards. Betting round, starting left of the button.

### The draw
This is the part that makes it Drawmaha. Starting left of the button, each player announces how many cards they're drawing. The WSOP lets you draw one to three cards, or stand pat. Once you've named a number, you're stuck with it. The dealer burns and deals the turn, then deals out the draw cards. Betting round.

Rooms disagree on a lot of the draw details, so ask: some let you draw all five, some only two, and some deal the draw cards before the turn card instead of after.

### River and showdown
River card, final betting round, showdown. Only your **final five** cards count. Anything you threw away is gone, even if it was a great Omaha card.

### Two independent halves
- **Omaha half:** use exactly two of your five cards and exactly three board cards. Best five-card high hand.
- **Draw half:** use all five of your own cards. Ordinary high-hand rankings. The board does nothing.

Ties split their half. Odd chips go by position (check the house rule).

## Worked examples

### The monster that only gets half

Alice has kings full of fours, a monster in the draw half. But in the Omaha half she can only use two of her cards, so her best is a pair of kings. Bob's ace-high flush (A♠ J♠ with the board's Q♠ 8♠ 3♠) is the best Omaha hand, and his five cards on their own are just ace-high. One pot, two different winners.

### The scoop

Carol's 6-5-4-3-2 is a straight in the draw half, and the same hand makes a nine-high straight in the Omaha half (6-5 plus the board's 9-8-7). Dave's two pair and three kings can't match either. Carol scoops.

### Same board, different winner

Gus has the better draw hand (a pair of aces) so he wins that half. But on a 5-6-7 board, Hana's 9-8 in her hand makes a nine-high straight, beating Gus's 8-4 eight-high straight. Which two cards you pair with the board matters.

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Using more than two hole cards for the Omaha half.** Exactly two. Always.
- **Discarding half your Omaha hand to chase the draw.** Only your final five cards count at showdown.
- **Falling in love with big Omaha cards.** A-K-Q-J-x with no pair makes a strong Omaha hand but a weak draw hand: a one-way hand.
- **Ignoring how many cards your opponents drew.** A one-card draw is scary. A four-card draw is usually a pair at best.
- **Thinking one player has to win both halves.** It's totally normal for two different players to each win half.

## Lore and reputation

Drawmaha (also spelled Dramaha) is a mixed-game cult favorite. One online poker room claims the game was invented at a poker club in Stockholm in 2006 and was originally called Sviten, though that's the room's own claim. It is spread in Las Vegas mixed games, especially during the WSOP. It has a lot of cousins: the draw half can be 2-7 low, ace-to-five low, badugi, or even "49" (a pip count where face cards are worth zero and the highest total wins).

A popular twist called Sviten Special: if you draw exactly one card, it's dealt face up and you can keep it or reject it for a face-down replacement.
