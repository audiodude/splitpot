## It's like Omaha Hi-Lo, except the low doesn't have to be good

[Omaha Hi-Lo](/games/omaha-hi-lo) says a low only counts if it's 8-or-better, and if nobody has one, high takes the whole pot. Omaha Hi-Lo Regular deletes that clause. The WSOP's entire rule is: all the rules of Omaha High-Low 8/O apply, "except that there is no qualifier for low." So there's *always* a low half, and somebody wins it, even if the best low is a 10-high ugly mess.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- Four hole cards, flop, turn, river, four betting rounds. Same as [Omaha Hi-Lo](/games/omaha-hi-lo).
- **Exactly two** hole cards and **exactly three** board cards for each hand.
- Half the pot goes to the best high. Half goes to the best **ace-to-five low**, whether or not it's 8-or-better.
- Ace-to-five: aces low, straights and flushes don't count against you, the [wheel](/glossary#wheel) is best, and you compare from the highest card down.
- The pot is split nearly every hand. The high hand only scoops if the same player also owns the best low.
- Rare. This lives in the WSOP's live-action rule sheet and Dealer's Choice, not in a normal rotation.

## Full rules

### The deal
Identical to Omaha Hi-Lo. Dealer button, blinds or antes, four hole cards each, betting, burn and flop, betting, burn and turn, betting, burn and river, final betting round. The WSOP spreads Omaha Hi-Lo as limit, pot-limit or no-limit, and this game uses the same options.

### Building your hands
You make a high hand and a low hand, each from **exactly two hole cards and exactly three board cards**. They can use different hole cards.

- **High:** best five-card poker hand.
- **Low:** the best [ace-to-five low](/glossary#a5-low) you can make. Aces are low, straights and flushes are ignored, and A-2-3-4-5 is the best possible. You compare from the highest card down, so 9-6-3-2-A beats 9-6-5-4-2.

### No qualifier
In regular Omaha Hi-Lo you need five different ranks, all 8 or lower, to have a low at all ([eight-or-better](/glossary#eight-or-better)). Here you don't. Every player has to use two hole cards and three board cards, so every player has *some* low hand, and the best one wins half the pot. A board of K-Q-9-T-4 means O8 high takes everything, but here the low half still gets paid.

### Ties, quartering, and the odd chip
Tied hands split their half. One high winner and two tied lows means half and a quarter each. The [odd chip](/glossary#odd-chip) works the same as in Omaha Hi-Lo.

### Rooms disagree
- **Only one room writes this rule down.** The WSOP live-action sheet is the only room document we found that defines this game by name. The WSOP's tournament rules and Golden Nugget's list a Stud version ("Seven Card Stud Hi/Low Regular") but no Omaha one. This page uses the WSOP sheet.
- **How paired lows rank.** The WSOP's Omaha text doesn't say how a hand with a pair ranks against hands without one, because in O8 a pair is never a qualifying low. By analogy with ace-to-five lowball, the likely reading is that any unpaired hand beats any paired hand. That is our inference, not a quoted rule. If the board is paired and it matters, ask the floor.
- **What "no qualifier" means.** If someone says "Omaha 8 with no qualifier," confirm they don't mean a 9-or-better qualifier, which a few casinos use as a house rule. That's a different game.

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Folding because the board has no low.** Someone always owns the low half. A 9-high or 10-high low wins money.
- **Counting the high hand's scoop as a sure thing.** The high hand takes the whole pot only if the same player also has the best low.
- **Forgetting the two-and-three rule.** You still need two hole cards and three board cards, so you can't dodge a paired board for your low.
- **Paying off a mediocre high because "I can't lose the low."** If you hold the low, you've won half the pot and no more.
- **Assuming the room knows this game.** Confirm there's no qualifier before the deal.

## Lore and reputation

This is a rarity. A PokerChipForum thread about a no-qualifier Big O has its author saying he never sees anyone play it. Replies argue the qualifier is a big part of the game's appeal, and one poster claims there's no low about 60 percent of the time. That claim is unverified forum chatter, not a rule source. Treat the game as a Dealer's Choice option rather than a staple. The WSOP's sheet lists it right under Omaha 8/O.
