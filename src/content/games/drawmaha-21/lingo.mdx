## Drawmaha with a closest-to-21 [pip count](/glossary#pip-count) draw half

[Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha) skeleton: five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)), half to the hole-card total closest to 21, board ignored. [Limit](/glossary#limit) or [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit). BARGE's rulebook is the only rule-sheet source; it isn't in the WSOP rules.

## Cheat sheet

- Points: A = 1 (never 11), 2 to T = face value, J/Q/K = 0.
- BARGE: play the best subset of your five cards that totals 21 or less, ignoring the cards that would push you over. No [bust](/glossary#bust). Closest to 21 wins.
- Tiebreak: more cards used wins. A-K-Q-T-T is a five-card 21, K-T-T-8-6 is a three-card 20, 9-8-7-6-5 is a three-card 21.
- Haney's version: going over 21 busts the draw half and you play for Omaha only. Ask the table which.
- Omaha half: exactly two hole cards plus three board, high. A player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop). Ties that survive the card-count tiebreak split the half.
- Draw: BARGE 0 to 3 cards. Odd chip and turn order: house rules, since the WSOP doesn't spread it.

## Watch out for

- Ace is 1, ten is 10, faces are 0.
- Forgetting the more-cards tiebreak: a five-card 21 beats a three-card 21.
- Whether a zero-point card counts toward the card total is not settled between BARGE's examples and one of Haney's.
- No source says what happens if every hand busts under the Haney rule.
- Haney rates it 6/10 and "somewhat gimmicky". His complaint: nobody knows what a playable hand looks like, so play is slow and tight. BARGE ran it in the EMBARGO 2026 Drawmaha mix.

## Examples

Under BARGE rules, final hands: Gus 4♠ 5♦ 6♥ A♣ 5♣ = 21 using five cards, Hal 9♠ 8♦ 7♥ 6♣ 5♠ = 21 using three cards (9 + 7 + 5), Ivy K♣ T♥ T♠ 8♣ 4♦ = 20 (T + T, the rest ignored). Gus wins the 21 half on the tiebreak. Under Haney's rule Hal and Ivy would simply bust. Haney's best-case dealt hand is A♦ A♣ 8♦ 8♣ 3♥, a five-card 21.
