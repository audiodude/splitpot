## It's razz, except straights and flushes ruin your hand

You know razz: seven-card stud, lowest five-card hand wins, ace is low, straights and flushes don't count against you, so A-2-3-4-5 is the best hand. London Lowball deals exactly the same way, but ranks hands **ace-to-six**: the ace is still low, but **straights and flushes count against you**, as in deuce-to-seven. The best hand is **6-4-3-2-A, not all one suit**. And traditionally, it's played **pot-limit**.

**How rare is it?** Very. BARGE's rulebook calls it "seldom seen in games played in American casinos", Wikipedia says it's played "almost exclusively in Europe", and no US room rulebook I found (WSOP, TDA, Robert's) includes it. You'll almost certainly never see it at a casino mixed-games night. It's here for completeness.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- Deal like razz: ante, two down and one up, then three more up cards and a last card down. Betting after every card, five rounds.
- **Highest door card brings it in** (ace low, ties by suit, K♠ highest). After that, the player with the lowest hand showing acts first.
- Lowest five-card hand from your seven cards wins the whole pot. No qualifier.
- **Ace is low, but straights and flushes count against you.** Best hand: 6-4-3-2-A offsuit. 5-4-3-2-A is a straight, so it's bad.
- Pairs are bad, as always.
- Traditionally pot-limit: minimum bet is the size of the bring-in, maximum is the pot.

## Full rules

### The deal and bring-in
Everyone antes. Each player gets two cards face down and one face up, then three more face up and a last card face down, with betting after every card. Whoever shows the highest door card brings it in. The ace counts low here, ties break by suit, and K♠ is the highest. After the first round, the player showing the lowest hand acts first.

### Pot-limit betting
BARGE's rule: "For all rounds, the minimum bet will be equal to the size of the bring-in and the minimum raise is the size of the last bet or raise. The maximum wager is the size of the pot." Every source I found says the traditional game is pot-limit, and Wikipedia says the *name* London Lowball is typically reserved for the pot-limit version. BARGE and Wikipedia mention no-limit variants. This is not a fixed-limit game with razz-style raise caps.

### Showdown: ace-to-six
Use any five of your seven cards for the lowest hand. The ace is low, but a straight or flush loses to any non-straight, non-flush hand with a worse high card. Best to worst, BARGE's list starts: 6-4-3-2-A, 6-5-3-2-A, 6-5-4-2-A, 6-5-4-3-A, 7-4-3-2-A, 7-5-3-2-A... Pairs are worse than any unpaired hand. There's no joker. Ties split; odd chips by the stud rules.

### Rooms disagree
- **Where it's played:** Wikipedia says "almost exclusively in Europe". BARGE says it's seldom seen in American casinos and first played at BARGE in 2018. A review of Stewart Reuben's *Poker 24/7* says it was popular in London clubs and "has never been used in U.S. casinos".
- **Structure:** all sources say pot-limit; some mention no-limit.
- **Hand scale:** Wikipedia and BARGE agree 6-4-3-2-A is best.

## Worked examples

I have no verified worked examples for this game, because the hand evaluator doesn't support ace-to-six yet. Here's the idea in plain English.

- **6-4-3-2-A offsuit** is the nuts. **5-4-3-2-A** is a five-high straight, so it loses to a hand like K-Q-J-9-8.
- **7-5-4-3-A** beats **7-6-4-3-A**, because you compare from the highest card down.
- A flush, even A-2-3-5-7 in hearts, is a flush and loses to any unpaired offsuit hand. [INFERENCE from "flushes count against you" as in deuce-to-seven; no source gives this exact example.]

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Assuming straights and flushes don't count.** They do. That's the whole difference from razz.
- **Thinking A-2-3-4-5 is the nuts.** It's a straight. The nuts is 6-4-3-2-A.
- **Playing it with fixed-limit betting.** It's a pot-limit game.
- **Confusing it with draw ace-to-six lowball.** Pagat treats that as a separate game.

## Lore and reputation

Stewart Reuben, the British chess organizer and co-author of *Pot-Limit & No-Limit Poker* with Bob Ciaffone, played in a London scene where pot-limit seven-card stud and London Lowball were popular. A review of his memoir *Poker 24/7* says the game "led to a great deal of money changing hands" and that he didn't rate it highly. (I only read the review, not the book.) BARGE treats it as a rarity: it was first played there in 2018.
