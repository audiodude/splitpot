## It's Hold'em, except the bet sizes are decided for you

Same cards, same board, same hands as no-limit. The only thing that changes is that nobody gets to choose how much to bet. Every bet and raise is a fixed size, and that's why limit Hold'em is the "H" in H.O.R.S.E.: it's the game every mixed table can play without a stack-size argument.

You're not going to bluff anyone out of a pot for their whole stack here. You're going to call a lot more, with a lot more hands, and the pots are going to get big anyway.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- Two hole cards, flop, turn, river. Button, small blind, big blind. Four betting rounds.
- Pre-flop and flop use the [small bet](/glossary#small-bet), equal to the big blind. Turn and river use the [big bet](/glossary#big-bet), double the small bet. At $4/$8: $4 bets before the turn, $8 after.
- A raise is the previous bet plus the current limit. There's no "raise to whatever".
- Under WSOP rules, a round allows one bet plus **four raises** with three or more players. After that you can only call or fold. Heads-up from the start of a round: raises are unlimited.
- Button straddles aren't allowed. A new player posts the big blind to get a hand.
- Showdown is plain Hold'em: best five of seven, high hand only.

## Full rules

### The deal
Dealer button, small blind, big blind, two cards each, exactly like no-limit Hold'em. The small blind is half the big blind, though some rooms round it down at stakes like $5/$10. The dealer [burns](/glossary#burn-card) a card before the flop, turn, and river.

### The betting
Pre-flop betting starts left of the big blind. After the flop, it starts with the first live player left of the button.

**Bet sizes**
- Pre-flop and on the flop: the small bet. At $4/$8, every bet and raise is exactly $4.
- On the turn and river: the big bet, $8.

**Raising.** You raise by exactly one bet size on top of the bet you're facing. On the flop at $4/$8: bet $4, raise to $8, re-raise to $12, then $16, then $20. On the turn the steps are $8: bet $8, then $16, $24, $32, $40.

**The cap.** Under WSOP rule 130, a round allows a bet and four raises when three or more players aren't all-in. A [raise cap](/glossary#raise-cap) means after the fourth raise you can only call or fold. Straddles don't count as a bet for the pre-flop cap. If the round *starts* heads-up, raising is unlimited (rule 131). If a round starts multiway and shrinks to two players mid-round, the cap still applies.

**Short all-ins.** An all-in for less than half a bet does not reopen the betting for players who already acted. Half a bet or more counts as a full bet or raise (WSOP rule 133; the TDA uses the same half-bet idea). See [short all-in](/glossary#short-all-in).

**Buy-in and posting.** The WSOP full buy-in is ten small bets. A player entering a limit game posts the big blind to be dealt in. No straddling on the button.

**Kill pots.** Some rooms run a [kill](/glossary#kill-pot). In the WSOP rules, a "full kill" is a kill blind of double the big blind that doubles the limits, and a "half kill" is 1.5x with limits raised by that amount. It's usually a lowball and hi-lo thing, and rooms may or may not run it in Hold'em.

### Showdown
Best five of seven, any combination of your hole cards. Ties split the pot, and the odd [chip](/glossary#odd-chip) goes to the first tied seat clockwise from the button. Side pots split separately.

### Rooms disagree
The raise cap is a **house rule**. WSOP: one bet plus four raises, unlimited heads-up. PokerListings says one bet plus three raises, unlimited when only two players remain. The TDA says the house limit applies even heads-up until the event is down to two players. This page uses the WSOP version. Ask the dealer what the cap is before you re-raise the third time.

The small blind and exact limits ($6/$12, $8/$16) also vary by room, and kills are optional.

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Trying to bet a number.** In limit you bet one size. NLHE habits like min-raising or "pot-sizing" mean nothing here.
- **Betting the small bet on the turn.** The bet doubles on the *turn*, not the flop. Small bet twice, big bet twice.
- **Raising when the round is capped.** Call or fold. That's it.
- **Playing like it's no-limit.** Bluffs work worse, and draws are cheaper to chase, so you play closer to showdown with more drawing hands. Value-bet your good hands.
- **Straddling the button.** Not allowed in limit.

## Lore and reputation

Wikipedia calls limit Hold'em historically the most popular form of Hold'em in US casino live action, until no-limit overtook it with the televised WSOP and WPT. In mixed games it's the "H" in H.O.R.S.E., which debuted at the WSOP in 2002 with a $2,000 buy-in, and it's in the WSOP Poker Players Championship's 8-game and 10-game mixes (the 10-game mix, since 2015, includes limit Hold'em). Wikipedia also mentions the University of Alberta's "Cepheus" program (Science, January 2015) which plays heads-up limit Hold'em nearly perfectly.
