## Drawmaha with a [badugi](/glossary#badugi) draw half

[Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha) skeleton: five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half the pot to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)), half to the best badugi from your own five cards, board ignored. [Limit](/glossary#limit) or [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit); WSOP cap is seven players. Also called Dramadugi or Drawmaha Low-Dugi. **Aces are low.**

## Cheat sheet

- Badugi half: best subset of up to four of your five final cards, all different suits and ranks. More cards beats fewer; same count, lowest high card wins. Nuts: A-2-3-4 [rainbow](/glossary#rainbow).
- No [qualifier](/glossary#qualifier): a three-card badugi can win the half if nobody has four. WSOP example: A-2-3 beats A-3-4.
- Omaha half: exactly two hole cards plus three board, high.
- WSOP draw: 1 to 3 or [pat](/glossary#snow), turn dealt before the draw cards. BARGE: 0 to 3. Ties split their half; a player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop).
- Odd chip: WSOP says first player clockwise from the button.

## Watch out for

- Four low rainbow cards make a great badugi and a weak Omaha hand: a [one-way hand](/glossary#one-way-hand) in the wrong direction.
- A four-card K-high badugi still beats a three-card A-2-3.
- Keeping a card that breaks the badugi (a pair, or a second card in the same suit) to chase Omaha.
- Haney rates it 8/10 and Badger says it tilts people more than the other variants. Don't confuse it with LOB, which was four cards and three draws.
- Don't mix up with [Badugi High](/games/badugi-high-drawmaha): that one ranks four-suited hands high, so the nuts is four aces.
