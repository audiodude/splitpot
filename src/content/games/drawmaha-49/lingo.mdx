## Drawmaha with a high [pip count](/glossary#pip-count) draw half

[Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha) skeleton: five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)), half to the highest point total on your five final cards, board ignored. [Limit](/glossary#limit) or [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit); WSOP cap is seven players.

## Cheat sheet

- Points: A = 1, 2 to T = face value, J/Q/K = 0. Max is T-T-T-T-9 = 49. Highest total wins. No poker rankings on this half; suits irrelevant.
- Ties on the points split the half. No card-count tiebreak is documented.
- Omaha half: exactly two hole cards plus three board, high. Both halves always pay out, and a player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop).
- WSOP draw: 1 to 3 or [pat](/glossary#snow). BARGE: 0 to 3. "Draw2maha 49" caps at 2. Turn dealt before the draw cards at the WSOP; some rooms draw first.
- Odd chip: WSOP says first player clockwise from the button.
- Average card is about 4.2 points (Haney): about 21 is typical, 33 is "way above average", mid-30s wins often.
- [Blockers](/glossary#blocker) matter: T-T-T-9-8 (47) means nobody can top 45.

## Watch out for

- Faces are worth zero, so Omaha cards (A-K-Q-J) are dead weight on the points half. Mostly a [one-way hand](/glossary#one-way-hand) game, and scoops are harder than in [Zero](/games/drawmaha-zero).
- Some online sources score faces as 1. BARGE, WSOP and Haney all say 0.
- The ace is 1, not 11 or 14.
- Only four tens and four nines exist, so everybody is drawing to the same few cards. A three-card draw is usually a fold.
- Haney rates it 5/10. Forum opinion is divided, and one poster says it plays better limit than pot-limit.

## Examples

Final hands: Ana T♠ T♦ 9♣ 8♥ 3♠ = 40, Ben K♣ J♦ T♥ 9♦ 7♣ = 26, Cat A♥ 9♠ 9♥ 7♦ 6♠ = 32. Ana takes the 49 half. On a board of K♠ K♥ Q♦ 4♦ 2♣, Ben's K♣ J♦ makes trips and beats Ana's kings and tens and Cat's kings and nines, so he takes the Omaha half despite the lowest total.
