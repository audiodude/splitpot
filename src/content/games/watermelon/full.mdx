## It's like a double board game, except there are three boards and points decide who wins

Five hole cards, three complete boards, and a scoring system. Win a board, earn a point. The player with the most points takes the **whole** pot.

**Rarity: very low, and thinly documented.** Exactly one source describes it: Card Player Lifestyle's mixed-game rules page. It is not in the WSOP 2026 live-action rules. Anything the source doesn't state is marked below. A 2+2 forum thread titled "Watermelon?? Pineapple? who knows" exists but we couldn't open it, and it may be about a different game.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- A five-card Omaha variant with three boards, each with flop, turn and river, and normal betting between streets.
- Best hand on a board earns a point. Three points total.
- Tied for a board: half a point each.
- Most points wins the entire pot.
- Tied on points: the pot is divided among the tied players.
- Not stated by the source: structure, burns, player count, odd chip.

## Full rules

### Deal and streets
Five hole cards, then three boards with usual betting rounds between streets. The source gives no burn procedure, player maximum, or betting limit. Pot-limit is our assumption. [INFERENCE]

### Showdown
Each board is an Omaha-style hand. The source states only that each board is a full Omaha-style board. Exactly two of five hole cards plus three from that board is our reading. [INFERENCE] Whether you may use different pairs on different boards is not stated. [INFERENCE: yes, as in double-board games.]

### Scoring
- Best hand on a board: 1 point.
- Tie on a board: half a point each.
- Most points takes the whole pot. Two boards beat one.
- If players tie on points (1/1/1 for example, or 1.5 each) the pot is shared among those tied players. The source gives no odd-chip rule.

### Rooms disagree
There is only one source, so no disagreement is documented. We don't merge it with any similarly-named Pineapple variant.

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Expecting a payout per board.** It is winner-take-all by points. Winning two boards beats winning one.
- **Missing that 1/1/1 is a three-way chop** of the whole pot.
- **Assuming rules the source doesn't give**, like pot-limit betting. Confirm before the deal.

## Lore and reputation

Card Player Lifestyle includes it in the flop-games list of its mixed-game rules page, which presents common variants for tournaments and low-stakes fixed-limit cash formats, mostly at its Mixed Game Festivals. We found no direct statement that it was spread at a festival event. Treat it as a rule-sheet-named, otherwise home-game or dealer's-choice variant.
