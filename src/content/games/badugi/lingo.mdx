## Four cards, three [draws](/glossary#draw), best [badugi](/glossary#badugi) wins

[Limit](/glossary#limit) 1-1-2-2 is standard; [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit) and [no-limit](/glossary#no-limit) are rarer. Button and [blinds](/glossary#blinds). Ace is low.

## Cheat sheet

- Hand = largest subset with all different ranks and all different suits. You pick which duplicates to drop. More cards beats fewer, then lowest high card wins.
- Nuts: A-2-3-4 [rainbow](/glossary#rainbow), then A-2-3-5, A-2-4-5, A-3-4-5, 2-3-4-5, A-2-3-6...
- Draw 0-4. Standing [pat](/glossary#snow) is zero. Pre-draw action starts left of the big blind; post-draw rounds start left of the button.
- Show all four cards at showdown. Unused cards don't break ties, and suits aren't ranked. Equal ranks chop.
- WSOP procedure: push-and-pull, an exposed pitched card stays with you, up to four in a row. Joining: wait for the BB or post a full BB.
- [Odd chip](/glossary#odd-chip): the Badugi section is silent, so the general WSOP button-game rule presumably applies (or the mixed-games worst-position rule). [Inference.]

## Watch out for

- Counting a paired or suited hand as four cards. K-Q-J-10 rainbow beats A-2-3 + dup.
- Overplaying high badugis on the first draw. Three-card A-2-3 drawing to the missing suit has ten outs (Wikipedia).
- Max players: WSOP and Pagat say eight; most rooms play six.
- Origin is disputed (Pagat: Winnipeg, Korea, prisons, Paul Clark around 2004; Wikipedia: Clark from Vietnam). A WSOP $1,500 Badugi event has run since 2023.
