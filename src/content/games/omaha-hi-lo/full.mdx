## It's like Omaha, except the pot gets cut in half

You know Omaha: four hole cards, five board cards, and you **must** use exactly two from your hand and exactly three from the board. O8 keeps all of that and adds a second way to win. Half the pot goes to the best high hand. The other half goes to the best *low* hand, if anyone has one.

## 30-second cheat sheet

- Everyone gets four hole cards. Blinds, flop, turn, river, just like Hold'em.
- Every hand has two jobs: make the best high, and make the best low.
- A low only counts if it's five different ranks, all **8 or lower**. Aces are low. Straights and flushes don't hurt you.
- Best possible low: A-2-3-4-5, the "wheel".
- Nobody has a qualifying low? The high hand takes the **whole** pot.
- Usually played Limit. It's a staple of every mixed rotation (HORSE, 8-Game).

## Full rules

### The deal
Dealer button, small blind, big blind. Four cards each, face down. Betting starts left of the big blind. Flop (three cards), turn, river, with a betting round after each. In limit play the first two streets use the small bet and the last two use the big bet.

### Building your hands
You build **two separate hands**, and each one uses exactly two cards from your hand and exactly three from the board. They can use the same cards or different ones.

- **High:** the usual poker ranking. Best five-card hand wins.
- **Low:** five cards of different ranks, none higher than 8. Aces count as low. A-2-3-4-5 is the best, and then you compare from the top card down, so 8-6-4-2-A beats 8-7-3-2-A. Straights and flushes don't matter for low.

### Qualifying
A low that isn't 8-or-better doesn't exist. If the board has three or more cards above 8, or it's paired up so there aren't three separate low ranks, nobody can make a low and the high winner scoops the whole pot.

### Ties and quartering
Ties split their half. If two players tie for low, they each get a quarter of the pot. That's called getting **quartered**, and it's why people say A-2 isn't always the nuts.

## Worked examples

### One player takes both halves

Alice has a pair of kings in her hand with a king on the board, so three kings for high, and she uses A-3 with 2-5-7 for the low. She wins both halves. Bob's A-4 makes a worse low than Alice's A-3, and he has no pair.

### No low on board

<Example game="omaha-hi-lo" id="no-low" />

The K-9-T-9-4 board only has one card of 8 or lower, so nobody can make a five-card low. Alice's A-2-3-5 looks great but is worthless here. High takes everything.

### Counterfeited

<Example game="omaha-hi-lo" id="counterfeit" />

Alice has A-2 and thinks she has the nut low, but the board already has a 2 and an ace. Her two low cards are duplicated by the board, so she can't make a good low. Bob's 3-4 with the board's 2-6-7 wins low instead. Alice still takes high with her pair of nines and ace on the board.

### Quartered

<Example game="omaha-hi-lo" id="quartered" />

Alice and Bob both have A-2 for the same low, and they split it. Carol made the best high with three jacks, so she gets half. Alice and Bob each get a quarter.

## How you'll embarrass yourself

- **Using three hole cards or one.** Exactly two from your hand. Always.
- **Playing a lone A-2 hand with nothing else.** It's a good start but it's the nuts only until someone else holds A-2 too, and then you're getting quartered.
- **Forgetting the qualifier.** Calling it a low when there's a nine in it. It's not a low.
- **Chasing the low when you can only half the pot** and missing that a single strong high can win the whole thing when there's no low.

## Lore and reputation

Omaha Hi-Lo, "Omaha eight-or-better" or just O8, is the great equalizer of the mixed game table. It rewards the player who can think about two hands at once. Weaker players go home with a fraction of the pot, called "scooped" by a patient regular who only plays hands that can make both halves.
