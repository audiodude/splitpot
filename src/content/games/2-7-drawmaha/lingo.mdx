## Drawmaha with a [2-7 low](/glossary#27-low) draw half

Same skeleton as [Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha): five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)), half to the best deuce-to-seven low from your own five. Limit or pot-limit; WSOP cap is seven players.

## Cheat sheet

- Draw half: five final hole cards, board ignored. Aces high, straights and flushes count against you, lowest hand wins.
- [Nut low](/glossary#nut-low): 7-5-4-3-2 not all one suit. A-2-3-4-5 is ace-high, not a [wheel](/glossary#wheel).
- Omaha half: exactly two hole cards plus three board, high.
- No qualifier. Both halves always pay out; ties split their half and a player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop).
- WSOP draw: 1 to 3 or [pat](/glossary#snow), counts locked once declared. Other rooms differ (BARGE 0 to 5, some 2-card caps).
- Procedure: the WSOP deals the turn and then the draw cards. Some rooms draw first and then turn. Ask.
- Odd chip: WSOP says first player clockwise from the button.

## Watch out for

- Pat 7-low or 8-low is usually a favorite on the draw half. Badger's rule: don't draw.
- Your best 2-7 hands (7-4-3-2-x) are terrible Omaha hands and rarely [scoop](/glossary#scoop). The [one-way](/glossary#one-way-hand) trap runs in both directions.
- 6-5-4-3-2 is a straight, so it loses to K-Q-J-9-8.
- Breaking a good Omaha holding to draw for low: discards are gone from both halves.
- Pair of aces is the worst pair, not the best.
