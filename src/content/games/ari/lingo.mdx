## Archie plus a [community card](/glossary#community-card) that plays for high only

Everything else is Archie: five cards, three [draws](/glossary#draw), [limit](/glossary#limit), half the pot to the high and half to the [A-5 low](/glossary#a5-low), each with a [qualifier](/glossary#qualifier).

## Cheat sheet

- One community card goes in the middle at the end of the initial deal (face up, [INFERENCE]: the WSOP text doesn't say).
- High: best five of your five plus the board card. WSOP qualifier is "6's or better" (read as a pair of sixes, [INFERENCE]).
- Low: your five hole cards only, A-5, [eight-or-better](/glossary#eight-or-better). The board card doesn't play.
- Low-straight draws gain value: the board card helps them make a high straight too, so scoops are likelier than in Archie (Haney).
- Qualifier fallbacks (no qualifier either way) are Archie's by "All the rules of Archie apply", an [INFERENCE] since the Ari text doesn't repeat them. See Archie for the [odd chip](/glossary#odd-chip) conflict.

## Watch out for

- WSOP says 6s for Ari's high qualifier, but the Card Player Lifestyle sheet says qualifiers are the same as its Archie (9s). Ask.
- Counting the board card in your low.
- Evaluating draws without the board card. Haney's example is a low draw like 3-4-5, which gets straight outs for the high half from the shared card.
