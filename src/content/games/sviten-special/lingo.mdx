## Drawmaha with the [one-card-open rule](/glossary#one-card-open)

Straight [Drawmaha](/games/drawmaha): five hole cards, flop, one [draw](/glossary#draw), turn, river. Half to the best five-card high from your own five cards, half to Omaha high ([2-and-3](/glossary#omaha-rule)). Usually [pot-limit](/glossary#pot-limit), sometimes [limit](/glossary#limit). Common at Nordic and Baltic festivals; not in the WSOP rules.

## Cheat sheet

- One-card draw: the replacement is dealt face up. Keep it, or reject it and take the next card face down (PokerNews: that second card is forced; PokerListings and Card Player Lifestyle don't say).
- Two or more cards: face down as normal. [Pat](/glossary#snow): nothing to show.
- Showdown is plain Drawmaha High: draw half = five-card high from your five final cards, Omaha half = exactly two hole cards plus three board. No [qualifier](/glossary#qualifier). A player can [scoop](/glossary#scoop).
- Draw cap and turn-before-draw-cards order: whatever the table uses for Drawmaha (WSOP: 1 to 3 or pat, turn first).
- Origin is a dispute, not a fact: PokerNews says 2004, Bengtsson, Sweden; SwC Poker says summer 2006, the Sviten club in Stockholm.

## Watch out for

- The face-up card is public, and so is whether you kept it. Opponents get a free read on one-card draws.
- The rule applies to exactly one card drawn, not to two-card draws.
- A rejected card is discarded and the replacement is forced (per PokerNews).
- Discards are gone from both halves, so breaking an Omaha holding to draw is still a trap.
- A strong draw hand that's a [one-way hand](/glossary#one-way-hand) still only gets half the pot.
