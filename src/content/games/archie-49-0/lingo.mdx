## Archie with a [pip-count](/glossary#pip-count) half: A=1, 2-10 face value, J/Q/K=0

Structure is straight Archie: five cards, three [draws](/glossary#draw), [limit](/glossary#limit), button and [blinds](/glossary#blinds). Only the scoring changes. Festival-only, rules unsettled.

## Cheat sheet

- Card Player Lifestyle version: pot splits between lowest total (best 0) and highest total (best 49, four tens and a nine), "typically" with qualifiers. Their example: 35 or more for high, 8 or less for low.
- Poker Chip Forum report (Bellagio Mixed Game Festival, second-hand): two games. **49** is a points hand qualifying at 35 or more, split with the best poker hand. **Zero** is a points hand qualifying at 10 or fewer, split with the best poker hand.
- [UNVERIFIED] which is standard. Ask the dealer for the qualifiers and the other half's definition.
- No-qualifier fallbacks undocumented. Regular Archie in the WSOP rules splits among all remaining players, but whether that carries over is [INFERENCE].
- Reshuffle rule is Archie's: shuffle old discards and mucks, not the current draw's discards, and the last stub card only if it completes all draws.
- Related and better documented: Drawmaha 49 and Drawmaha Zero.

## Watch out for

- Tens are 10, not zero.
- For 49 you want tens, nines and eights, not kings.
- For the points half, suits and pairs don't matter.
- Low-points qualifier is 8 (Card Player Lifestyle) or 10 (forum). High is 35 in both.
- Archie's own pair-of-nines versus pair-of-sixes qualifier split probably carries over [INFERENCE].
- Not in the WSOP rules. Two sources reported it, and one group called both versions "duds".
